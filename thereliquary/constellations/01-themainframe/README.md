## Record Role

**The Mainframe** is preserved as the first Reliquary relic in the Twelve Constellations of Digital Antiquity.

The page frames computation as the ancestral condition that precedes later digital systems.

Its sequence begins:

> Before the network.  
> Before the window.  
> Before the search.  
> Before the ledger.

The record then reduces the earliest machine purpose to one essential function:

> **To compute.**

It specifically distinguishes this from later purposes such as connecting, entertaining, or governing the world.

---

## Preservation Theme

The Mainframe preserves the memory of early computation.

The source page describes a world in which:

- entire rooms were devoted to processing instructions;
- memory existed in fragments;
- speed was measured in moments;
- machines existed primarily to compute.

The relic therefore preserves a foundational stage of digital civilization before later layers of networking, interface, search, or ledger systems emerged.

---

## Preserved Artifacts

The page identifies four preserved artifact categories:

- Punch Cards
- Terminals
- Mainframes
- Early Computing Systems

These are presented as representative artifacts within the relic chamber.

They support the record’s larger purpose without reducing the relic to one device or one specific machine.

## The Mainframe Preserves an Idea

The source is explicit:

> **The Mainframe does not preserve a device.**

> **It preserves an idea.**

That idea is expressed through three realizations:

- calculation could be delegated;
- instructions could persist;
- logic could be mechanized.

The page concludes this observation with:

> **Every digital world descends from this realization.**

This is the defining interpretive boundary of the relic.

## Inscription

The preserved inscription is:

> **Every digital civilization inherits a machine it never met.**

This sentence functions as the chamber’s memorial statement.

It frames computation as an inherited foundation of later digital civilization.
