## Record Role

This page preserves **Digital Cash** as the sixth dependency in The Luminaries.

The record establishes the transition from secure digital communication to cryptographically protected digital value.

The page states:

> **Public-key cryptography secured communication. Digital Cash applied those ideas to economic value.**

The lineage therefore advances from mathematical trust in communication toward cryptographic trust in money.

---

## The Discovery

The record preserves **David Chaum** through his work on cryptographic electronic cash during the 1980s.

It specifically identifies:

- DigiCash;
- the eCash protocol;
- privacy-preserving digital payments.

The page notes that these systems predated Bitcoin by decades.

It also preserves an important distinction:

> **Although the system relied upon a central issuer, it proved that cryptography could support digital money.**

The significance of the record is therefore proof of cryptographic digital value, not decentralization.

---

## What Became Possible

The page describes Digital Cash as transforming cryptography from:

The record identifies several practical concepts that followed:

This lineage therefore marks the point at which cryptographic systems begin to carry economic value rather than information alone.

## Why Digital Cash Follows Public-Key Cryptography

The Luminaries is organized by dependency rather than fame.

The page defines the relationship as:

```text
Public-Key Cryptography
→ secure identity and communication

Digital Cash
→ extends those principles into economic exchange
```

Digital Cash follows Public-Key Cryptography because cryptographically protected value depends upon the ability to authenticate and secure digital interaction.

The page further states:

> **Without Chaum's work, the path toward decentralized digital scarcity would have been far less clear.**
