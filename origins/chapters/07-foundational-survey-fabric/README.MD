## Chapter Role

Chapter VII explains why BitPangea requires permanent canonical spatial reference beneath visible geography, Parcels, interfaces, engines, databases, and experience.

A digital World does not inherit physical ground.

Its spatial truth must therefore be established deliberately.

The Foundational Survey Fabric exists to provide canonical spatial meaning that belongs to BitPangea itself rather than to any particular implementation.

It does not define:

- Parcels;
- ownership;
- World membership;
- geography;
- civilization;
- runtime implementation;
- application state;
- or higher-layer semantics.

It establishes the reference basis against which those higher systems can remain coherent.

---

## The Survey Problem

The chapter frames the foundational inquiry as:

> What permanent spatial truth must exist before the World can meaningfully claim that one place is here and another is there?

If BitPangea begins with:

- geography;
- Parcel graphics;
- a game engine;
- a database schema;
- or a renderer,

then one of those systems risks becoming the authority over place.

The Foundational Survey Fabric exists to prevent that inversion.

## Architecture Boundary Rule

The chapter preserves a key discipline for lower Architecture:

> Protect the boundary. Do not design the layer above from the layer below.

A lower domain may constrain what higher domains must not violate.

It should not quietly absorb the responsibilities of those higher domains.

The Survey Fabric establishes what higher spatial systems must respect.

It does not become those systems.

## The Survey Domain Is Not the World

One of the chapter’s most important distinctions is:

> **The Survey Domain is not the World.**

The Survey Domain is the finite mathematical reference domain within which canonical Survey references are valid.

That makes it reference capacity.

It does not make every valid Survey reference BitPangea territory.

The governing distinction is:

```text
Survey Domain
→ where canonical Survey reference is mathematically valid

World
→ which spatial locations belong to BitPangea
```

A valid Survey reference does not automatically establish World membership.

Every authoritative World-space location must, however, be representable within the Survey Domain.

---

## The Survey Fabric Is Not the Parcel Cadastre

The Foundational Survey Fabric and Parcel Cadastre are distinct Architecture domains.

The chapter preserves:

> **Survey units are not Parcels.**

A Survey:

- cell;
- subdivision;
- reference element;
- coordinate construct;
- mathematical unit;
- or implementation unit

does not become a Parcel merely because it describes space.

The relationship is:

```text
Foundational Survey Fabric
→ canonical spatial reference

Parcel Cadastre
→ exactly 21,000,000 canonical Parcels defined against that reference
```

This prevents Parcel geometry from becoming the deepest spatial truth merely because Parcels are visually prominent.

## Canonical Survey Reference

The Survey Fabric must make location persistent across changing representations and implementations.

A **Canonical Survey Reference** is the permanent deterministic spatial reference through which BitPangea identifies where a valid Survey location or exact spatial extent lies.

Its meaning must remain stable even if:

- interfaces change;
- encodings change;
- aliases change;
- display forms change;
- implementations change;
- compatible Specification versions evolve.

The chapter preserves the principle:

> Representation may change. Place must not.

## Requirements, Specification, Conformance, and Reference Vectors

The chapter preserves the institutional structure:

```text
Requirements
↓
Specification
↓
Conformance
↓
Reference Vectors
```

### Requirements

Answer:

> What must be true?

### Specification

Answers:

> Precisely how does the Foundational Survey Fabric work?

### Conformance

Answers:

> How does software prove it correctly implements that Specification?

### Reference Vectors

Answer:

> Given this exact input, what exact answer must every conforming implementation produce?

This separation protects BitPangea from confusing architectural requirements, formal mathematics, implementation proof, and canonical test evidence.
