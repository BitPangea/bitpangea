## Chapter Role

Chapter IX explains why BitPangea requires enduring Architecture above and around its spatial foundation.

The spatial foundation establishes:

- one finite World;
- The Extent;
- exactly 21,000,000 Parcels;
- World Form;
- Foundational Survey Fabric;
- Parcel Cadastre.

But a persistent digital World must also support:

- operational state;
- identity;
- rights;
- control;
- legitimate change;
- persistence;
- provenance;
- interoperability;
- services;
- Builders;
- and evolving experience.

The Architecture exists to preserve those enduring responsibilities without collapsing them into one undifferentiated system.

---

## The Architecture Belongs in The Atlas

The chapter preserves **The Architecture** as a branch of **The Atlas**.

It is not a constitutional Domain of The Codex.

Its components are Architecture domains.

This distinction matters because an Architecture domain may be:

- permanent;
- foundational;
- deeply important;
- institutionally established;

without thereby becoming constitutional.

---

## From the 3×3 Scaffold to The Architecture

Earlier Creator-period inquiry explored a provisional three-category model:

- Spatial Ground;
- World Runtime;
- Civilization.

That model helped separate:

- designed foundations;
- governed operation;
- emergent civilization.

It later expanded into a 3×3 scaffold that exposed responsibilities such as:

- Foundational Survey Fabric;
- General Spatial Interpretation;
- Parcel Cadastre;
- Persistent World State;
- Identity / Rights / Control;
- Protocols / Services / Execution;
- Builder / Creation;
- Social / Economic / Cultural;
- Experience.

The model did not survive unchanged.

Its value was that it exposed responsibilities requiring more precise separation.

The governing principle that survived is:

> The architecture must determine the diagram. The diagram must not determine the architecture.

---

## Nine Recognized Architecture Domains

Chapter IX preserves nine recognized Architecture domains:

1. **Foundational Survey Fabric**
2. **Spatial Ground**
3. **General Spatial Interpretation**
4. **Parcel Cadastre**
5. **World Runtime**
6. **Identity / Rights / Control**
7. **Persistence / Provenance**
8. **Interoperability**
9. **Experience Architecture**

Their presence means BitPangea has identified enduring architectural responsibilities requiring explicit treatment.

Membership does not automatically mean:

- constitutional status;
- full design maturity;
- implementation;
- equal maturity;
- or strict dependency order.

---

## Membership Is Not Dependency Order

The chapter explicitly distinguishes:

### Architecture Membership

Answers:

> Which enduring responsibilities belong to The Architecture?

### Dependency

Answers:

> Which Architecture domain must be sufficiently established before another can proceed without guessing?

These are different questions.

The strongest demonstrated dependency remains:

```text
Foundational Survey Fabric
↓
Spatial Ground
↓
General Spatial Interpretation
↓
Parcel Cadastre
```

Beyond Parcel Cadastre, the Architecture may be more networked than linear.

The chapter preserves:

> Only claim strict ordering where dependency has actually been demonstrated.

---

## Why Architecture Domains Exist

Architecture domains exist because different responsibilities require:

- different authority;
- different permanence;
- different freedoms to change;
- different forms of evidence;
- different lifecycle expectations.

Without separation:

- reference can become authority;
- implementation can become canon;
- representation can become truth;
- convenience can become permanence.

The Architecture exists to prevent those collapses.

---

## Architecture Boundary Rule

Chapter IX preserves one of the strongest general principles to emerge from the Foundational Survey Fabric work:

> **Protect the boundary. Do not design the layer above from the layer below.**

A lower domain may establish constraints higher domains must respect.

It should not define the higher domain’s:

- internal responsibilities;
- organization;
- implementation;
- lifecycle

unless such dependency is irreducibly necessary.

Examples include:

- Parcel Cadastre owns Parcel truth but should not define the full rights architecture.
- Identity / Rights / Control may constrain action but should not become World Runtime.
- World Runtime may execute legitimate change but should not become authority over every truth it references.
- Experience Architecture may expose deeper systems but should not redefine them.

---

## Foundational Survey Fabric

The Foundational Survey Fabric establishes BitPangea’s canonical spatial reference.

Its governing question is:

> Where can canonical spatial reference be established?

Its responsibility is deliberately narrow.

It does not define:

- Parcels;
- World membership;
- rights;
- experience.

That narrowness is architectural strength.

---

## Spatial Ground

Spatial Ground sits above the Foundational Survey Fabric in the demonstrated spatial dependency.

Its role is to determine how canonical Survey reference becomes usable as enduring World-space architecture without redefining the Survey Fabric beneath it.

Its precise responsibilities remain subject to formal Architecture inquiry.

---

## General Spatial Interpretation

General Spatial Interpretation sits between Spatial Ground and Parcel Cadastre.

Its role is to address higher spatial meaning and interpretation above canonical Survey truth and below authoritative Parcel truth.

Its key boundaries are:

- it may interpret spatial truth;
- it must not redefine the Foundational Survey Fabric;
- it must not replace the Parcel Cadastre.

---

## Parcel Cadastre

The Parcel Cadastre establishes the permanent identities, fixed positions, and authoritative territorial definitions of exactly:

**21,000,000 Parcels**

Its governing question is:

> Which Parcel is this, and where does its canonical territory exist?

The Cadastre owns Parcel truth.

It may depend on deeper spatial Architecture without owning that deeper Architecture.

---

## World Runtime

World Runtime concerns BitPangea’s operational existence.

It must support capabilities such as:

- maintaining operational state;
- evaluating permissible change;
- executing valid actions;
- coordinating World services;
- preserving continuity across sessions and clients;
- recovering from failure;
- continuing coherently through time.

Its governing question is:

> How does the World continue to exist and change without surrendering coherence?

World Runtime coordinates legitimate change.

It does not become all truth.

---

## Identity / Rights / Control

The chapter preserves Identity / Rights / Control as one recognized Architecture domain containing related but distinct responsibilities.

### Identity

Asks:

> Who or what is this?

### Rights

Asks:

> What governed relationship exists?

### Control

Asks:

> What authority may actually be exercised?

The chapter also preserves the distinctions:

- Identity is not ownership.
- Ownership is not control.
- Control is not permission.
- Permission is not authority.

The domain may later require further internal decomposition.

---

## Persistence / Provenance

Persistence / Provenance addresses continuity through time.

It must help BitPangea know:

- what changed;
- where it came from;
- what authority produced it;
- what evidence supports it;
- what preceded it;
- whether later changes altered the record.

Persistence concerns endurance.

Provenance concerns origin and traceable lineage.

Together they allow BitPangea to preserve more than current state.

---

## Interoperability

Interoperability exists so BitPangea does not become permanently dependent on one implementation.

Systems may differ.

Canonical meaning must not.

The governing principle is:

> Implementation diversity must not become semantic diversity.

Interoperability supports reliable exchange among distinct systems without granting those systems authority to redefine BitPangea truth.

---

## Experience Architecture

Experience Architecture concerns how visitors, Builders, inhabitants, and future participants encounter BitPangea.

It may include:

- globe experience;
- flat lived World;
- navigation;
- Parcel interaction;
- Builder interfaces;
- discovery;
- visualization;
- immersion;
- future interaction modes.

The governing principle is:

> Experience is where the World becomes perceptible—not where the World becomes authoritative.

Experience may evolve more freely than deeper World architecture.

---

## Civilization Is Not an Architecture Domain

Chapter IX preserves a critical maturation of the earlier 3×3 model:

**Civilization is not itself a formal Architecture domain.**

BitPangea should establish the conditions under which civilization can emerge.

It should not architect:

- culture;
- economy;
- community;
- collective meaning;
- social organization;
- history

as though they were software layers to install.

The chapter preserves:

> Architecture enables. Civilization emerges.

---

## Builders Belong Above the Architecture

Builders use Architecture.

They do not become foundational authority merely because creation is essential.

Builders may depend upon:

- Parcel Cadastre;
- Identity / Rights / Control;
- World Runtime;
- Persistence / Provenance;
- Interoperability;
- Experience Architecture.

The Architecture makes legitimate creation possible.

Builders create within the World.

---

## Authority Does Not Flow Through Page Order

Presentation order does not imply authority.

A lower domain does not own everything above it.

A higher domain does not acquire authority over everything it references below.

Responsibilities remain bounded.

The chapter preserves:

> **Reference does not transfer authority.**

This principle applies across the Architecture.

---

## Permanence Gradient

Not every Architecture domain should change at the same rate.

The chapter preserves the principle:

> The deeper the layer, the more permanent it should be. The closer the layer is to the user, the more freedom it should have to evolve.

Conceptually:

- Foundational Survey Fabric → extreme persistence
- Spatial Ground → very high persistence
- General Spatial Interpretation → high architectural stability
- Parcel Cadastre → permanent Parcel identity and territorial stability
- World Runtime / Identity / Persistence / Interoperability → governed evolution
- Experience Architecture → comparatively greater freedom to evolve

This is a design principle rather than a complete formal change-control hierarchy.

---

## Replacement Principle

The Architecture should allow implementations to become obsolete without requiring the World to become obsolete with them.

The chapter preserves:

> Implementations should be allowed to become obsolete without requiring the World to become obsolete with them.

And:

> The Parcel should outlive the technology used to experience the Parcel.

And:

> The World should outlive the technology used to experience the World.

---

## Cross-Domain Relationships

Many real operations will involve multiple Architecture domains.

A future construction event may conceptually involve:

```text
actor identity
↓
rights / control
↓
Parcel Cadastre reference
↓
World Runtime validation and execution
↓
persistent state
↓
provenance
↓
interoperable exposure
↓
experience representation
```

That interaction does not mean all domains should collapse into one system.

Each Architecture domain should eventually be able to answer:

- What truth do I own?
- What truth do I reference?
- What may I change?
- Whose authority do I rely upon?
- What evidence must I preserve?

---

## Architecture Membership, Maturity, and Status

The chapter recognizes that Architecture domains may differ in maturity.

A domain may be:

- recognized;
- provisional;
- reserved;
- under active inquiry;
- institutionally established.

Those states should not be interpreted as dependency order.

Likewise, presentation order should not masquerade as dependency order.

The chapter preserves the need to distinguish:

- membership;
- presentation;
- maturity;
- dependency.

## What Is Established

Chapter IX preserves the following as established strongly enough for Version 0.1:

- BitPangea requires enduring Architecture above its spatial foundation.
- The Architecture belongs within The Atlas, not The Foundation.
- The Architecture recognizes nine domains.
- Architecture-domain membership does not establish constitutional status.
- Architecture-domain membership does not establish strict dependency order.
- The demonstrated spatial dependency is:
  **Foundational Survey Fabric → Spatial Ground → General Spatial Interpretation → Parcel Cadastre**.
- Foundational Survey Fabric and Parcel Cadastre remain distinct authorities.
- Parcel Cadastre remains authoritative for Parcel truth.
- World Runtime is required for governed operational continuity.
- Identity / Rights / Control requires explicit Architecture treatment.
- Persistence / Provenance requires explicit Architecture treatment.
- Interoperability is necessary for technological durability.
- Experience Architecture must remain distinct from deeper World authority.
- Civilization is not itself a formal Architecture domain.
- Builders operate through and above the Architecture.
- Reference does not transfer authority.
- The permanence gradient governs expected change.

The chapter preserves unresolved architectural questions including:

- final distinction between Architecture membership and presentation order;
- dependencies beyond the first four domains;
- cross-domain or bidirectional dependencies;
- World Runtime / Identity / Rights / Control relationship;
- internal decomposition of Identity / Rights / Control;

- World Runtime / Persistence / Provenance boundary;
- Persistence / Provenance relationship to historical institutions;

- domain maturity and status model;
- final cross-domain authority model;
- technical implementation of all Architecture domains.

These questions should remain open until the Architecture itself justifies their answers.

The chapter preserves an earlier 3×3 formulation:

> Design the ground. Govern the runtime. Enable the civilization.

Its mature form is:

> **Establish what must endure. Govern what must change. Leave open what should emerge.**

And once the Architecture has done enough:

> **allow the World to happen.**
