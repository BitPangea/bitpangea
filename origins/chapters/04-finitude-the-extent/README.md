## Chapter Role

Chapter IV examines one of BitPangea’s deepest structural conditions:

**the World is finite.**

Digital space can ordinarily be expanded by instruction.

BitPangea deliberately rejects that convenience at the level of the World.

The chapter preserves the constitutional conclusion that Finitude is not a redundant standalone World Property.

Instead:

> Finitude is preserved as a condition of **WP-S-I — The Extent**.

The Extent carries the complete spatial scope of BitPangea.

Finitude describes an essential condition of that scope.

---

## Why Finitude Matters

Physical worlds inherit limitation.

Digital systems do not.

A digital environment can often create:

- more coordinates;
- more territory;
- more records;
- more regions;
- more instances.

BitPangea takes a stronger position.

Its spatial limitation should belong to the World itself rather than be imposed later as an economic rule upon otherwise unlimited digital space.

Conceptually:

```text
not:

unlimited digital space
↓
policy declares some portion scarce

but:

finite World
↓
all later spatial systems operate within that condition
```

This places Finitude beneath:

- ownership;
- allocation;
- markets;
- economics;
- and later civilizational systems.

## Finitude Is Not Scarcity Alone

Chapter IV distinguishes Finitude from scarcity.

**Scarcity** describes limited availability.

**Finitude** describes the condition that the World itself possesses a complete spatial scope.

A system can be scarce without being finite.

BitPangea’s proposition is stronger:

**exactly 21,000,000 Parcels exist within one finite World.**

The Parcel count therefore exists within a deeper spatial condition.

The World is finite first.

The Parcel Cadastre must be reconciled to that World.

The chapter identifies **WP-S-I — The Extent** as the constitutional property concerning the complete spatial scope of BitPangea.

The relationship is:

```text
WP-S-I — The Extent

Finitude

```

This structure reflects constitutional economy.

BitPangea should not multiply permanent constitutional concepts merely because several words can describe aspects of the same underlying truth.

The chapter therefore preserves:

> Discovery does not require multiplication of canon.

## The Extent Is Not the Survey Domain

A major architectural distinction preserved in Chapter IV is:

**The Extent ≠ Survey Domain**

The Extent concerns:

**the complete spatial scope of the World**

The Survey Domain concerns:

**the finite mathematical reference capacity of the Foundational Survey Fabric**

Every authoritative World-space location must be representable within the Survey Domain.

But:

**valid Survey reference ≠ World membership**

The governing boundary is:

> Reference capacity is not territory.

The Survey Fabric establishes where canonical reference can exist.

Higher spatial architecture determines what belongs to the World.

This inquiry should not automatically reopen previously examined concepts such as:

- Edge;

**do not multiply canon when one established concept already carries the enduring truth.**

## Finitude Without a Wall

Chapter IV distinguishes constitutional truth from visual or experiential design.

The constitutional condition is:

**BitPangea possesses a complete spatial Extent.**

The design question is:

> How should that terminal condition become perceptible?

Possible expressions may exist.

But Finitude itself does not require:

- a wall;
- an ocean;
- darkness;
- a void;
- a collision barrier;
- a terrestrial coastline;
- or another familiar game or map convention.

The constitutional layer constrains design without prescribing it.

## The Frontier Is Different

Chapter IV preserves an important terminology correction.

**The Verge**

is a spatial / World-form design concept.

**The Frontier**

is an epistemic concept concerning the relationship among:

- what is known;
- what is established;
- what has been explored;
- and what remains unknown.

The two terms should not be used interchangeably.

This correction is part of the historical reasoning preserved by *Origins*.

## Finite Does Not Mean Fully Parcelized

Chapter IV preserves the constitutional possibility that:

> The Parcel fabric may not exhaust the World.

BitPangea contains exactly **21,000,000 Parcels**.

But that does not establish that every meaningful spatial condition within The Extent must be an ordinary Parcel.

Possible non-Parcel spatial conditions may include:

- World-scale infrastructure;
- Verge conditions;
- Outliers;
- other constitutionally permissible spatial phenomena.

The correct relationship is not:

```text
World = 21,000,000 Parcels
```

Instead:

```text
World
↓
contains one coherent spatial architecture
↓
within which exactly
21,000,000 Parcels
must exist
```

The World remains the greater whole.
