# Origins — Chapter XVI

## What Remains Open

**BitPangea: Origins · Version 0.1 · September 2026**

This directory contains the web presentation of **Chapter XVI — What Remains Open** from **BitPangea: Origins — Version 0.1**, *The Foundational Manuscript of a Finite Digital World*.

The chapter maps BitPangea’s unresolved architectural landscape without treating every unanswered question as the same kind of work.

Its governing discipline is:

> **Resolve what coherence requires. Defer what dependency requires. Preserve as open what civilization requires.**

And its governing principle is:

> **An open question should remain open until either the architecture requires an answer, the evidence justifies one, or the future earns the right to provide it.**

## Chapter Role

Chapter XVI is not a backlog.

It is a map of architectural uncertainty.

The chapter distinguishes unresolved questions that:

- must eventually be resolved for canonical operation;
- should wait for prerequisite Architecture;
- are parked pending stronger evidence;
- should remain intentionally open for future Builders, institutions, or civilization.

The value of the chapter is therefore not in maximizing closure.

It is in preserving the correct status of each open problem.

---

## Four Kinds of Openness

Chapter XVI distinguishes at least four categories.

### Required Resolution

Questions the World must eventually answer in order to become canonically operational.

Examples include:

- Survey mathematics;
- Parcel establishment;
- identity;
- authorization;
- Runtime state;
- integrity;
- other indispensable systems.

Their unresolved status is temporary.

### Dependent Resolution

Questions that matter but should not yet be answered because prerequisite Architecture has not matured.

The correct status is:

> **wait for the dependency**

A dependent question is being protected from premature resolution.

### Parked Inquiry

Questions that may matter later but presently lack enough evidence for active resolution.

Parked does not mean rejected.

A parked inquiry may return if later Architecture gives it independent work to do.

### Intentional Openness

Questions that may properly belong to future civilization rather than the Creator.

Examples include:

- culture;
- social convention;
- future institutions;
- historical interpretation;
- many economic outcomes;
- unexpected uses of the World.

The chapter preserves:

> **Some questions await answers. Others preserve possibility.**

## Open Does Not Mean Unconstrained

An unresolved problem may still be tightly bounded.

For example, final Parcel geometry remains open, but any acceptable solution must still respect:

- one finite World;
- exactly **21,000,000 Parcels**;
- persistent Parcel identity;
- fixed position;
- World Form;
- canonical Survey reference;
- equal-area preference;
- local coherence;
- Parcel Adjacency;
- World-scale Parcel Contiguity;
- The Verge;
- Outliers where adopted;
- representation independence.

The solution is open.

The problem is not undefined.

## Preserve the Question

The chapter treats precise unanswered questions as architectural assets.

A strong inquiry protects BitPangea from shallow answers.

The chapter preserves:

> **Good unanswered questions are part of the architecture of discovery.**

Examples include carefully framed Verge and Parcel-geometry inquiries that preserve all relevant constraints rather than simply asking to “solve” the topic.

---

## Dependency Before Schedule

BitPangea should not confuse architectural sequence with calendar pressure.

An inquiry should proceed because its dependencies are sufficiently mature.

Not because:

- a date arrived;
- an earlier list placed it next;
- a Creator-period Day implies completion.

The chapter preserves:

> **The Days record progress. They do not command progress.**

Architectural dependency has priority over scheduling neatness.

---

## Governing Principle

Chapter XVI closes with:

> **Resolve what coherence requires. Defer what dependency requires. Preserve as open what civilization requires.**

And:

> **An open question should remain open until either the architecture requires an answer, the evidence justifies one, or the future earns the right to provide it.**

Its final discipline is:

> **BitPangea would be unfinished if it could no longer distinguish the questions that require answers from the possibilities that require freedom.**
