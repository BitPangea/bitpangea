# Origins — Chapter XI

## World Runtime

**BitPangea: Origins · Version 0.1 · September 2026**

This directory contains the web presentation of **Chapter XI — World Runtime** from **BitPangea: Origins — Version 0.1**, *The Foundational Manuscript of a Finite Digital World*.

The chapter preserves **World Runtime** as the governed operational Architecture domain through which BitPangea evaluates legitimate change, executes permitted operations, preserves current World condition, coordinates with other authoritative domains, and continues coherently through time.

Its governing principle is:

> **World Runtime exists to permit change without surrendering continuity.**

## Chapter Role

Chapter XI explains how BitPangea moves from persistent spatial truth into persistent operational truth.

A World may already possess:

- The Extent;
- exactly **21,000,000 Parcels**;
- Foundational Survey Fabric;
- Parcel Cadastre;
- actors;
- rights;
- control relationships.

But without a governed mechanism for preserving legitimate change from one moment to the next, the World remains static.

World Runtime exists to make BitPangea operational without allowing mutable state to overwrite deeper canonical truth.

---

## Runtime Responsibility

The chapter preserves the core responsibility:

> **Allow the World to change without surrendering coherence.**

World Runtime does not replace:

- Foundational Survey Fabric;
- Parcel Cadastre;
- Identity / Rights / Control;
- Persistence / Provenance;
- Interoperability;
- Experience Architecture.

Its responsibility is narrower:

**govern legitimate operational change and preserve coherent World State.**

---

## From Spatial Truth to Operational Truth

The deeper spatial Architecture answers questions such as:

- Where is this place?
- Which Parcel is this?
- What territory defines it?
- What canonical spatial relationships exist?

World Runtime asks:

- What exists here now?
- What operation is being requested?
- Is the operation permitted?
- What state should change?
- What state should remain unchanged?
- What must persist afterward?

The chapter preserves the distinction:

```text
Spatial Architecture
→ persistent place

World Runtime
→ persistent operational condition
```

A Parcel may remain the same Parcel while nearly everything above it changes.

That is why Runtime must remain a distinct Architecture domain.

---

## Persistent World State

BitPangea requires persistent operational state.

If something legitimately becomes true of the World, that condition must be capable of surviving:

- the session that created it;
- the client that displayed it;
- the interface that initiated it.

Possible future World State may include:

- constructed objects;
- infrastructure;
- World features;
- configuration;
- service condition;
- authorized relationships;
- mutable environmental conditions;
- other legitimate operational state.

The exact categories remain open.

The requirement does not:

> What legitimately becomes true of the World must be capable of remaining true after the interaction that caused it has ended.

---

## State Is Not Spatial Truth

The chapter strictly separates mutable World State from canonical spatial truth.

Conceptually:

```text
Foundational Survey Fabric
→ canonical spatial reference

Parcel Cadastre
→ canonical Parcel identity and territory

World Runtime
→ current mutable operational condition
```

A structure may appear on a Parcel and later disappear.

The Parcel remains.

World-state mutation must not become permission to alter canonical Survey or cadastral truth.

---

## Legitimate State Transition

Persistence alone is not enough.

A database can remember data.

World Runtime must distinguish legitimate change from arbitrary mutation.

Future Runtime must be able to reason about:

- valid change;
- invalid change;
- authorized change;
- unauthorized change;
- temporary change;
- persistent change;
- failed change;
- superseded change.

Conceptually:

```text
Actor
↓
Identity resolved
↓
rights / control evaluated
↓
requested operation validated
↓
World Runtime executes
↓
World State changes
```

The World should not change merely because a client requests it.

It should change because a valid operation has passed through the appropriate authorities and Runtime rules.

---

## Request, Authorization, Execution, Result

A major distinction preserved in Chapter XI is:

```text
request
≠
authorization
≠
execution
≠
result
```

### Request

Someone wants something to happen.

### Authorization

The action is presently permitted under the relevant authority and conditions.

### Execution

The legitimate operation is actually carried out.

### Result

The World now occupies a new operational condition.

Even if an implementation performs these rapidly, the concepts should remain separate.

---

## Execution

Execution belongs to World Runtime.

Conceptually:

```text
State A
↓
authorized valid operation
↓
execution
↓
State B
```

The chapter preserves the distinction:

> **Authority permits. Runtime changes. Provenance remembers.**

Identity / Rights / Control may establish standing and authority.

Runtime performs the legitimate mutation.

Persistence / Provenance may preserve how that mutation occurred.

---

## Runtime as Orchestration

World Runtime is partly an orchestration domain.

A consequential operation may involve several Architecture domains.

A future construction action may conceptually include:

1. actor requests construction;
2. identity resolves;
3. target Parcel resolves through the Parcel Cadastre;
4. rights and control are evaluated;
5. Runtime validates the operation;
6. relevant protocols govern interaction;
7. Runtime executes legitimate change;
8. persistent World State updates;
9. Persistence / Provenance receives evidence;
10. Interoperability exposes the canonical result;
11. Experience Architecture presents it.

This interaction does not make Runtime the authority over every participating domain.

---

## Runtime Does Not Own Everything

Because Runtime participates in many operational workflows, it must be prevented from becoming universal authority.

World Runtime should not become authoritative for:

- The Extent;
- canonical Survey reference;
- Parcel identity;
- Parcel territorial definition;
- actor identity;
- rights provenance;
- historical evidence;
- interoperability semantics;
- experience representation.

The governing principle remains:

> **Reference does not transfer authority.**

Runtime coordinates legitimate operation.

It does not become all World authority.

## Protocols

Protocols define coherent operational interaction.

They may eventually govern:

- message structure;
- operation semantics;
- validation requirements;
- reference forms;
- state-transition rules;
- error behavior;
- expected responses;
- authority dependencies.

The chapter preserves:

> Protocols define coherent participation in World operation.

And the boundary:

> Protocol is not authority.

A protocol may define how a Parcel is referenced.

The Parcel Cadastre still owns Parcel truth.

## Services

Services provide capability.

Possible future services may include:

- Parcel lookup;
- identity resolution;
- authorization;
- construction;
- navigation;
- World State;
- discovery;
- history;
- infrastructure.

The chapter preserves the distinction:

> **A protocol defines coherent interaction. A service provides capability.**

A service implementation may be replaced without redefining the World if canonical meaning, protocols, and authority boundaries remain intact.

---

## Services Are Not Architecture Authorities by Default

A service may expose information from an authoritative domain without becoming authoritative itself.

Examples:

```text
Parcel service
→ exposes cadastral information

Parcel Cadastre
→ owns Parcel truth
```

Likewise:

```text
identity service
→ exposes identity capability

Identity / Rights / Control
→ owns the governed identity relationship
```

The implementation provides capability.

The Architecture determines authority.

## Deterministic Canonical Change

The chapter distinguishes intentional uncertainty from accidental disagreement.

BitPangea may support:

- randomness;
- simulation;
- games;
- emergent behavior;
- creative variation.

But equivalent valid canonical operations against equivalent relevant state should not produce contradictory canonical outcomes merely because different conforming software executed them.

The technical mechanism remains open.

The architectural requirement does not.

---

## Validation Before Mutation

The chapter preserves:

> **Validate before mutating canonical World state whenever possible.**

Conceptually:

```text
request
↓
resolve canonical references
↓
evaluate authority
↓
validate operation
↓
execute
↓
persist resulting state
```

The purpose is to prevent invalid requests from partially altering the canonical World.

---

## Atomicity and Coherent Change

Some operations may affect multiple objects or domains.

A future operation could:

- create a structure;
- modify associated state;
- connect infrastructure;
- change permissions;
- produce provenance evidence.

If one part succeeds and another fails, Runtime must avoid leaving contradictory canonical state.

The chapter preserves:

> Consequential World change should preserve consistency across every canonical state it affects.

No specific database transaction model is mandated.

---

## Runtime and Persistence / Provenance

Chapter XI distinguishes:

### World Runtime

Answers:

> What is true now?

### Persistence / Provenance

Helps answer:

> How did this become true?

World Runtime may produce evidence such as:

- operation identity;
- actor reference;
- authority reference;
- prior relevant state;
- resulting state;
- time ordering;
- execution outcome.

Persistence / Provenance may preserve that lineage.

The governing boundary is:

> **Runtime changes the present. Provenance preserves the path to the present.**

---

## State Is More Than a Snapshot

A World that preserves only current state may persist but still be unable to explain itself.

A mature BitPangea may eventually need to distinguish:

- current state;
- prior state;
- transition;
- actor;
- authority;
- execution;
- time;
- result;
- evidence.

The exact retention architecture remains unresolved.

## Time and Ordering

Persistent operation requires meaningful ordering.

This does not require:

- Earth time;
- a specific calendar;
- a simulated day/night cycle.

It requires the ability to distinguish relevant ordering such as:

- before;
- after;
- current;
- previous;
- future-effective;
- expired;
- superseded.

The final temporal architecture remains open.

The requirement for meaningful ordering does not.

---

## Concurrency

A multi-actor World may receive simultaneous or competing operations.

Runtime must eventually answer questions such as:

- Which operation becomes canonical first?
- Can both changes coexist?
- Does one invalidate another?
- Must one be retried?
- What state becomes authoritative afterward?

The technical mechanism remains open.

The architectural requirement is that BitPangea remain one coherent operational World under concurrent activity.

---

## Failure Is Part of the Architecture

A durable World cannot assume perfect execution.

Possible failures include:

- network failure;
- service unavailability;
- client disconnects;
- malformed messages;
- process crashes;
- implementation defects;
- timeouts;
- infrastructure outages.

Runtime should eventually support principles such as:

- failure containment;
- safe retry;
- state verification;
- graceful degradation;
- recovery;
- auditability;
- consistency checking.

Failure is not outside the architecture.

It is part of the environment in which the World must endure.

---

## Recovery

Persistence without recoverability is incomplete.

The chapter asks:

> How can correct operational state be restored after failure without arbitrarily rewriting the World or its history?

Possible future mechanisms may include:

- validated checkpoints;
- replayable operations;
- redundant state;
- integrity verification;
- authoritative recovery procedures.

These are possibilities, not adopted designs.

The principle is:

> **Persistence must include the ability to recover coherent state.**

---

## Integrity

Canonical World State must be trustworthy.

BitPangea must eventually be able to distinguish state that is:

- valid;
- internally consistent;
- produced through legitimate transition;
- not silently altered outside authorized pathways.

Possible technical mechanisms may someday include:

- cryptography;
- signatures;
- verification;
- replication;
- consensus;
- audit structures.

No particular mechanism is mandated.

The requirement is:

> Participants and systems must be able to distinguish legitimate World change from unexplained mutation.

Some Runtime requirements may benefit from:

```text
World redesigned to justify it
```

That inquiry remains open.

They may:

- submit operations;

- present experiences.

Interoperability must preserve:

- reference meaning;
- protocol semantics;

- interpretable valid results;

The chapter preserves:

> **One operational World may support many conforming implementations.**

Builders should not write directly into canonical World State however they choose.

The safer architecture is:

```text
Builder
↓
requests legitimate change
↓
Identity / Rights / Control evaluates authority
↓
World Runtime validates and executes
↓

↓
Persistence / Provenance preserves lineage
```

The Builder can be powerful without becoming sovereign.

Runtime protects the boundary between creative freedom and World integrity.

Experience Architecture may allow a participant to:

- walk;
- inspect;
- navigate;
- construct;
- communicate;
- interact.

But something appearing in an interface does not automatically become canonical World State.

Conceptually:

```text
Experience Architecture

World Runtime

World State

```

This allows many future experiences to coexist above one operational World.

## One World, Many Clients

A mature BitPangea may be encountered through many applications.

Those applications should not create incompatible Worlds merely because their interfaces differ.

The chapter preserves the symmetry:

```text
one canonical spatial truth
→ many representations

one canonical operational World
→ many experiences
```

This separation supports technological durability.

## Runtime Boundaries

Not everything that happens around BitPangea belongs to canonical World State.

Examples that may remain outside canonical World State include:

- local user-interface preferences;
- private drafts;
- local camera positions;
- unsaved experiments;
- transient communications;
- client-only visualizations.

The chapter preserves:

```text
client state ≠ World state
temporary state ≠ persistent state
private representation ≠ canonical World condition
displayed possibility ≠ executed World change
```

Runtime should preserve what belongs to the World.

It should not own everything surrounding it.

## World State as Shared Responsibility

Changing canonical World State has architectural weight.

A legitimate persistent change means:

> From this point forward, this is part of the operational World unless later legitimately changed.

That is why identity, rights, authorization, validation, execution, persistence, provenance, and integrity converge around Runtime activity.

The World must permit enough change to support life while preserving enough discipline to remain coherent.

## Change Without Loss of Identity

Chapter XI extends the spatial continuity principle.

Earlier spatial Architecture preserves:

> The World should be capable of changing without losing where it is.

World Runtime adds:

> **The World should be capable of changing without losing what it is.**

Buildings may appear.

Infrastructure may evolve.

Rights may change.

Services may improve.

Technology may be replaced.

Civilization may become more complex.

BitPangea should remain one continuous World through those changes.

## What Is Established

Chapter XI preserves the following as established strongly enough for Version 0.1:

- World Runtime is a recognized Architecture domain.
- BitPangea requires persistent operational World State.
- World State is distinct from foundational spatial truth.
- Legitimate change must be evaluated before canonical state mutation.
- Identity / Rights / Control informs authorization.
- World Runtime executes legitimate operational change.
- Request, authorization, execution, and result are distinct concepts.
- Protocols define coherent operational interaction.
- Services provide capabilities.
- Protocols and services do not automatically gain authority over the domains they reference.
- Reference does not transfer authority.
- Runtime coordinates specialized authorities rather than absorbing them.
- Consequential state transitions should preserve consistency.
- Persistent state must survive sessions, clients, and interface changes.
- World Runtime must support many clients encountering one canonical operational World.
- Persistence includes recoverability and integrity.
- Runtime should produce sufficient evidence for Persistence / Provenance.
- Interoperability must support multiple conforming implementations without contradictory World meaning.
