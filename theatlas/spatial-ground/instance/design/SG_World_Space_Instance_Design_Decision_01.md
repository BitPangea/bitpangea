# Spatial Ground — World-Space Instance Design Decision 01

**BitPangea · The Atlas · Spatial Ground**  
**Record Type:** Creator Design Decision — Canonical World-Space Instance  
**Creator Period:** Day #122 · September 26, 2026  
**Status:** COMPLETE — CREATOR DESIGN DECISION  
**Subject:** Topological form, expression simplicity, and relation between Ground limit and recognizable World silhouette  
**Canonical Status:** Candidate design decision only. The World-space instance is not yet adopted.

---

## 1. Decision

The canonical BitPangea World-space instance SHALL be designed as:

> **one connected, closed, hole-free canonical World-space region, expressed by one primary FSF spatial-set definition and carried into Spatial Ground through one SG-CJSON `fsf` root leaf.**

The intended canonical instance SHALL therefore avoid Ground-level constructive inclusion/exclusion unless later shown to be strictly necessary.

The selected instance form is:

```text
D_WORLD
=
fsf(WORLD_REGION)
```

and not:

```text
difference(...)
union(...)
intersection(...)
```

for the final canonical World-space definition unless the single-region expression proves impossible under the adopted FSF mathematics.

This is an **instance design choice**, not a reduction of the general SG-CJSON language.

---

## 2. Topological Choice

The actual BitPangea World-space instance SHALL be:

- singular;
- connected;
- closed;
- hole-free;
- finite;
- compositionally asymmetric;
- recognizable as one supercontinental whole.

For the canonical instance:

```text
W ≠ ∅
```

```text
W is connected
```

```text
∂W ⊆ W
```

and the Creator design intention is:

```text
W has no internal non-World holes
```

This last property is a Creator-period design choice for the instance.

It is not implied merely by the constitutional connectedness requirement.

---

## 3. Why Hole-Free Was Chosen

The Spatial Ground architecture permits holes in principle.

That permission does not require the Creator to use them.

A hole-free canonical instance is selected because it gives BitPangea the strongest combination of:

- one-World legibility;
- permanence;
- mathematical simplicity;
- independent reconstruction;
- lower conformance burden;
- simpler limit semantics;
- cleaner handoff to higher layers;
- freedom for higher layers to create lakes, void-like experiences, terrain depressions, exclusions, or other interpreted interior phenomena without turning them into foundational non-World-space.

The Ground should be simpler than the geography built upon it.

---

## 4. SG-RV-024 Consequence

This design choice has an important architectural benefit.

The currently reserved adversarial case `SG-RV-024` concerns the interaction between:

- Ground-level set subtraction;
- a potentially non-closed final set;
- the Closed World-Space rule.

The actual canonical World-space instance SHALL avoid that production dependency by using a single closed FSF region rather than a Ground-level `difference` construction.

Therefore:

> **SG-RV-024 remains a valid language-level / FSF interoperability issue, but it is not required to resolve the canonical BitPangea World-space instance if the final instance remains a single closed FSF region.**

This does not delete or conceal the blocker.

It removes it from the critical path of the actual instance.

---

## 5. Ground Limit and Recognizable Silhouette

The exact Spatial Ground limit SHALL provide the canonical **macro-silhouette** of BitPangea.

This means the recognizable one-supercontinent identity should not be based on an unrelated visual outline floating above a materially different Ground boundary.

The selected relation is:

> **At macro scale, the iconic BitPangea silhouette derives from the actual canonical Ground limit.**

However, coincidence of identity does not collapse Ground into rendering.

Higher layers MAY:

- soften;
- texture;
- illuminate;
- stylize;
- symbolically simplify;
- locally exaggerate;
- visually interpret

the edge.

Those visual treatments SHALL remain subordinate to the canonical Ground limit.

---

## 6. Exact Limit vs. Visual Coastline

The following distinction is adopted:

### Canonical Ground Limit

- exact;
- mathematical;
- membership-governing;
- two-dimensional;
- permanent after adoption.

### Visual / Geographic Edge

- higher-layer;
- rendered;
- experiential;
- potentially textured or stylized;
- may contain local coastline-like detail not semantically significant to Ground.

The two should be recognizably aligned at World scale.

They need not be pixel-identical at every rendered scale.

---

## 7. Macroform

The canonical instance SHALL preserve the established World-form direction:

> **a singular, compositionally balanced, asymmetric digital supercontinent.**

The World SHALL not be:

- a circle;
- a square;
- a symmetric emblem;
- a literal Bitcoin symbol;
- a literal “21”;
- an infinity symbol;
- a direct tracing of historical Pangea;
- a collection of disconnected islands pretending to be one continent.

The silhouette itself should become recognizable through use.

---

## 8. Articulation

The canonical macroform SHOULD be deeply articulated.

The exact limit MAY contain:

- major lobes;
- broad bays;
- deep inlets;
- peninsular extensions;
- channels that approach separation without creating disconnection;
- asymmetric protrusions and recesses.

The governing test is:

> **Push articulation until just before unity becomes doubtful.**

The World must still read unmistakably as one supercontinent.

---

## 9. No Ground-Level Islands

The canonical instance SHALL NOT include disconnected World-space islands.

Any visually island-like or near-separated forms must remain connected to the canonical World-space whole at Ground level.

Higher layers may later create:

- island-like terrain;
- platforms;
- floating experiences;
- interpreted water divisions;
- visual separations.

Those do not alter Ground membership.

---

## 10. No Ground-Level Inland Holes

The canonical instance SHALL NOT create inland non-World enclaves solely to represent:

- lakes;
- seas;
- craters;
- voids;
- protected areas;
- inaccessible areas;
- visual negative space.

Those are higher-layer concepts unless independently required by constitutional architecture.

Ground answers only whether space participates in The World.

---

## 11. One Primary FSF Region

The preferred production representation is:

```json
{
  "op": "fsf",
  "value": "<canonical-fsf-world-region>"
}
```

The exact value SHALL be a canonical FSF spatial-set expression established under final production FSF mathematics.

Spatial Ground SHALL not encode the actual macroform by a long procedural sequence of additions and removals if one exact canonical region can express it.

The preservation principle is:

> **One World. One primary region. One membership predicate.**

---

## 12. Why a Single FSF Region Is Better

A single-region canonical instance:

- minimizes permanent primitives;
- reduces semantic-equivalence burden;
- avoids operator-order dependence;
- avoids hidden constructive history;
- makes reconstruction easier;
- keeps the final artifact human-auditable;
- leaves geometry ownership entirely in FSF;
- makes the instance independent of the history of how the shape was designed.

The design process may use many intermediate constructions.

The adopted artifact should not need to remember them.

---

## 13. Design History Is Not Canon

The Creator may use:

- sketches;
- procedural generators;
- mathematical exploration;
- hand-edited curves;
- parametric tools;
- AI-assisted visual studies;
- candidate geometries;
- iterative deformation

to arrive at the exact World region.

None of those design methods becomes canonical merely because it was used.

Upon adoption, the canonical region itself governs.

---

## 14. Orientation

No Ground orientation is ontologically privileged.

The canonical region may later have a preferred **portrait orientation** for recognition, presentation, and Atlas use.

That portrait orientation SHALL NOT change:

- membership;
- canonical identity;
- FSF position;
- equivalence.

Orientation is a presentation/design record above membership truth.

---

## 15. Globe Relationship

The established globe-first experience does not make Ground spherical.

Spatial Ground remains the canonical two-dimensional planar membership layer inherited from FSF.

The globe is a higher presentation of The World.

The planar canonical Ground region SHALL therefore be capable of supporting a globe-first experience without treating the globe as Ground mathematics.

---

## 16. Parcel Independence

This World-space instance decision does not determine:

- Parcel count;
- Parcel geometry;
- rhombille orientation;
- Frontier morphology;
- cadastral subdivision;
- Parcel ownership.

The Parcel Cadastre remains above Spatial Ground.

The exact Ground region is established before final Parcel fabric is imposed.

---

## 17. Candidate Instance Constraints

Any actual geometry proposed as the canonical BitPangea World-space candidate SHALL pass all of the following Creator design tests:

1. **One-World test** — unmistakably one connected whole.
2. **Silhouette test** — recognizable in solid black or monochrome.
3. **Asymmetry test** — avoids emblematic symmetry.
4. **Balance test** — asymmetry remains compositionally deliberate.
5. **Articulation test** — contains memorable major forms.
6. **No-fragmentation test** — no disconnected World-space islands.
7. **No-hole test** — no Ground-level internal non-World enclaves.
8. **Closed-limit test** — every canonical limit point is World-space.
9. **FSF-expression test** — expressible as one canonical FSF region.
10. **Reconstruction test** — independently reproducible from the adopted artifact.
11. **Ground-purity test** — contains no terrain, Parcel, ownership, or experience semantics.
12. **Higher-layer freedom test** — does not overdetermine future geography.

A candidate failing any mandatory test SHALL NOT proceed to instance adoption.
