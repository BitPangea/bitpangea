# Spatial Ground — World-Space Instance Design Decision 02

**BitPangea · The Atlas · Spatial Ground**  
**Record Type:** Creator Design Decision — Canonical World-Space Instance  
**Creator Period:** Day #122 · September 26, 2026  
**Status:** COMPLETE — CREATOR DESIGN DECISION  
**Subject:** Canonical macro-composition, mass distribution, articulation, and portrait orientation  
**Canonical Status:** Candidate design decision only. The World-space instance is not yet adopted.

---

## 1. Decision

The canonical BitPangea World-space macroform SHALL be designed as:

> **a horizontally dominant, compositionally balanced, strongly asymmetric supercontinent built from one central body and four major attached lobes, with two principal deep bays and one narrow connective waist that approaches—but never reaches—fragmentation.**

The overall form SHALL feel:

- singular;
- organic;
- digital rather than terrestrial-copying;
- calm at distance;
- memorable in silhouette;
- increasingly articulated under closer inspection.

The macroform SHALL not be based on literal symbolism.

---

## 2. Overall Aspect

The canonical macroform SHALL use a preferred overall bounding aspect of approximately:

```text
1.30 : 1
```

width to height.

This ratio is a Creator design target rather than a Ground invariant.

The final exact FSF region MAY vary modestly from this ratio if necessary to improve:

- compositional balance;
- silhouette recognition;
- articulation;
- globe presentation.

The form SHOULD remain clearly broader than tall without becoming ribbon-like.

---

## 3. Dominant Mass Structure

The supercontinent SHALL have:

- one dominant central body;
- four major attached lobes;
- no detached islands;
- no internal Ground holes.

The lobes are geometric components of one continuous World-space region.

They do not represent:

- nations;
- Regions;
- cultures;
- ownership zones;
- economic divisions;
- political divisions;
- Parcel classes.

They are simply macroform.

---

## 4. The Four-Lobe Composition

For design work, the four primary attached lobes SHALL be referred to neutrally as:

```text
Lobe A
Lobe B
Lobe C
Lobe D
```

These are design labels only and are not canonical place names.

### Lobe A — Northwestern Reach

- broad;
- substantial;
- slightly blunt at the outer extreme;
- attached through a wide shoulder;
- visually stabilizes the upper-left portion of the silhouette.

### Lobe B — Northeastern Reach

- longer and more articulated than Lobe A;
- narrower at its attachment;
- terminates in a distinctive outward sweep;
- contributes the strongest high-level directional gesture.

### Lobe C — Southeastern Reach

- medium-to-large;
- extends downward and outward;
- includes one major concavity on its inner edge;
- provides visual counterweight to the northeastern reach.

### Lobe D — Southwestern Reach

- shorter but heavier;
- broad near the core;
- less elongated than B or C;
- acts as a visual anchor and prevents the form from appearing top-heavy.

The four lobes SHALL differ clearly enough that the silhouette remains recognizable even without internal detail.

---

## 5. Dominant Diagonal

The preferred composition SHALL have a subtle dominant diagonal running:

```text
northwest → southeast
```

This diagonal SHALL organize the visual energy of the form without becoming a straight geometric axis.

The World should feel naturally weighted along that diagonal while remaining balanced overall.

The diagonal is compositional.

It is not an FSF axis or a spatial privilege.

---

## 6. Central Body

The central body SHALL be:

- the largest contiguous mass;
- visibly capable of holding the composition together;
- broad enough that the World never reads as four lobes joined by a small hub;
- irregular rather than circular.

The central body SHOULD contain at least one broad convex shoulder and one deep concavity so that it does not read as a simple blob.

---

## 7. Principal Bay 01

The first principal bay SHALL be positioned between:

```text
Lobe B and Lobe C
```

on the eastern side of the supercontinent.

It SHALL be:

- deep;
- memorable;
- broad at its mouth;
- irregular internally;
- clearly subordinate to World unity.

This bay should become one of the strongest recognition features of the silhouette.

It SHALL NOT cut so deeply that B and C appear to be separate islands.

---

## 8. Principal Bay 02

The second principal bay SHALL sit between:

```text
Lobe C and Lobe D
```

on the southern-to-southwestern side.

It SHALL be:

- smaller than Principal Bay 01;
- deeper relative to its width;
- more enclosed in appearance;
- compositionally useful in breaking the lower silhouette.

This second bay creates asymmetrical repetition without mirroring the first.

---

## 9. Secondary Inlets

The World MAY contain several smaller inlets and recesses.

These SHOULD be:

- irregularly distributed;
- non-periodic;
- clearly subordinate to the two principal bays;
- large enough to matter at regional scale;
- too restrained to make the silhouette noisy at planetary scale.

The macroform should remain legible when reduced to a small icon.

---

## 10. Narrow Connective Waist

The canonical macroform SHALL include one deliberately narrow connective zone between the central body and one major reach.

The preferred location is between:

```text
the central body and Lobe B
```

This connective waist SHALL:

- create near-separation;
- remain visibly and mathematically connected;
- heighten the sense of distinct places within one World;
- provide one of the silhouette's most memorable tensions.

The design rule is:

> **Approach fragmentation. Never cross it.**

---

## 11. No Bilateral Symmetry

The macroform SHALL reject:

- left-right symmetry;
- top-bottom symmetry;
- radial symmetry;
- four-way symmetry;
- emblematic balance.

Balance SHALL come from mass, counter-mass, reach, void, and articulation rather than mirrored geometry.

---

## 12. Compositional Balance

Although strongly asymmetric, the World SHALL appear compositionally balanced.

The intended balance is:

- Lobe A balances part of C;
- Lobe D anchors the lower-left;
- Lobe B provides reach and directional energy;
- the central body remains the visual stabilizer.

No single lobe should feel like an accidental appendage.

---

## 13. Silhouette Hierarchy

The silhouette SHALL read in three nested levels.

### Planetary Scale

The viewer perceives:

- one supercontinent;
- broad asymmetry;
- the four-lobe structure;
- the two major bays;
- the dominant diagonal.

### Regional Scale

The viewer begins to perceive:

- secondary inlets;
- local peninsular forms;
- subtle recesses;
- more complex connective geometry.

### Local Scale

Higher layers may reveal:

- terrain;
- coastline-like treatment;
- Parcel fabric;
- infrastructure;
- visual connectivity.

Ground SHALL not encode those higher-layer meanings.

---

## 14. Preferred Portrait Orientation

The canonical **portrait orientation** for public identity and Atlas presentation SHALL orient the supercontinent so that:

- the dominant NW→SE diagonal descends gently from left to right;
- Lobe B occupies the upper-right;
- Lobe C occupies the lower-right;
- Lobe D anchors the lower-left;
- Lobe A occupies the upper-left.

The intended presentation tilt is approximately:

```text
15°–20° clockwise from a purely horizontal long-axis presentation
```

The exact final portrait rotation SHALL be chosen after the exact FSF geometry exists.

This orientation is presentational only.

It SHALL NOT be part of Ground membership.

---

## 15. Globe Presentation

In the preferred globe-first experience, the canonical portrait SHOULD place the supercontinent near the visual center of the globe-facing hemisphere.

The World should not appear:

- cramped against a pole;
- split across the primary viewing seam;
- visually compressed against the limb.

The globe orientation may later be tuned independently of Ground geometry.

---

## 16. Flat Atlas Presentation

The secondary flat Atlas presentation SHOULD use the same preferred portrait orientation unless a cartographic requirement demands otherwise.

The flat form SHOULD preserve:

- the same recognizable silhouette;
- the same dominant diagonal;
- the same relative lobe relationships.

Projection choice SHALL NOT alter Ground membership.

---

## 17. Macroform and Pangea

The canonical macroform SHALL remain a homage to the idea of Pangea:

> **one great continuous land-like whole**

but SHALL NOT:

- trace historical Pangea;
- imitate a real continental reconstruction;
- borrow recognizable modern continental coastlines;
- rely on Earth geography for identity.

The homage is conceptual, not cartographic.

---

## 18. Digital-Age Identity

The silhouette should feel as though it belongs to a digital world rather than a fantasy Earth.

That identity SHOULD come from:

- unusual but coherent macro-articulation;
- controlled near-separations;
- deliberate asymmetry;
- compositional precision;
- later intrinsic digital connectivity revealed at closer scales.

It SHOULD NOT depend on literal circuit-board, pixel, Bitcoin, or technological iconography at Ground scale.

---

## 19. Geometry Construction Guidance

When the actual FSF region is created, the initial geometry SHOULD begin from:

1. one broad central mass;
2. four major attached lobes;
3. two principal bays;
4. one narrow connective waist;
5. a small number of secondary inlets;
6. smoothing and refinement while preserving recognition.

The exact geometry SHOULD be iterated by silhouette testing rather than by adding arbitrary detail.

---

## 20. Silhouette Test

Every candidate geometry SHALL be tested in at least these representations:

```text
solid black on white
solid white on black
small-icon scale
globe-distance scale
flat Atlas scale
```

A candidate passes only if the same World remains immediately recognizable.

---

## 21. Complexity Limit

The exact limit SHALL not be made artificially fractal or infinitely detailed.

The canonical geometry SHOULD contain:

- a finite number of major articulation features;
- a finite exact representation under FSF;
- enough detail for identity;
- no unnecessary micro-irregularity.

Higher layers may create richer coastline-like visual detail without changing Ground.

---

## 22. Mass Distribution Target

As a design target, the visible mass SHOULD feel approximately distributed as:

```text
Central body        ~45%
Lobe A              ~15%
Lobe B              ~15%
Lobe C              ~15%
Lobe D              ~10%
```

These percentages are compositional guidance only.

They SHALL NOT become canonical measurements unless later explicitly adopted.

The purpose is to prevent any lobe from dominating the World.

---

## 23. Negative-Space Design

The principal bays and inlets SHALL be treated as intentional negative-space components of the silhouette.

The negative space should contribute to recognition as strongly as the outward lobes.

This supports:

> **order without sameness**

through a balance of mass and absence rather than repetition.

---

## 24. What Is Now Fixed

The following Creator design choices are now fixed for the candidate instance:

- overall horizontal dominance;
- approximate 1.30:1 bounding aspect target;
- one central body;
- four major attached lobes;
- one dominant NW→SE compositional diagonal;
- two principal bays;
- one narrow connective waist;
- no bilateral or radial symmetry;
- no detached Ground islands;
- no Ground holes;
- preferred portrait arrangement of the four lobes;
- exact Ground limit as the source of macro-silhouette identity.
