## 1. Purpose

This baseline establishes the minimum governance architecture required before adoption of the Spatial Ground Requirements.

It operationalizes decisions already resolved in Architectural Resolution Pass #3 and carried through Pass #4.

It does not reopen those decisions.

It establishes:

1. the adoption authority;
2. the institutional effect of an Adoption Act;
3. the three record states;
4. the separation of evidence from authority;
5. the relationship between adoption, correction, supersession, model replacement, scope change, stewardship, and implementation;
6. the rule that governance events establish status but never become inputs to World membership.

---

## 2. Adoption Authority

During the Creator Period, the authority to adopt Spatial Ground records belongs to:

> **The Creator, acting as the Atlas adoption authority, by a recorded Adoption Act in The Atlas.**

This is an institutional office exercised by a person.

It is not:

- an unrecorded personal preference of the Creator;
- The Atlas acting without an authorized adopter;
- The Codex;
- The Foundation;
- a steward;
- Conformance;
- an implementer;
- publication;
- widespread use;
- technical control.

The person decides.

The recorded institutional act makes the decision canonical.

After adoption, the record governs rather than the adopter's later recollection.

## 3. Distinct Roles

| Role | Function | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Creator / designer | Designs candidates within constitutional constraints | Design authority before adoption |
| Review / Conformance / independent implementation | Produces evidence | Evidence only |
| Atlas adoption authority | Adopts by recorded Adoption Act | Canonical status authority |
| The Atlas | Home of the act and adopted record | Institutional record home |
| Steward | Custody and preservation | No semantic authority |
| Implementer | Realizes adopted architecture | No canonical authority |
| Constitutional sources | Constrain permissible architecture | Upstream constitutional authority |

Evidence never adopts.

Implementation never adopts.

Publication never adopts.

Use never adopts.

## 4. Three Record States

Record states belong to records, never to World-space.

### 4.1 Candidate

A **Candidate** is a proposed record or definition.

A Candidate is explicitly noncanonical.

It may be:

- revised;
- tested;
- compared;
- rejected;
- withdrawn;
- archived.

A rejected or withdrawn Candidate remains historically a Candidate.

It does not become a new authority state merely because it is archived.

---

### 4.2 Adopted

An **Adopted** record is canonical because it is the subject of a valid recorded Adoption Act.

“Established” is a synonym for Adopted.

It is not a fourth state.

For Requirements and Specification, adoption makes the record governing and later revision may occur through governed supersession.

For the canonical World-space instance, adoption fixes the membership truth permanently within Spatial Ground and The Atlas.

---

### 4.3 Superseded

A **Superseded** record is a formerly primary artifact that has been replaced by a designated successor through a valid recorded act.

A superseded record:

- loses current authority;
- remains historically preserved;
- must remain resolvable from historical references;
- does not imply that World-space moved or changed.

For the canonical World-space instance, supersession may preserve only the same membership meaning.

---

## 5. What Is Not a Record State

The following are not canonical record states:

- Proposed — same as Candidate;
- Established — same as Adopted;
- Mature — evidence condition;
- Readiness certified — evidence condition;
- Operational — implementation condition;
- Under review — annotation;
- Disputed — annotation;
- Correction proposed — annotation;
- Archived — preservation condition;
- Historical — archival label.

No annotation, maturity designation, review state, or operational condition changes canonical World membership.

---

## 6. Adoption Act

An Adoption Act is the sole institutional act that changes a record from Candidate to Adopted.

The Adoption Act must record:

1. exactly what is adopted;
2. the primary artifact and sufficient identity/integrity evidence;
3. the governing Requirements and, where applicable, Specification versions;
4. the Survey specification or compatible lineage relied upon, where applicable;
5. the declared primary form for each semantic element;
6. constitutional conformity, with source traceability;
7. evidence relied upon, identified as evidence and not as authority;
8. the adopter;
9. the capacity in which the adopter acts;
10. the authority exercised;
11. explanatory design intent, where appropriate;
12. the effective adoption moment;
13. the prior record state;
14. the resulting record state.

An Adoption Act must be explicit and institutionally traceable.

Canonical status must never be inferred from:

- correctness;
- publication;
- implementation;
- certification;
- popularity;
- custody;
- technical control;
- filename;
- convention.

---

## 7. Three Separate Adoptions

Three distinct adoption events must not be conflated:

1. **Requirements adoption**
2. **Specification adoption**
3. **Canonical World-space instance adoption**

Each requires its own Adoption Act.

Adoption of the Requirements does not adopt the Specification.

Adoption of the Specification does not adopt the canonical World-space instance.

Only adoption of the canonical World-space instance exhausts Creator design authority over that instance.

---

## 8. Requirements Adoption

For the Spatial Ground Requirements:

- Candidate status applies before Gate A;
- Gate A consists of the recorded Adoption Act;
- adoption makes the Requirements governing;
- later amendment or replacement must occur through controlled supersession;
- Requirements adoption does not establish any particular World-space membership set.

The Requirements may be revised after adoption through proper governance because their adoption is not the irreversible World-space instance adoption.

---

## 9. Specification Adoption

For the Spatial Ground Specification:

- it remains Candidate until Gate B;
- adoption makes the formal model and governing rules canonical;
- it remains distinct from the canonical World-space instance;
- later supersession requires governed proof of continuity where existing canonical meaning must remain unchanged.

---

## 10. Canonical World-Space Instance Adoption

The canonical World-space instance is adopted only after its readiness evidence is complete.

At that moment:

- one definition becomes canonical;
- Creator design authority over that instance is exhausted;
- established World-space becomes permanent within Spatial Ground and The Atlas;
- no architectural authority may redesign, relocate, enlarge, reduce, or replace the membership set.

This act is distinct from Requirements adoption and Specification adoption.

---

## 11. Evidence and Authority

Evidence may include:

- Conformance results;
- validation results;
- independent implementation results;
- constitutional conformity review;
- reconstruction evidence;
- preservation evidence;
- lineage verification;
- adversarial review;
- formal equivalence evidence.

Evidence informs the adoption authority.

Evidence does not make a record canonical.

No validator, auditor, implementation, or test framework acquires adoption authority by producing strong evidence.

---

## 12. Correction

The governing rule is:

> **What was adopted is what the Adoption Act records.**

A correction restores agreement with that record.

It does not appeal to unrecorded intent.

### Tier 1 — Restoration

Restoration brings copies, derived artifacts, or implementations back into agreement with the canonical record.

Stewards and implementers may perform restoration.

No Adoption Act is required if canonical status does not change.

### Tier 2 — Meaning-Preserving Supersession

A new primary artifact may replace the old primary artifact if semantic identity is preserved.

The adoption authority must designate the successor by recorded act.

### Tier 3 — Correction of a Defective Normative Record

If the normative record is defective, ambiguous, contradictory, or the wrong artifact was recorded, the adoption authority may adopt a corrected record on evidence.

A valid, unambiguous adopted definition cannot be “corrected” merely because someone later prefers a different design.

---

## 13. One Canon During Review or Dispute

During dispute, review, or proposed correction:

- the existing Adopted record remains canonical;
- candidate corrections remain noncanonical;
- dispute annotations do not create membership states;
- only a valid adoption-authority act may change which artifact is primary.

There is never a period in which two competing records are both canonical.

---

## 14. Scope Change

A scope change alters the responsibilities of Spatial Ground.

It does not alter established World-space membership.

A scope change requires:

- explicit proposal;
- proof of architectural necessity;
- shared-architecture review;
- constitutional return if the proposal depends on foundational truth;
- adoption by the Atlas adoption authority where a new governing record is required.

Implementation practice cannot silently enlarge Spatial Ground scope.

---

## 15. Model Replacement

A model replacement substitutes a different formal model while preserving canonical meaning.

It requires proof of semantic continuity.

It may not be used to disguise a change in established World-space membership.

Where continuity cannot be established, the replacement cannot be treated as meaning-preserving.

---

## 16. Stewardship

A steward may:

- preserve;
- replicate;
- publish;
- verify;
- restore copies;
- migrate derived forms;
- renew integrity evidence;
- report defects.

A steward may not:

- adopt;
- redesign;
- alter semantic meaning;
- settle ambiguity by personal interpretation;
- designate a new primary artifact without adoption authority;
- claim canonical authority from custody.

Stewardship is custody, not sovereignty.

---

## 17. Implementation

Implementation realizes adopted architecture.

Operational status does not:

- create adoption;
- create membership;
- create authority;
- change record state;
- supersede the canonical record.

An implementation that disagrees with canonical truth is wrong or nonconforming; it does not create a competing truth.

---

## 18. Constitutional Return

If a matter depends on constitutional identity, permanence, an adopted Codex, a World Property, or another foundational truth, the matter returns upward.

The Atlas may route the question.

The Atlas does not thereby gain authority to revise constitutional truth.

A formal constitutional revision, if ever validly made by whatever upstream authority and procedure govern it, is outside this governance baseline.

---

## 19. No Architectural World-Space Change Authority

No act within:

- Spatial Ground;
- The Atlas;
- stewardship;
- implementation;
- ordinary governance;
- post-Creator architectural succession

may alter established World-space.

There is no dormant architectural World-space change power.

This baseline does not define any upstream constitutional revision authority or procedure.
