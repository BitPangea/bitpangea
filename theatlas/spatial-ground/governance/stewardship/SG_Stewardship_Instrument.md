# Spatial Ground — Stewardship Instrument

**BitPangea · The Atlas · Spatial Ground**  
**Instrument Type:** Stewardship Governance Instrument  
**Creator Period:** Day #122 · September 26, 2026  
**Status:** COMPLETE — READY FOR RECORDING  
**Institutional Home:** The Atlas  
**Scope:** Spatial Ground canonical records and preservation packages  
**Semantic Authority Conferred:** None  
**Canonical World-Space Instance Effect:** None

---

## 1. Purpose

This Instrument establishes the standing stewardship regime for Spatial Ground.

Its purpose is to ensure that canonical Spatial Ground records remain:

- available;
- verifiable;
- reproducible;
- preservable;
- recoverable across institutions, hosts, formats, implementations, and time.

The governing rule is:

> **Stewardship is custody and preservation without semantic authority.**

No stewardship act creates, changes, interprets, enlarges, contracts, or reassigns World-space.

---

## 2. Institutional Principle

Spatial Ground canon SHALL NOT depend upon:

- one person;
- one company;
- one repository host;
- one custodian;
- one private key;
- one software implementation;
- one operating institution.

The identity of World-space resides in its adopted records and formal rules.

Possession of those records does not confer authority over them.

---

## 3. Steward

A **Spatial Ground Steward** is a person, office, or institution recognized by the Atlas adoption authority to undertake preservation duties.

Recognition confers:

- responsibility;
- standing to report preservation status;
- standing to attest to custody and fidelity.

Recognition does **not** confer:

- adoption authority;
- semantic authority;
- design authority;
- interpretive authority;
- ownership of World-space;
- jurisdiction over World-space.

---

## 4. Custodian in Fact

Any person or institution holding a faithful and independently verifiable copy of canonical Spatial Ground records is a:

> **Custodian in Fact**

A Custodian in Fact may preserve, replicate, verify, and provide evidence.

A Custodian in Fact does not become a recognized Steward merely by:

- possession;
- hosting;
- repository control;
- infrastructure control;
- private-key possession;
- organizational continuity.

A faithful copy held by a Custodian in Fact retains its evidentiary value.

---

## 5. Steward Permissions

A recognized Steward MAY:

1. hold canonical records;
2. replicate canonical records;
3. publish faithful copies;
4. cross-verify records against other preserved copies;
5. verify artifact identity and integrity;
6. renew integrity evidence;
7. migrate copies to new storage formats where semantic equivalence is demonstrable;
8. regenerate preservation packages;
9. restore damaged or divergent derived copies to agreement with canon;
10. report discrepancies;
11. preserve evidence relating to conflicts or defects;
12. maintain redundant custody arrangements.

---

## 6. Steward Prohibitions

A Steward SHALL NOT:

1. alter normative meaning;
2. correct a primary normative artifact;
3. designate which artifact is primary;
4. adopt any Spatial Ground artifact;
5. reinterpret ambiguous canon with authority;
6. resolve a conflict between canonical records by preference;
7. create a new World-space instance;
8. alter membership;
9. alter the canonical limit;
10. claim ownership of World-space by reason of custody;
11. claim jurisdiction over World-space by reason of custody;
12. make canonical intelligibility dependent upon private institutional knowledge;
13. use possession of signing keys as a claim of authority.

---

## 7. Canonical Restoration

A Steward may perform **Tier 1 restoration**.

Tier 1 restoration means:

> restoring a copy, mirror, package, index, derived representation, or implementation state to agreement with already-established canon.

Tier 1 restoration does not include:

- revising Requirements;
- revising the Specification;
- superseding a primary artifact;
- correcting adopted normative meaning.

Where restoration would require such an act, the matter SHALL be referred to the Atlas adoption authority or its lawful successor.

---

## 8. Format Migration

A Steward MAY migrate preserved records to new formats only if:

1. the original primary artifact remains preserved where possible;
2. the migrated artifact is explicitly identified as derived or successor representation;
3. equivalence is demonstrated under the governing Specification;
4. no semantic element changes;
5. provenance identifies the source artifact.

Format migration never changes place.

---

## 9. Integrity Evidence

Stewards MAY create and renew:

- cryptographic hashes;
- signatures;
- manifests;
- timestamp evidence;
- replicated archive records;
- cross-steward verification records.

Integrity evidence proves facts about artifact identity and preservation.

It does not itself define canonical meaning.

---

## 10. Private Keys

Private keys may authenticate acts of preservation.

Private keys SHALL NOT constitute institutional authority.

Therefore:

- possession of a key grants no semantic power;
- loss of a key destroys no canonical meaning;
- replacement of a key changes no canonical meaning;
- a signature cannot convert a non-canonical artifact into canon.

---

## 11. Multiple Stewards

Multiple independent Stewards are permitted and preferred.

The preservation model SHOULD avoid single-custodian dependence.

Stewards SHOULD cross-verify canonical records periodically.

Disagreement between preserved copies SHALL be surfaced as evidence.

No Steward may resolve the disagreement by unilateral declaration.

---

## 12. Steward Recognition

A Steward is formally recognized by a recorded act of:

- the Creator acting as Atlas adoption authority during the Creator Period; or
- the lawful successor Atlas adoption authority afterward.

A recognition record SHALL identify:

- the Steward;
- the preservation scope;
- the records or domains covered;
- the date of recognition;
- the recognizing authority;
- the acceptance of this Instrument.

Recognition may be withdrawn without altering canonical Ground.

---

## 13. Steward Acceptance

A recognized Steward SHALL acknowledge:

> I accept custody and preservation responsibility without authority to alter, reinterpret, adopt, own, or redesign Spatial Ground canon.

This acknowledgment is institutional evidence.

It is not an input to membership.

---

## 14. Loss of All Recognized Stewards

If all recognized Stewards cease to exist:

1. canonical Ground remains unchanged;
2. faithful preserved copies remain valid evidence;
3. Custodians in Fact may continue preservation;
4. no custodian gains adoption authority by being the last surviving holder;
5. a future lawful Atlas authority may recognize new Stewards.

Canon does not enter an ownerless or undefined semantic state.

---

## 15. Conflict With Canon

If a Steward-maintained copy conflicts with the established primary artifact:

- the primary artifact governs;
- the Steward copy is defective;
- the Steward SHALL preserve evidence of the discrepancy;
- the Steward SHOULD restore its copy to agreement.

If uncertainty exists about which artifact was adopted, the matter is a fact-finding question for the Atlas adoption authority or lawful successor.

The Steward supplies evidence but does not decide.

---

## 16. Editorial Maintenance Distinction

Stewardship is not Atlas editorial maintenance.

Atlas maintainers may prepare governed revisions to revisable normative text.

Only the authorized adoption process can designate such a revision as governing.

A Steward may preserve both predecessor and successor records after that determination.

---

## 17. First Stewardship Standing

Until one or more separate Steward Recognition Records are issued:

- The Atlas remains the institutional home of the canonical Spatial Ground record;
- any faithful repository holder acts only as Custodian in Fact;
- no hosting arrangement is presumed to carry stewardship authority.

This avoids inventing an office-holder from repository possession.

---

## 18. Required Preservation Package

The Spatial Ground preservation package SHOULD preserve at minimum:

1. adopted Requirements;
2. adopted Specification;
3. canonical Ground Definition;
4. Adoption Act;
5. FSF dependency declaration and identified lineage;
6. Conformance records;
7. canonical Reference Vector corpus;
8. independent implementation evidence;
9. integrity manifest;
10. succession and stewardship instruments;
11. preserved superseded records where governance requires them.

---

## 19. Standing

**STEWARDSHIP REGIME — ESTABLISHED**

**SEMANTIC AUTHORITY OF STEWARDS — NONE**

**CUSTODIAN-IN-FACT STATUS — ESTABLISHED**

**MULTIPLE STEWARDS — PERMITTED AND PREFERRED**

**KEY POSSESSION AS AUTHORITY — PROHIBITED**

**LOSS OF STEWARDS — NO EFFECT ON CANON**

---

## 20. Governing Closing Rule

> **Preserve the canon. Verify the canon. Restore copies to the canon. Never become the canon.**
