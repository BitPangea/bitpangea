## Architectural Role

Spatial Ground is the Atlas domain that adds canonical World membership to canonical Survey space.

Its responsibility is narrow:

> **Does this canonical Survey space participate in The World?**

For every valid Survey location within its operative domain, Spatial Ground must ultimately provide exactly one canonical membership answer:

- World-space; or
- non-World-space.

Membership is total, two-valued, deterministic, singular, and permanent once the canonical World-space instance is adopted.

---

## Relationship to the Foundational Survey Fabric

The Foundational Survey Fabric owns canonical spatial reference and mathematics.

Spatial Ground depends on that lower-layer truth and does not duplicate it.

Spatial Ground may consume, as defined by the Survey Fabric:

- Survey Domain;
- valid Survey references;
- reference identity and equality;
- coordinates;
- geometry;
- topology;
- spatial operations;
- measurement and scale;
- precision;
- refinement;
- adjacency;
- compatible translation across Survey versions.

Spatial Ground must not introduce a parallel coordinate system, geometry, topology, measurement system, precision system, or spatial reference regime.

A useful architectural formulation is:

> **The Foundational Survey Fabric supplies canonical spatial reference and mathematics. Spatial Ground adds canonical participation in The World.**

---

## Relationship to World-Space

World-space is canonical Survey space that participates in The World.

Spatial Ground owns **World-membership meaning**.

It does not originate the constitutional existence of The World, and it does not assign higher meaning to World-space after membership has been determined.

World-space:

- lies within the finite Survey Domain;
- lies strictly inside the governed FSF production placement envelope;
- does not coincide with the entire Survey Domain;
- is non-empty;
- is singular;
- is connected;
- has exact, deterministic membership;
- remains permanent once adopted.

Non-World-space is the relational complement of World-space within the operative Survey Domain. Spatial Ground assigns it no independent intrinsic meaning.

---

## Constitutional Subordination

Spatial Ground remains subordinate to the constitutional sources of BitPangea.

The current architecture expresses, without originating:

- **Singularity** — one World, therefore one canonical World-space;
- **Existence** — canonical World-space is non-empty;
- **Continuity** — canonical World-space is connected;
- **Extent conformity** — the canonical definition must conform to the constitutional Extent of The World.

Constitutional Extent establishes finite, permanent spatial scope but does not independently determine the exact geometry, representation, membership limit, measurement, implementation, interpretation, or exact membership set.

Where Spatial Ground and an applicable constitutional source conflict, the constitutional source prevails.

## Creator-Period Design and Adoption

The governing institutional sequence is:

```text
Constitutional truth constrains
→ the Creator designs the exact permissible spatial expression in The Atlas
→ a recorded Adoption Act adopts one definition
→ Spatial Ground canonically expresses and preserves it
→ higher layers interpret it
→ implementation realizes it
```

An exact World-space design may be a Creator-period choice where the Constitution leaves room.

Once the canonical World-space instance is adopted, Creator design authority over that instance is exhausted.

No act available within Spatial Ground, The Atlas, stewardship, implementation, ordinary governance, or post-Creator architectural succession may alter established World-space.

The Declaration contemplates formal constitutional revision. Any such revision remains upstream of Spatial Ground and The Atlas and is not a Spatial Ground change mechanism.

## Canonical Definition

Exactly one canonical determination of World-space may govern at a time.

The architecture requires the definition to be:

- finitely expressible;
- decidable for membership;
- validatable by deterministic rules;
- semantically comparable where multiple representations are permitted;
- closed against hidden state and undocumented rules;
- refinement-closed;
- representation-independent.

The canonical definition, its governing rules, and the inherited Survey specification must together be sufficient to reconstruct the same membership meaning without private explanation or original runtime state.

---

## Canonical Limit

The canonical limit is a consequence of the World-membership definition.

Spatial Ground does not need to treat the limit as an independent entity.

Membership at and around the limit must remain determinate.

The canonical limit may coincide with a recognizable geographic, cartographic, or experiential edge, but:

> **Coincidence is not identity.**

Visual World Form and experiential geography remain higher-layer concerns.
