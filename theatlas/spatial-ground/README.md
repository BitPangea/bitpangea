## Relationship to Foundational Survey Fabric

Spatial Ground depends upon the Foundational Survey Fabric.

The Foundational Survey Fabric establishes canonical reference.

Spatial Ground does not replace or redefine that reference system.

Its role is to relate canonical reference space to enduring BitPangea ground and World-space.

---

## Relationship to General Spatial Interpretation

Within the demonstrated spatial dependency, Spatial Ground provides:

> **the enduring World-space basis required by General Spatial Interpretation**

General Spatial Interpretation sits above Spatial Ground and may describe higher spatial entities against that established World-space.

Spatial Ground does not absorb the interpretive responsibilities of the domain above it.

---

## Relationship to World-Space

The source identifies World-space as part of Spatial Ground’s responsibility but leaves the exact relationship open.

Spatial Ground is the domain in which canonical Survey reference becomes enduring BitPangea World-space.

The exact:

- World-space semantics;
- membership rules;
- non-Parcel-space treatment;
- Extent expression

remain unresolved.

---

## Relationship to World Form and Geography

The source explicitly prevents Spatial Ground from becoming visible geography or World Form.

Spatial Ground may support later systems that define World Form, Outliers, The Verge, terrain, infrastructure, and other spatial structures.

Those structures remain higher-order architecture and must not be collapsed into the ground itself.
