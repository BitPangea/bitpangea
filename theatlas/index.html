The Canonical Map of the World

One unified supercontinent. One finite world. 21 million parcels.

The Atlas is the canonical cartographic design home of ₿itPangea: the place where the constitutional constraints of the World are translated into intentional geography, parcel design, naming, visual form, and cartographic memory.

BitPangea is constitutionally constrained, but intentionally designed. The Foundation and The Codex establish what must remain true. The Atlas is where the Creator determines how those truths are expressed as an actual World.

The Founding Constraint

BitPangea consists of one finite, complete, continuous digital World containing exactly 21,000,000 Parcels. Its Parcel population is fixed. The World does not expand or duplicate, and no central authority may alter that constitutional fact.

Current Status

Through Day #106, The Atlas now distinguishes more clearly between visible cartographic design and the deeper spatial architecture that may permanently support The World. The Creator Period continues to preserve adopted design directions while allowing foundational spatial architecture to remain exploratory until it survives further review.

For World form, BitPangea continues with the adopted Creator direction of a singular, compositionally balanced, asymmetric digital supercontinent, inspired by Pangea in ancestry rather than reconstructed from it. The experience is now clarified as globe-first from space, followed by an atmospheric descent and cloud transition into BitPangea as a flat World. Progressive Revelation continues to govern how geography, connectivity, and Parcel structure become visible with proximity.

For Parcel morphology, the rhombic form produced by the three-part hexagonal construction remains a useful local Parcel representation concept. Day #105 clarified, however, that Parcel morphology and foundational spatial order are separate Creator design problems. The rhombic grammar is therefore no longer presumed to define BitPangea’s deepest permanent spatial architecture.

Days #105–106 introduced a strong but unadopted spatial architecture contender: Foundational Survey Fabric → General Spatial Interpretation → Parcel Cadastre. The Survey Fabric would establish permanent spatial reference; General Spatial Interpretation would define non-Parcel spatial entities against that reference; and the Parcel Cadastre would be dedicated specifically to the exactly 21,000,000 Parcels.

Day #103’s constitutional finding remains unchanged: the exactly 21,000,000 Parcels do not constitutionally exhaust every cartographically valid portion of The World. The Outliers and The Verge therefore remain constitutionally permissible Creator design space, with exact design still open.

Constitution, Design, and Implementation

BitPangea now distinguishes four complementary functions across constitutional definition, intentional design, verification, and technical realization.

The Foundation & The Codex

Establish what must be true: permanent constraints, constitutional identity, intrinsic structure, and governing invariants.

The Atlas

Establishes what is intentionally designed within those truths: geography, Parcel arrangement, non-Parcel structure, naming, visual form, and the concrete cartographic World.

Creator Constraint Review

Tests significant design choices before adoption and returns only genuine dependencies to narrow constitutional inquiry.

Implementation

Determines how Atlas design is technically rendered, accessed, and expressed without turning technical choices into constitutional law.

A design decision becomes constitutionally significant only when changing it would alter Parcel identity, Boundary, Coordinate, Adjacency, Contiguity, permanence, an adopted Codex, or another foundational truth. Day #103 formalized the response: Creator Idea → Constraint Review → PASS / UNCERTAIN / FAIL → narrow inquiry only when necessary → return to design. Days #105–106 added a complementary rule for deeper architecture: separate permanent spatial truth from the visible systems that may change above it.
