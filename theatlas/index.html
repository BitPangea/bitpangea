Atlas

The Canonical Map of the World

One unified supercontinent. One finite world. 21 million parcels.

The Atlas is the canonical cartographic design home of ₿itPangea: the place where the constitutional constraints of the World are translated into intentional geography, parcel design, naming, visual form, and cartographic memory.

BitPangea is constitutionally constrained, but intentionally designed. The Foundation and The Codex establish what must remain true. The Atlas is where the Creator determines how those truths are expressed as an actual World.

The Founding Constraint

BitPangea consists of one finite, complete, continuous digital World containing exactly 21,000,000 Parcels. Its Parcel population is fixed. The World does not expand or duplicate, and no central authority may alter that constitutional fact.

Current Status

Day #100 opened the Creator Period with the first deliberate exploration of BitPangea’s visible World form and Parcel arrangement. The Atlas now carries two Promising, not Adopted design directions.

For World form, BitPangea is presently being explored as a singular organic digital supercontinent inspired by Pangea as a homage to humanity’s movement into the digital age. Existing BitPangea imagery is preserved as meaningful pre-Creator visual precedent, but it does not bind the final coastline, silhouette, palette, or cartographic representation.

For Parcel arrangement, the leading design candidate is Model C1: approximately 3–5 recurring Parcel morphologies of equal fundamental area, forming one continuous Parcel fabric across the World. The forms currently carry no assigned official symbolic, functional, geographic, or hierarchical meaning.

Constitution, Design, and Implementation

BitPangea now distinguishes three complementary layers of creation.

The Foundation & The Codex

Establish what must be true: permanent constraints, constitutional identity, intrinsic structure, and governing invariants.

The Atlas

Establishes what is intentionally designed within those truths: geography, Parcel arrangement, naming, visual form, and the concrete cartographic World.

Implementation

Determines how Atlas design is technically rendered, accessed, and expressed without turning technical choices into constitutional law.

A design decision may later become constitutionally significant if changing it would alter Parcel identity, Boundary, Coordinate, Adjacency, permanence, or another adopted constitutional dependency. Until such a trigger exists, design remains design.

The Creator Period Not everything that exists in BitPangea must be constitutionalized. Some things are simply created within the Constitution.

Cartographic Layers

World Shape

Parcel Logic

The evolving design for exactly 21,000,000 permanent Parcels, presently led by Model C1: approximately 3–5 equal-area recurring morphologies.

Place Memory

The record of names, regions, settlement patterns, historical geography, and meanings that may emerge through the life of the World.

Principles Guiding The Atlas

  • One single connected supercontinent
  • Fixed total supply of 21 million parcels
  • Design must remain consistent with the Foundation and The Codex
  • Precise geography may be intentionally created without requiring a Codex for every feature
  • Parcel design may evolve until constitutional dependencies make particular choices permanent
  • Naming, visual form, terrain, landmarks, and cartographic conventions belong primarily to design
  • Implementation choices should remain distinct from constitutional law
  • No central authority with the power to expand or duplicate the map
  • Designed for long-term civilization building
  • World-form design should preserve the distinction between cartographic expression and constitutional ontology
  • The digital Pangea homage is a promising Creator direction rather than a constitutional requirement
  • Parcel morphology may vary while preserving equal fundamental area and peer spatial standing
  • The leading Parcel concept is controlled variation: order without sameness
  • Recurring Parcel forms initially carry no assigned official symbolic or hierarchical meaning
