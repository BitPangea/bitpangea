## 3. Compatible Succession

A compatible successor remains within the same continuity lineage only if it preserves all previously established canonical spatial meaning.

A compatible successor may:

- clarify normative language without changing meaning;
- extend exact representational capacity;
- add newly governed geometry or operations;
- strengthen validation;
- add Conformance obligations;
- add Reference Vectors;
- add another explicitly governed lossless representation;
- improve implementation-independent proof.

A compatible successor may not:

- renumber established canonical references;
- relocate an established Point;
- reinterpret an established coordinate;
- silently snap or approximate established values;
- change the mathematical identity of a previously valid object;
- alter canonical spatial meaning while claiming ordinary continuity.

Compatible evolution increments the `MINOR` component when the governing normative profile changes while preserving prior spatial meaning.

Purely editorial corrections that do not change normative meaning may retain the existing Specification identifier and SHALL be tracked through ordinary document revision history rather than by inventing new spatial meaning.

---

## 4. Incompatible Transition

A proposed change that cannot preserve the established meaning of `FSF-SPEC-1.0` SHALL NOT be represented as an ordinary compatible successor.

Such a proposal requires:

- explicit identification as an incompatible transition;
- separate higher review under the valid BitPangea authority process;
- preservation of the historical meaning of `FSF-SPEC-1.0`;
- explicit migration rules if any migration is ever authorized;
- no retroactive reinterpretation of existing canonical place.

If an incompatible successor is ever validly adopted, the `MAJOR` component SHALL change.

A new major identifier does not erase or rewrite the historical semantics of the prior major lineage.
