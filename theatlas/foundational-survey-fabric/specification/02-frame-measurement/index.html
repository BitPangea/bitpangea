Canonical Frame
& Measurement

Specification Section 02 · Derived from the Requirements Framework

The specification boundary for BitPangea’s native spatial scale, permanent orientation, global coordinate frame, and exact canonical measurement system.

This section translates accepted Foundational Survey Fabric requirements into the specification scope for canonical frame and measurement. It defines the structural requirements that the eventual formal mathematics must satisfy for scale, direction, origin, orientation, angular relationship, distance, area, and geometric length.

This page does not yet choose the final coordinate representation, positive rotational convention, angular unit, distance formula, area formula, or path-length mathematics. Those remain open until the candidate mathematical design is selected and tested against the complete requirements framework.

Specification Principle One World. One foundational frame. Many local perspectives.
02.1 · Native Linear Scale

Pang

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall use the Pang as BitPangea’s native canonical unit of linear spatial measure.

Pang defines spatial scale. It does not define Parcel identity, Survey-cell identity, display scale, physical-world distance, or ultimate spatial precision. One Pang shall have the same exact canonical meaning everywhere within the Survey Domain.

02.2 · Canonical Area

Square Pang

Canonical area shall be expressed in square Pangs. Area shall describe the exact amount of canonical BitPangea space occupied by an extent without assigning ownership, value, use, zoning, identity, or other higher-layer meaning.

The precise mathematical method used to compute canonical area remains unresolved pending selection of the Survey Fabric’s formal geometry.

02.3 · Native Directional Frame

Pankor · Panvath · Panoris · Panvel

The canonical directional frame shall use BitPangea-native directional terminology:

  • Pankor — North
  • Panvath — South
  • Panoris — East
  • Panvel — West

Higher layers may translate, rotate, mirror, rename, or otherwise present direction differently, provided those representations remain deterministically resolvable to the canonical Survey frame.

02.4 · Canonical Origin

One Permanent Zero-Reference

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall establish one permanent canonical origin from which authoritative spatial position is determined.

The origin shall serve mathematical reference rather than geographic, civic, cultural, economic, or symbolic importance. Its exact placement remains open until the final mathematical design is selected.

02.5 · Global Coordinate Frame

One Foundational Frame

All authoritative BitPangea spatial location shall resolve to one global canonical coordinate frame defined by the permanent origin, canonical orientation, native Pang scale, and exact reference mathematics.

Local, Parcel-relative, regional, dimensional, application-specific, and user-facing coordinate systems may exist above the foundation, but they shall not become competing sources of canonical World-space truth.

02.6 · Handedness and Rotation

Singular Mathematical Orientation

The canonical two-dimensional coordinate frame shall use one permanent handedness and one canonical rotational orientation so that direction, transformation, geometry, and angular interpretation remain identical across conforming implementations.

The specific positive rotational convention remains unresolved until the formal mathematical design is selected. Once established, mirrored or reversed views shall be treated only as derived transformations.

02.7 · Canonical Angular System

One Exact Way to Turn

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall define one exact canonical angular system for orientation and rotation relative to the permanent directional frame.

The specific canonical angular unit and notation remain unresolved. Degrees, headings, local directions, and other presentation conventions may be used above the foundation when they map exactly back to the canonical angular system.

02.8 · Canonical Distance

Exact Spatial Separation

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall define one exact canonical measure of straight spatial separation expressed in Pangs.

Canonical distance shall describe geometry only. Routing distance, traversal time, accessibility, travel cost, perceived distance, network distance, and other functional meanings remain higher-layer concerns. The exact distance mathematics remains open pending selection of the formal geometry.

02.9 · Canonical Area

Exact Spatial Measure

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall define one exact canonical method for determining area in square Pangs for valid canonical spatial extents.

Canonical area shall remain independent of ownership, use, value, identity, governance, or other higher-layer semantics.

02.10 · Canonical Length

Path and Boundary Length

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall define one exact canonical method for measuring the length of paths, lines, boundaries, and other line-like spatial expressions.

Canonical length shall remain distinct from straight-line spatial separation. The Survey Fabric determines geometric length; higher layers determine what the measured path or boundary means.

02.11 · Address and Measure Agreement

One Spatial Truth

Canonical Survey references and Pang-based position, distance, area, and extent shall remain exactly and reproducibly related.

The eventual specification may use direct Pang coordinates, hierarchical addressing, symbolic refinement, or another exact representation, but authoritative address and authoritative measurement must resolve to the same canonical spatial truth.

02.12 · Transformation and Scale

Transform the Representation, Not the Truth

Derived views may rotate, mirror, translate, zoom, enlarge, compress, or otherwise transform presentation, but no transformation may redefine canonical orientation, Pang scale, or authoritative World-space location.

One Pang shall remain one Pang everywhere, regardless of how a higher-layer view represents it.

Established by Requirements

Pang as native linear scale; square Pang as canonical area measure; Pankor, Panvath, Panoris, and Panvel as native directions; one permanent origin; one global canonical frame; uniform Pang scale; absolute canonical reference; singular mathematical orientation; exact canonical distance, area, and path or boundary length; and exact agreement between canonical reference and measurement.

Still Open in the Specification

Exact coordinate representation; exact origin placement; positive rotational convention; angular unit and notation; exact distance mathematics; exact area mathematics; exact path-length mathematics; and the final formal relationship between canonical addressing and Pang-based coordinate measure remain unresolved until the candidate mathematical design is selected and tested.
