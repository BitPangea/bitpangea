Spatial Expressions
& Geometry

Specification Section 04 · Derived from the Requirements Framework

The specification boundary for exact positions, extents, edges, geometric relationships, transformations, and canonical spatial equivalence.

This section translates accepted Foundational Survey Fabric requirements into the specification scope for spatial expressions and geometry. It defines the exact geometric concepts that the eventual formal mathematics must support without turning those expressions into semantic entities of their own.

The Survey Fabric shall provide one exact spatial mathematics capable of expressing positions, paths, boundaries, and extents while leaving identity, ownership, function, World membership, and civilizational meaning to higher layers.

Specification Principle One spatial mathematics. Many spatial expressions.
04.1 · Canonical Positions

Exact Spatial Position

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall support exact canonical reference to position within the valid Survey Domain.

A position shall remain a spatial expression rather than an independently identified foundational object. Higher layers may attach identity, function, or meaning to a referenced position without altering the Survey Fabric itself.

04.2 · Canonical Extents

Exact Spatial Extent

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall support exact canonical expression of spatial extents through the same foundational reference system used for position.

An extent may describe the exact geometry of space without identifying what occupies it or what that extent means.

04.3 · Endpoints and Limits

Exact Endpoint Semantics

Canonical paths, lines, boundaries, and other spatial expressions shall use exact endpoint conventions so that conforming implementations interpret their geometry identically.

Endpoint meaning shall remain mathematical rather than semantic. Higher layers determine what an endpoint represents.

04.4 · Boundary Conventions

Exact Edge and Inclusion Rules

The specification shall define exact conventions for shared edges, boundary inclusion, exclusion, contact, and extent limits.

These conventions shall establish how geometry meets without deciding what that meeting means for Parcels, Regions, infrastructure, ownership, or other higher-layer entities.

04.5 · Continuity

Exact Continuity and Discontinuity

The Survey Fabric shall provide exact mathematical foundations sufficient to determine whether valid canonical spatial expressions are continuous or discontinuous.

Continuity shall remain a derived geometric property rather than an independently stored foundational record.

04.6 · Connectedness

Exact Spatial Connection

The specification shall support deterministic evaluation of connectedness among canonical spatial references or extents.

The Survey Fabric determines whether space connects. Higher layers determine whether that connection matters.

04.7 · Intersection

Exact Common Space

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall support exact determination of whether two valid spatial expressions intersect and, where mathematically appropriate, the exact geometry of their intersection.

Intersection shall remain a geometric result, not a statement about ownership, access, conflict, legality, or identity.

04.8 · Overlap

Exact Shared Extent

The specification shall support exact determination of spatial overlap between canonical extents.

Overlap may reveal a geometric condition, but any higher-layer consequence arising from that condition remains outside the Survey Fabric.

04.9 · Containment

Exact Spatial Nesting

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall support exact determination of whether one canonical spatial expression or extent is contained within another.

Such containment shall remain mathematical. Semantic containment among Parcels, Regions, rights, institutions, or other higher-layer entities belongs above the foundation.

04.10 · Separation

Exact Spatial Separation

The Survey Fabric shall support exact determination of whether spatial expressions are separate and, where required, their canonical geometric separation.

Qualitative meanings such as near, remote, local, neighboring, or accessible remain higher-layer interpretations.

04.11 · Derived Spatial Predicates

Relationships Derived from Geometry

Touch, intersection, overlap, containment, separation, shared boundary, connectedness, and related predicates shall ordinarily be derived from the exact canonical geometry rather than stored as independent foundational facts.

The specification shall define the geometry once so conforming systems can derive the same relationships whenever needed.

04.12 · Transformations

Derived Views of Canonical Space

The Survey Fabric shall support exact interpretation of transformations such as translation, rotation, reflection, mirroring, and scaling relative to the canonical frame.

Such transformations shall not create alternate canonical truths. They remain derived views or constructions resolvable back to the unchanged foundational frame.

04.13 · Higher-Layer Geometry

Reference Without Shape Constraint

Parcels, Regions, World-form features, infrastructure, Outliers, and other higher-layer spatial entities shall be free to define exact supported geometry without being forced to align with Survey subdivisions or indexing structures.

The Survey Fabric provides reference, not shape.

04.14 · Equivalent Geometry

Exact Spatial Equivalence

The specification shall define exact canonical rules for determining when differently expressed references, extents, compositions, or geometric constructions represent the same canonical BitPangea space.

Spatial equivalence shall not imply that higher-layer entities referencing equivalent space share identity, ownership, rights, history, or function.

04.15 · No Foundational Entity Identity

Geometry Is Not an Entity

Points, lines, areas, boundaries, and related spatial forms shall not presently be treated as independent foundational object classes with their own identity or lifecycle.

They remain exact mathematical expressions that higher layers may use to define entities appropriate to their own functions.

Established by Requirements

Exact deterministic geometry; canonical positions and extents; exact endpoint and boundary semantics; one spatial mathematics for points, lines, and areas; derived continuity, connectedness, touch, intersection, overlap, containment, separation, and shared-boundary relationships; nonexclusive spatial reference; exact domain limits; derived transformations; higher-layer geometry independent of Survey subdivision; and exact spatial equivalence.

Still Open in the Specification

The final canonical primitive set, exact formal representation of positions and extents, boundary inclusion notation, line and curve representation, transformation notation, predicate algorithms, canonical equivalence algorithm, and precise composition rules remain unresolved until the formal mathematical design is selected and tested.
