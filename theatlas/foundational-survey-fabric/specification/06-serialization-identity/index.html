Specification Section 06 · Derived from the Requirements Framework

The specification boundary for canonical interchange, normative representation, standard identity, and preservation of one mathematical truth across multiple durable forms.

This section translates accepted Foundational Survey Fabric requirements into the specification scope for serialization and specification identity. It defines how authoritative Survey references, extents, and foundational spatial expressions must be represented for deterministic interchange across independent conforming implementations.

It also establishes the distinction between the mathematical truth of canonical space and the documents, encodings, versions, or verification systems used to describe and exchange that truth. Formats may evolve. Canonical spatial meaning must remain unchanged.

Specification Principle One meaning. Many durable expressions.
06.1 · Canonical Serialization

One Authoritative Interchange Form

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall define a canonical machine-readable serialization for authoritative Survey references, spatial extents, and other foundational spatial expressions.

Independent conforming implementations may use different internal data structures or storage methods, but authoritative interchange shall normalize to the canonical representation defined by the specification.

06.2 · Deterministic Encoding

Unambiguous Machine Representation

The canonical serialization shall be deterministic so that the same canonical spatial meaning produces the same authoritative serialized result.

Equivalent but noncanonical encodings may exist for convenience, but they shall normalize deterministically to the authoritative form where canonical interchange is required.

06.3 · Normative Representation

One Governing Meaning Across Formats

The Survey specification may be expressed through multiple human-readable, machine-readable, formal, archival, or future durable representations.

Those representations shall express one governing mathematical meaning and shall not establish competing spatial truths.

06.4 · Representation Consistency

Deterministic Agreement Across Normative Forms

Where multiple normative representations are maintained, the specification process shall define rules sufficient to ensure deterministic consistency among them.

If a conflict arises among normative representations, the governing specification shall provide a clear precedence or resolution rule so canonical meaning remains unambiguous.

06.5 · Specification Identity

The Standard Must Be Identifiable

The Foundational Survey Fabric specification, serialization rules, conformance profiles, and reference materials shall carry explicit identity information sufficient for independent implementations to determine which governing standard applies.

Specification identity shall identify the standard and its applicable rules without becoming part of the spatial identity of the places described by that standard.

06.6 · Versioning

Version the Standard, Not the Place

Specification documents, serialization rules, conformance profiles, and test materials may carry governed version history.

Compatible specification evolution shall preserve previously established canonical spatial meaning. A new document version shall not renumber, relocate, reinterpret, or otherwise change an existing canonical place.

06.7 · Compatibility

Preserve Established Spatial Meaning

Conforming implementations shall be able to determine whether a specification or serialization version is compatible with previously established canonical Survey meaning.

Compatible changes may clarify rules, extend capabilities, improve conformance material, or add durable representations, provided they do not alter established spatial truth.

06.8 · Spatial Meaning Independent of Format

The Format Is Not the Place

Canonical spatial meaning shall remain independent of any particular text format, binary encoding, data structure, schema language, storage system, or interchange technology.

A representation may become obsolete while the mathematical place it describes remains unchanged and recoverable through the governing specification.

06.9 · Verification Independence

Mathematics Before Verification Technology

Canonical Survey meaning shall not depend on any particular cryptographic algorithm, signature scheme, blockchain, consensus network, database, or authentication technology.

Such technologies may authenticate specifications, records, or canonical succession above the spatial mathematics, but they shall not define where a Survey reference points.

06.10 · Provenance Boundary

Identity of the Standard Is Not Identity of the Place

Specification identity, document provenance, publication history, signatures, and verification metadata may establish which standard or representation is authoritative.

Those mechanisms shall remain separate from the canonical spatial meaning of references established under that standard.

06.11 · Interoperable Exchange

Many Implementations, One Interchange Truth

A conforming implementation shall be able to emit, receive, parse, validate, and interpret canonical serialized Survey expressions without dependence on proprietary internal state or inaccessible implementation knowledge.

Canonical interchange shall therefore support independent verification and reproducibility across implementations.

06.12 · Durable Expression

Spatial Truth Should Outlive Its Formats

The governing specification shall preserve enough formal meaning that canonical Survey truth can be re-expressed in future durable formats without changing the mathematics beneath it.

New representations may be introduced where useful, but no representation shall create an alternate canonical space.

Established by Requirements

Canonical serialization and interchange; deterministic normalization to authoritative representation; explicit specification identity and versioning; persistence of spatial meaning across compatible specification evolution; independence of spatial meaning from cryptographic, blockchain, database, or verification technology; and preservation of one mathematical truth across multiple durable representations.

Still Open in the Specification

The exact machine-readable format, encoding syntax, field order, canonical byte representation, schema language, version identifier format, normative-representation precedence rule, compatibility declaration format, and cryptographic or provenance mechanisms used above the spatial mathematics remain unresolved until the formal specification and conformance model are selected and tested.
