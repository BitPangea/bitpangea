Survey Domain
& Ground Model

Specification Section 01 · Derived from the Requirements Framework

The specification boundary for BitPangea’s finite canonical Survey Domain and its permanent two-dimensional ground model.

This section translates accepted Foundational Survey Fabric requirements into the specification scope for the Survey Domain and Ground Model. It defines the properties that the eventual formal mathematics must satisfy before any exact domain geometry, coordinate encoding, addressing grammar, or implementation representation can be adopted.

This page does not yet select the final mathematical shape of the Survey Domain. It records the required structural model that the eventual specification must realize.

Specification Principle Reference capacity is not territory.
01.1 · Canonical Survey Domain

Finite Reference Domain

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall define one finite canonical Survey Domain. The domain establishes the exact scope within which canonical BitPangea spatial reference is valid.

The Survey Domain is a reference architecture, not a cartographic declaration of World membership. A location may be validly referenceable within the Survey Domain without thereby becoming BitPangea World-space.

01.2 · Dimensional Model

Two-Dimensional Planar Ground

The canonical Survey Domain shall presently be modeled as a two-dimensional planar spatial ground. Vertical, volumetric, immersive, nested, terrain, elevation, and other higher-dimensional or surface expressions belong above the Foundational Survey Fabric.

The Survey Fabric therefore anchors permanent ground position without becoming the visible, geographic, architectural, rendered, or experiential surface of BitPangea.

01.3 · Domain Coherence

One Connected Reference Space

The canonical Survey Domain shall be one connected and internally coherent reference domain rather than a collection of disconnected sectors or a domain containing arbitrary internal holes where canonical reference is unavailable.

Higher spatial layers may designate portions of that reference capacity differently without requiring corresponding gaps in the foundational reference system.

01.4 · Domain Limits

Exact Reference Extent

The Survey Domain shall possess exact mathematically defined limits that determine where valid canonical Survey reference ends.

Those limits shall not themselves be treated as BitPangea’s World Boundary, Extent expression, Verge, coastline, Exterior, or other cartographic feature. Beyond the canonical Survey Domain, there is simply no valid BitPangea Survey reference.

01.5 · World-Space Separation

Reference Capacity and World Membership

World membership shall be established by higher spatial layers using canonical Survey Fabric references. The Foundational Survey Fabric shall determine where canonical reference is valid, while higher layers determine what portions of that reference capacity constitute The World.

The Survey Domain is therefore not required to geometrically match BitPangea’s irregular supercontinental World Form.

01.6 · Higher-Layer Geometry

No Required Alignment to Survey Subdivision

Parcels, Regions, World-form features, infrastructure, Outliers, and other higher-layer spatial entities shall not be required to align their authoritative geometry with Survey Fabric subdivisions, grid lines, or indexing structures merely because those structures exist.

The Survey Fabric provides reference. Higher layers remain responsible for shape.

01.7 · Center and Internal Partitions

No Foundational Semantic Privilege

The Survey Domain shall not establish a privileged civic, cultural, geographic, or institutional center. Any mathematically derived midpoint or centroid remains a geometric property unless a higher layer assigns meaning to it.

Internal sectors, quadrants, regions, zones, or partitions used for indexing, computation, refinement, or serialization shall likewise remain semantically neutral at the foundational level.

Established by Requirements

Finite domain; planar two-dimensional ground; one connected reference space; exact domain limits; separation of reference capacity from World-space; no mandatory grid alignment for higher geometry; terrain and surface above the foundation; no privileged foundational center or semantic internal partition.

Still Open in the Specification

The exact mathematical geometry of the finite Survey Domain, its numerical dimensions, its precise boundary representation, the exact coordinate mathematics, and the final addressing/refinement system remain unresolved until the candidate mathematical design is selected and tested against the complete requirements framework.
