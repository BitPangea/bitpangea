Canonical
Operations

Specification Section 05 · Derived from the Requirements Framework

The specification boundary for the exact operations by which canonical BitPangea space is measured, composed, compared, normalized, ordered, and converted without changing its underlying truth.

This section translates accepted Foundational Survey Fabric requirements into the specification scope for canonical spatial operations. It defines the behaviors that the eventual formal mathematics must support for measurement, composition, comparison, equivalence, ordering, precision conversion, and normalization.

These operations must remain exact, deterministic, semantically neutral, implementation-independent, and incapable of silently moving, approximating, or redefining canonical space.

Specification Principle At the foundation, equal means equal. Tolerance belongs above.
05.1 · Canonical Measurement

Exact Measure of Spatial Expressions

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall support exact canonical measurement of valid spatial expressions, including straight spatial separation, area, path length, and boundary length where applicable.

Measurement shall remain geometric. Functional meanings such as travel cost, accessibility, convenience, or legal significance belong to higher layers.

05.2 · Union

Exact Spatial Combination

The specification shall support exact union of canonical spatial references or extents where mathematically valid.

Union shall produce the exact combined canonical spatial extent without determining whether the resulting space constitutes one higher-layer entity, ownership domain, Region, or other semantic construct.

05.3 · Intersection

Exact Common Spatial Result

The specification shall support exact intersection operations that determine the canonical common space shared by valid spatial expressions.

The geometric result shall remain authoritative only as spatial truth. Any legal, institutional, ownership, governance, or access consequence remains outside the Survey Fabric.

05.4 · Difference

Exact Spatial Subtraction

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall support exact difference operations sufficient to derive the portion of one valid canonical extent not shared by another.

Difference shall remain a geometric operation and shall not itself imply removal, transfer, exclusion, loss of rights, or any higher-layer semantic consequence.

05.5 · Comparison

Exact Canonical Comparison

Equality, adjacency, intersection, containment, boundary contact, and related foundational comparisons shall be evaluated exactly rather than through implementation-specific tolerance.

Approximate comparison, engineering margins, fuzzy matching, and rendering thresholds may exist above the foundation, but shall not redefine canonical Survey truth.

05.6 · Equivalence

Equivalent Space, One Canonical Meaning

The specification shall define exact rules for determining whether differently expressed references, extents, compositions, or geometric constructions represent the same canonical BitPangea space.

Equivalent space does not imply equivalent higher-layer identity, ownership, rights, history, function, or authority.

05.7 · Canonical Ordering

Deterministic Order Without Semantic Rank

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall support deterministic ordering of canonical references and derived spatial components wherever required for normalization, serialization, verification, exact reproducibility, or conformance.

Canonical ordering shall remain semantically neutral and shall not imply importance, priority, ownership rank, cultural hierarchy, or geographic significance.

05.8 · Normalization

Canonicalize Equivalence

The specification shall define deterministic normalization rules capable of converting valid noncanonical but equivalent expressions into canonical form.

Normalization shall preserve spatial meaning exactly. It shall never move a location, alter an extent, or reinterpret a valid canonical place.

05.9 · Validity

Canonical, Noncanonical, or Invalid

Conforming implementations shall be able to distinguish canonical expressions, valid noncanonical representations, malformed expressions, impossible refinement expressions, and references outside the canonical Survey Domain.

Invalid or ambiguous inputs shall not be silently assigned spatial meaning through implementation-specific guesswork.

05.10 · Precision Conversion

Lossless and Lossy Transformation

The specification shall define deterministic rules that distinguish exact lossless transformations of canonical precision from lossy reduction, truncation, approximation, or generalization.

A lossy representation may be useful for rendering, storage, transmission, or convenience, but it shall not be treated as equivalent to complete canonical truth.

05.11 · Canonical vs. Storage vs. Rendering Precision

Separate Truth From Representation

Canonical spatial precision shall remain distinct from implementation storage precision and user-facing rendering precision.

Implementations may store or display less detail where useful, provided that any reduction is recognized as a partial or approximate representation rather than a redefinition of authoritative space.

05.12 · No Silent Snapping

Operations Must Not Move Place

Canonical operations shall not silently snap references to grid lines, refinement boundaries, vertices, preferred increments, or nearby representable positions.

Snapping and alignment may exist above the foundation only as explicit intentional transformations.

05.13 · Neutral Conflict Detection

Reveal Spatial Conflict Without Adjudication

The Survey Fabric shall provide exact canonical facts and geometric results sufficient for higher layers to detect conflicts, contradictions, overlaps, or invalid spatial relationships.

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall not adjudicate the institutional, legal, ownership, access, or governance consequences of those conflicts.

05.14 · Transformation Integrity

Derived Operations Must Resolve Back to Canonical Space

Rotation, mirroring, reflection, translation, scaling, zoom, compression, and related transformations may be used by derived views and higher layers.

Such operations shall remain exactly resolvable to the canonical Survey frame and shall not redefine Pang scale, canonical orientation, or authoritative World-space location.

05.15 · Proximity

Exact Closeness Without Semantic Meaning

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall support exact spatial separation and threshold-based proximity where required.

Terms such as near, local, neighboring, remote, or convenient remain higher-layer interpretations and shall not become foundational spatial semantics.

Established by Requirements

Exact measurement; spatial union, intersection, and difference; deterministic validity and normalization; canonical ordering; neutral conflict detection; exact spatial equivalence; derived scale transformation; exact proximity; separation of canonical, storage, and rendering precision; lossless versus lossy conversion; exact comparison; and prohibition of silent snapping.

Still Open in the Specification

The exact mathematical algorithms for composition, equivalence, ordering, normalization, precision conversion, and comparison; operation syntax; error classes; edge-case behavior; canonical return forms; and computational complexity limits remain unresolved until the formal mathematical design is selected and tested.
