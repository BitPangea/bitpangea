Specification Section 07 · Derived from the Requirements Framework

The specification boundary for what canonical Survey truth must be able to represent, compute, validate, resolve, and return as an authoritative result.

This section translates accepted Foundational Survey Fabric requirements into the specification scope for computability. It establishes the conditions that the eventual formal mathematics must satisfy for finite representation, exactness, canonical precision, controlled complexity, termination, validity, and authoritative results.

This page intentionally does not claim to contain the final computability specification. Findings #71–#83 deliberately preserve several mathematical choices as unresolved. Accordingly, this section records the required computational behavior and the questions the completed specification must answer once the underlying mathematics are selected and tested.

Specification Principle Canonical truth must not only be exact. It must be reachable.
07.1 · Finite Canonical Representation

Every Authoritative Expression Must Be Finite

Every canonical Survey reference, spatial extent, and foundational geometric expression shall possess a finite representation capable of exact interpretation under the governing specification.

Canonical spatial truth shall not depend on infinitely long descriptions, irreducible approximation, or unresolved mathematical limits.

07.2 · Exactness

Canonical Results Are Exact

Authoritative Survey results shall be produced through exact deterministic mathematics rather than implementation-specific floating-point tolerance, approximation, or convergence behavior.

Approximate numerical methods may be used by higher-layer rendering, visualization, simulation, or convenience systems, but such approximations shall not establish canonical spatial meaning.

07.3 · Canonical Precision

Precision Belongs to the Mathematics

Canonical precision shall be defined by the Survey Fabric’s exact mathematical and refinement model rather than by the storage capacity, display resolution, or numerical limitations of a particular implementation.

Implementations may store, transmit, or render reduced precision where useful, but reduced precision shall remain distinguishable from the complete canonical expression.

07.4 · Representable Address Space

Exact Addressability Without an Infinite Address Requirement

The Survey Fabric shall provide exact addressability for every valid location representable under its canonical mathematics.

It shall not be required to assign finite canonical addresses to every theoretical point of an unrestricted real-number continuum merely to simulate mathematical infinity.

07.5 · Minimal Primitive Set

Keep the Foundation Primitive

The completed specification shall use the smallest practical set of canonical primitives necessary to express permanent Survey truth exactly and deterministically.

Additional primitives shall be justified only when existing primitives cannot represent a required foundational truth without ambiguity or loss.

07.6 · Controlled Complexity

Necessary Detail Without Pathological Form

Canonical expressions may become as detailed as legitimately required, but the specification shall define structural and normalization rules that keep valid expressions finitely parseable, unambiguous, and suitable for exact verification.

Redundant or pathological representations need not be accepted as canonical when an equivalent normalized form can be deterministically produced.

07.7 · Hidden-State Independence

State May Speed the Calculation, Not Change the Truth

Authoritative Survey results shall depend only on explicit canonical inputs and the governing specification.

Cache history, database sequence, random state, user identity, ownership, session context, wall-clock time, or other hidden mutable conditions shall not alter canonical spatial meaning or the result of a canonical operation.

07.8 · Deterministic Termination

Canonical Operations Must Finish

Every operation required to interpret, validate, normalize, compare, measure, serialize, or otherwise establish authoritative Survey truth shall deterministically terminate for valid finite canonical inputs.

The specification shall not require indefinite convergence, implementation-specific approximation, or potentially non-terminating interpretation to determine canonical meaning.

07.9 · Validity States

Exact or Invalid

A candidate canonical reference, extent, or foundational expression shall either resolve exactly under the governing specification or fail validation as canonical Survey truth.

Unknown, unresolved, estimated, approximate, pending, or disputed states may be represented by higher layers, but shall not be treated as canonical spatial truth within the Foundational Survey Fabric.

07.10 · Lossless and Lossy Results

Precision Loss Must Be Detectable

The specification shall distinguish exact lossless transformations from lossy reduction, truncation, approximation, or generalization.

A result that discards canonical precision may remain useful as a derived representation, but shall not be represented as fully equivalent to the authoritative expression.

07.11 · Authoritative Result

What Counts as Canonical Output

An authoritative Survey result shall be one that is valid under the governing specification, derived solely from explicit canonical inputs, exactly and finitely resolvable, deterministically reproducible, and expressible in the canonical form required for that operation.

A result that depends on approximation, hidden state, unresolved ambiguity, silent snapping, or implementation-specific tolerance shall not constitute canonical Survey truth.

07.12 · Implementation Independence

Many Implementations, One Result

Independent conforming implementations shall be capable of deriving identical canonical results from identical canonical inputs.

Internal algorithms, optimization strategies, caches, storage models, and programming languages may differ so long as they do not alter the authoritative answer.

07.13 · Computational Limits

Limits Must Not Redefine Canonical Truth

Implementations may impose practical resource limits on memory, runtime, storage, or expression size, but such implementation limits shall not redefine the underlying canonical mathematics.

The exact relationship between formal computability guarantees and practical implementation limits remains unresolved until the candidate mathematical design is selected and tested.

07.14 · Specification Completion Condition

Requirements Before Mathematics, Mathematics Before Conformance

This section cannot become a complete formal computability specification until the Survey Domain mathematics, coordinate frame, canonical addressing model, geometric primitives, canonical operations, and serialization rules are formally selected.

Once those dependencies are resolved, this section shall define the exact computational rules by which conforming implementations establish authoritative Survey results.

Established by Requirements

Finite exact representation; exact canonical mathematics; distinction between canonical, storage, and rendering precision; detectable loss of precision; exact comparison; no silent snapping; finite addressability without pretending an unrestricted real-number continuum fits into canonical addressing; minimal primitives; hidden-state independence; deterministic termination; controlled complexity; exact-or-invalid canonical states; and implementation-independent authoritative results.

Not Yet Possessed

The completed computability specification does not yet exist. Exact primitive encodings, canonical numeric representation, refinement mathematics, operation algorithms, formal complexity constraints, parser limits, termination proofs, canonical error taxonomy, authoritative return structures, and executable conformance rules depend on mathematical choices that Findings #1–#85 intentionally left unresolved.
