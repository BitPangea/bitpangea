Canonical Addressing
& Refinement

Specification Section 03 · Derived from the Requirements Framework

The specification boundary for how BitPangea names canonical place, increases precision, preserves address meaning, and normalizes equivalent spatial references.

This section translates accepted Foundational Survey Fabric requirements into the specification scope for canonical addressing and refinement. It defines what the eventual address grammar and refinement mathematics must accomplish without prematurely fixing their final syntax or encoding.

The governing requirement is that canonical address meaning remain exact, deterministic, permanent, self-resolving, and extensible. Greater precision must extend the established reference system rather than renumber, relocate, reinterpret, or silently snap existing canonical places.

Specification Principle Precision by extension, not migration.
03.1 · Universal Addressability

Every Valid Representable Place Can Be Named

Every valid location representable within the canonical Survey Domain shall be addressable whether or not any Parcel, infrastructure element, stored object, or other higher-layer entity exists there.

Addressability shall not depend on materialization. The canonical address space may be complete while stored records remain sparse.

03.2 · Canonical Address Meaning

Permanent and Deterministic Reference

A canonical Survey address shall always resolve to the same canonical spatial meaning under the governing specification. The same canonical spatial meaning shall normalize to the same authoritative reference.

Display forms, aliases, encodings, human-readable labels, and local coordinate views may evolve, but they shall not redefine the canonical place beneath them.

03.3 · Address Grammar

Self-Resolving Canonical Structure

The canonical address grammar shall contain enough mathematical structure for a conforming implementation to resolve the spatial meaning of a valid address directly from the governing specification and the reference itself.

Canonical Survey references shall not depend on an authoritative lookup registry that assigns meaning to otherwise opaque identifiers.

03.4 · Absolute Reference

Independent of Higher-Layer Objects

Canonical Survey references shall be absolute within BitPangea’s one global canonical coordinate frame.

Their foundational meaning shall not depend on Parcels, Regions, landmarks, neighboring entities, ownership, or any other higher-layer object or relationship.

03.5 · Extensible Refinement

More Precision Without Rewriting Place

The Foundational Survey Fabric shall support additional canonical precision through refinement that extends established spatial reference rather than replacing it.

Finer refinement shall not renumber, relocate, reinterpret, or invalidate previously established canonical references. Higher layers shall remain able to preserve their identities and spatial relationships regardless of later refinement depth.

03.6 · Refinement Depth

Precision Without Semantic Tiers

Canonical refinement depth or equivalent precision shall be representable without assigning civilizational, functional, object-specific, or application-specific meaning to any particular depth.

Higher layers may define convenient resolution profiles or names, but those shall remain exact translations of the underlying canonical refinement mathematics.

03.7 · Sub-Pang Precision

Refinement Below the Pang

Spatial positions, distances, and extents smaller than one Pang shall be expressible through the Survey Fabric’s exact refinement mathematics.

The Pang remains the native canonical scale. The specification shall not require a permanently named hierarchy of subunits unless later formal design proves such names necessary.

03.8 · Finite Exact Representation

No Pretended Infinite Address

The canonical address system shall not require every theoretical real-number point to possess a finite address.

Every valid location representable under the canonical Survey mathematics shall be exactly addressable, and actual canonical references shall remain finite and deterministically computable.

03.9 · Canonicalization

One Authoritative Reference for One Meaning

The specification shall define deterministic normalization rules so that mathematically equivalent valid representations resolve to one canonical Survey reference.

Alternative textual forms, aliases, local-coordinate expressions, and equivalent encodings may exist, but canonicalization shall preserve place rather than alter it.

03.10 · No Reuse

Canonical References Remain Permanent

A canonical Survey reference shall never be reused or reassigned to a different spatial meaning because a higher-layer entity moves, disappears, changes ownership, or ceases to exist.

A place may become unused. Its canonical address shall not become available for somewhere else.

03.11 · Address and Measurement Agreement

Reference and Pang Mathematics Must Agree

Canonical Survey references and Pang-based position, distance, and extent shall remain exactly and reproducibly related.

The eventual design may use direct coordinates, hierarchical addressing, symbolic refinement, or another exact representation, but address meaning and canonical measurement shall resolve to the same spatial truth.

03.12 · No Silent Snapping

Normalization Does Not Move Place

Canonicalization shall normalize only mathematically equivalent references. It shall not move a location to a nearby grid line, refinement boundary, preferred increment, vertex, or other derived structure.

Any snapping or alignment performed by higher layers shall be an explicit spatial change rather than an interpretation of canonical address meaning.

Established by Requirements

Universal addressability within the valid Survey Domain; permanent deterministic address meaning; refinement by extension; self-resolving canonical addresses; non-reuse of references; deterministic canonicalization; absolute foundational reference; exact agreement between address and Pang measurement; sub-Pang precision through refinement; refinement depth without semantic tiers; and finite exact addressability rather than an unrestricted real-number continuum.

Still Open in the Specification

The exact address syntax, canonical grammar, encoding alphabet, coordinate-versus-hierarchical representation, refinement notation, branching or subdivision mathematics, maximum refinement policy, canonical normalization algorithm, and final machine-readable serialization remain unresolved until the formal mathematical design is selected and tested.
