Requirements Framework · Findings #78–#85

The rules ensuring that canonical spatial truth remains finite, exact, computable, verifiable, and independently conformable.

This section establishes the final requirements gate for the Foundational Survey Fabric. Canonical spatial references, extents, and geometric expressions must be finitely representable, exactly computable, free from hidden-state dependence, and governed by deterministic rules that terminate and remain suitable for independent verification.

These findings also define the path from requirements into formal specification: a minimal canonical primitive set, controlled complexity, exact rather than vague canonical states, a mandatory conformance core, and a readiness gate that a candidate mathematical design must survive before it can advance into prototype testing.

Atlas Working Principle Canonical truth must not only be exact. It must be reachable.
Requirement Finding #78

Finitely Representable Canonical Truth

Every canonical Foundational Survey Fabric reference, spatial extent, and foundational geometric expression should be finitely representable and deterministically computable under the governing specification. Canonical spatial truth should not depend on infinitely long descriptions, irreducible numerical approximation, implementation-specific convergence, or spatial expressions whose authoritative meaning cannot be conclusively resolved. The Survey Fabric may support extensible refinement to extraordinary future precision, but every actual canonical reference or spatial claim should possess a finite exact representation.

Governing PrincipleSpatial truth may be extraordinarily precise. It must still be finitely knowable.
Requirement Finding #79

Minimal Canonical Primitive Set

The Foundational Survey Fabric should ultimately use the smallest practical set of canonical geometric primitives necessary to express permanent BitPangea spatial truth exactly and deterministically. Higher layers may introduce richer spatial constructions, representations, and domain-specific geometry provided their authoritative spatial meaning can be reduced or mapped exactly to the canonical Survey mathematics. New foundational primitives should be added only when existing primitives cannot represent a required permanent spatial truth without loss or ambiguity.

Governing PrincipleKeep the foundation primitive. Let complexity compose above it.
Requirement Finding #80

No Hidden-State Dependence

Authoritative Foundational Survey Fabric results should be determined solely by the governing canonical specification and explicit canonical inputs. Hidden implementation state, cache history, database sequence, randomization, session context, user identity, ownership state, wall-clock time, or other external mutable conditions should not alter canonical spatial meaning or geometric results. Implementations may use internal state for efficiency, but such state must not affect the authoritative answer.

Governing PrincipleState may speed the calculation. It must not change the truth.
Requirement Finding #81

Canonical Truth Must Terminate

Every canonical Foundational Survey Fabric operation required to interpret, validate, normalize, compare, measure, serialize, or otherwise establish authoritative spatial truth should be deterministically computable to completion for valid finite canonical inputs. The governing mathematics should not require implementation-specific approximation, indefinite convergence, or potentially non-terminating interpretation in order to determine a canonical result. Computational efficiency may vary by implementation, but authoritative spatial meaning must remain finitely resolvable.

Governing PrincipleCanonical truth must not only be exact. It must be reachable.
Requirement Finding #82

Controlled Canonical Complexity

The Foundational Survey Fabric should permit canonical spatial expressions to become as detailed as legitimately required, but the governing specification should define deterministic structural and normalization rules that keep valid expressions finitely parseable, unambiguous, and suitable for exact verification. Exactness should not require acceptance of arbitrarily redundant or pathological representations when an equivalent canonical form can be deterministically produced. Specific implementation limits on size or complexity should remain unresolved until the mathematical design and conformance requirements are tested.

Governing PrincipleAllow necessary complexity. Eliminate unnecessary ambiguity and redundancy.
Requirement Finding #83

No Vague Canonical Spatial States

The Foundational Survey Fabric should not treat unknown, unresolved, estimated, approximate, pending, or disputed spatial states as canonical spatial truth. An authoritative Survey expression should either resolve exactly under the governing specification or fail validation as a canonical reference or extent. Higher layers may represent uncertainty, approximation, incomplete knowledge, proposed geometry, or disputed claims as part of their own state and provenance without altering the exactness of the foundational reference system.

Governing PrincipleThe foundation may be silent. It should not be vague.
Requirement Finding #84

Mandatory Conformance Core

The Foundational Survey Fabric should ultimately define a mandatory minimum conformance core that every conforming implementation must satisfy in order to claim canonical compatibility with BitPangea spatial truth. Additional optional capabilities, encodings, optimizations, or extensions may be permitted where useful, provided they preserve the mandatory core and do not alter, override, or create competing canonical spatial meaning. The exact conformance profile should be defined only after the Survey Fabric’s mathematical design is selected and tested.

Governing PrincipleOne mandatory truth. Optional capability above it.
Requirement Finding #85

Readiness Gate for Mathematical Design and Prototype

A candidate Foundational Survey Fabric design should advance from requirements into formal mathematical design and prototype testing only if it can satisfy the complete accepted Survey Fabric requirements without internal contradiction, preserve all immutable BitPangea spatial truths, remain exactly and independently implementable, support compatible future refinement and extension, and allow higher spatial and civilizational systems to develop without requiring mutation of the foundational reference architecture. Readiness should be demonstrated through conformance analysis, edge-case testing, independent implementation, and deliberate attempts to expose ambiguity or hidden dependency.

Governing PrincipleDo not choose the architecture because it looks elegant. Choose it only after it survives the requirements.

Section Standing

Findings #78–#85 establish the finite knowability, computability, and conformance requirements of the Foundational Survey Fabric: every canonical truth must be finitely representable and deterministically computable; the primitive set should remain minimal; hidden state must not affect authoritative results; required operations must terminate; canonical complexity must remain controlled and unambiguous; vague or unresolved states are not canonical truth; every conforming implementation must satisfy a mandatory core; and no candidate mathematical design should advance until it survives the complete requirements framework through conformance analysis, independent implementation, edge-case testing, and deliberate attempts to expose ambiguity or hidden dependency.
