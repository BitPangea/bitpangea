Requirements Framework · Findings #16–#17

The rules by which a reproducible spatial specification becomes canonical—and how that specification may evolve without moving the World beneath it.

This section establishes the distinction between technical reproducibility and canonical authority. A Survey Fabric specification may be independently understood, implemented, and verified, but canonical standing arises only through BitPangea’s valid consensus process within immutable constraints.

It also establishes the governing rule for specification evolution: documents, conformance material, and compatible extensions may change, while previously established spatial meaning may not be renumbered, relocated, or reinterpreted through ordinary revision.

Atlas Working Principle Authority should be the result of valid consensus, not the source of it.
Requirement Finding #16

Canonical Authority Through Valid Consensus

A candidate BitPangea specification can be independently reproduced and verified, but it acquires canonical authority only through the valid consensus process established within BitPangea’s immutable constraints.

Governing PrincipleAuthority should be the result of valid consensus, not the source of it. Consensus may govern continuity; it may not redefine immutable truth.
Requirement Finding #17

Specification Evolution Without Spatial Mutation

The Foundational Survey Fabric specification may evolve through clarification, compatible extension, improved conformance material, and other changes that preserve all previously established canonical spatial meaning. No ordinary specification revision should renumber, relocate, reinterpret, or otherwise alter established spatial truth. Any proposed change that would do so must be treated as a fundamentally different class of change and subjected to whatever higher constitutional or consensus review is required.

Governing PrincipleThe specification may evolve. Established spatial meaning may not.
