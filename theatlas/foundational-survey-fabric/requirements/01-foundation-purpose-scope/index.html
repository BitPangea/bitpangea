Foundational
Purpose & Scope

Requirements Framework · Findings #1–#6

The governing boundary of what the Foundational Survey Fabric is—and what it must never become.

This section establishes the minimum permanent purpose of the Foundational Survey Fabric. It defines the architectural boundary between foundational spatial truth and the higher systems that may reference, interpret, govern, or build upon that truth.

These findings do not select the final mathematics, addressing scheme, subdivision model, implementation technology, or higher-layer architecture. They establish the conditions those later designs must respect.

Atlas Working Principle The deepest layer should preserve spatial truth, not the civilization built upon it.
Requirement Finding #1

Minimum Permanent Content

The Foundational Survey Fabric should preserve only the minimum information necessary to reconstruct BitPangea's permanent spatial reference system exactly: its canonical domain, reference origin and orientation, coordinate mathematics, refinement rules, canonical addressing rules, and conformance definitions.

The Survey Fabric should not encode ownership, civilization, rendering, infrastructure, governance, application state, or other higher-level semantics unless later inquiry proves that one of those is irreducibly necessary to spatial reference itself.

Governing PrincipleThe deepest layer should preserve spatial truth, not the civilization built upon it.
Requirement Finding #2

Survey Units Are Not Parcels

A substrate unit should not be presumed to equal a Parcel. The Foundational Survey Fabric should provide a deeper spatial reference framework against which higher layers may define, compose, bound, or anchor Parcels. The exactly 21,000,000 Parcels belong to the Parcel Cadastre, not to the unit count of the Survey Fabric. Parcel identity must not depend on the current subdivision depth of the Survey Fabric.

Governing PrincipleThe Survey Fabric defines reference. The Parcel Cadastre defines Parcels.
Requirement Finding #3

Immutability Protects Spatial Identity

Only those facts required to preserve permanent spatial identity and continuity should be immutable. At the Survey Fabric level, this includes the canonical spatial reference system and the permanent meaning of canonical addresses under its specification. At the Parcel Cadastre level, this includes the fixed population of 21,000,000 Parcels, their permanent identities, fixed positions, and authoritative territorial definitions. Higher-layer state should remain mutable unless a later inquiry demonstrates that permanence is essential.

Governing PrincipleImmutability should protect identity and continuity, not freeze development.
Requirement Finding #4

Governable and Replaceable Higher Layers

All functions not required to preserve permanent spatial identity should remain governable, replaceable, or evolvable to the greatest extent practical. Rights, state, protocols, infrastructure, services, applications, governance, economic systems, and user experience should not be embedded into the Foundational Survey Fabric or Parcel identity merely because they are important. Their durability should be determined by their function, with the deepest non-spatial systems remaining governable and higher service and experience layers remaining increasingly replaceable.

Governing PrincipleFuture occupants should inherit permanent place, not permanent 2026 institutions.
Requirement Finding #5

Extension Over Mutation

BitPangea should be designed so that future capabilities can normally be added above the permanent spatial architecture without changing Parcel identity, scarcity, fixed position, territorial integrity, canonical addressing, or the Survey Fabric itself. New capabilities should compose with established lower layers by reference and extension rather than requiring mutation of permanent spatial truth.

Governing PrincipleExtend upward. Do not rewrite downward.
Requirement Finding #6

Preserve Unknown Future Use

BitPangea’s permanent architecture should preserve semantic and technical freedom for uses that cannot be anticipated in 2026. The Foundational Survey Fabric should establish permanent spatial reference, while the Parcel Cadastre should establish permanent Parcel identity and place, without either prescribing fixed use classes, interface models, actor types, application categories, or civilizational functions. Future systems should be able to attach new meaning and capability to established place through generic reference and extension rather than requiring changes to the permanent core.

Governing PrincipleDefine the place. Do not predefine its future.

Section Standing

Findings #1–#6 define the foundational purpose and scope of the Survey Fabric: minimum permanent content, separation from Parcels and higher systems, the proper reach of immutability, the governability of non-spatial layers, extension without downward mutation, and preservation of future design freedom.
