Addressability,
Canonical Meaning
& Refinement

Requirements Framework · Findings #7–#15

The rules by which BitPangea names place, preserves canonical spatial meaning, and becomes more precise without rewriting what already exists.

This section establishes the requirements for universal spatial addressability, deterministic canonical meaning, extensible refinement, and implementation-independent interpretation across the Foundational Survey Fabric.

It also defines the separation between canonical reference and higher-layer entities, ensuring that the Survey Fabric can describe where space is without absorbing what occupies that space or forcing higher systems to share its internal structure.

Atlas Working Principle Presentation may change. Place must not.
Requirement Finding #7

Universal Addressability Within the Survey Domain

Every valid location within the canonical BitPangea Survey Domain should be mathematically addressable, whether or not any stored object, Parcel, infrastructure element, or other spatial entity presently exists there. Addressability must not require materialization; the address space may be complete while stored records remain sparse.

Governing PrincipleIf a place can exist within the Survey Domain, BitPangea should be able to name where it is.
Requirement Finding #8

Deterministic and Permanent Address Meaning

Canonical Survey Fabric addressing should be deterministic and permanent in meaning. The same valid location, interpreted under the canonical specification, should always yield the same canonical spatial reference, and the same canonical address should always resolve to the same spatial meaning. Display labels, aliases, encodings, and user-facing representations may evolve, but they must never redefine the canonical address beneath them.

Governing PrinciplePresentation may change. Place must not.
Requirement Finding #9

Precision by Extension

The Foundational Survey Fabric should support increasing spatial precision through refinement that extends the existing reference system rather than replacing it. Finer future resolution must not renumber, relocate, or reinterpret previously established canonical addresses or foundational spatial truth. Higher layers that reference the Survey Fabric—including the Parcel Cadastre—must remain able to preserve their established identities and spatial relationships regardless of later refinement depth.

Governing PrinciplePrecision by extension, not migration.
Requirement Finding #10

Independent Implementations, Identical Results

The Foundational Survey Fabric should be specified so that independent conforming implementations derive the same canonical spatial results from the same inputs. Implementation technology may vary, but canonical address meaning, position, refinement behavior, subdivision interpretation, and other authoritative Survey Fabric operations must be exact and implementation-independent. Conformance tests and reference vectors should provide an objective means of proving equivalence.

Governing PrincipleMany implementations. One spatial truth.
Requirement Finding #11

The Survey Fabric Knows Space, Not the Things in Space

The Foundational Survey Fabric should not identify, classify, or describe the entities that occupy BitPangea space. Its role is limited to providing the permanent mathematical reference system through which spatial position can be referenced. Descriptions of an entity’s location, footprint, boundary, path, or spatial extent should be established by higher layers using canonical Survey Fabric references rather than being embedded in the Survey Fabric itself. New spatial capabilities should therefore extend upward by reference without requiring modification of the foundational spatial system.

Governing PrincipleThe Survey Fabric knows space, not the things in space.
Requirement Finding #12

Derived Spatial Relationships

The Foundational Survey Fabric should not preserve adjacency, overlap, containment, or other spatial relationships among Parcels or higher-layer entities as foundational records. Such relationships should be established or derived by the layers that define those entities using canonical Survey Fabric references. Where the Survey Fabric’s own mathematics inherently produces relationships such as subdivision, containment, or neighborhood among its reference constructs, those relationships should ordinarily be deterministically derivable from the specification rather than separately stored as permanent truth.

Governing PrincipleDerive what mathematics already knows. Store only what must independently exist.
Requirement Finding #13

Survey Domain Need Not Match World Form

The canonical Survey Domain should not be required to coincide geometrically with BitPangea’s irregular World Form. The Foundational Survey Fabric may use a simpler finite mathematical reference domain, while higher spatial layers determine which referenced locations constitute BitPangea’s cartographic World and how its supercontinental form is expressed. Mathematical reference capacity not used by The World must not be treated as additional territory, an Exterior, a neighboring realm, or cartographically valid World-space merely because the Survey Fabric can reference it.

Governing PrincipleReference capacity is not territory.
Requirement Finding #14

Specification Over Materialization

The Foundational Survey Fabric should exist primarily as a compact mathematical specification rather than as a permanently materialized inventory of every possible survey unit or address. Canonical spatial references should be derivable from the specification as needed. Implementations may cache, index, precompute, or materialize portions of the Survey Fabric for performance, but those artifacts must remain reconstructable and non-authoritative.

Governing PrincipleThe permanent truth lies in the specification, not in any particular stored representation. Specify the space. Materialize only what is needed.
Requirement Finding #15

One Foundational Reference, Many Derived Views

Authoritative spatial systems above the Foundational Survey Fabric should ultimately reference the same canonical BitPangea spatial reference system rather than establishing independent competing sources of permanent World-space truth. Higher layers may define local, derived, dimensional, application-specific, or user-facing coordinate systems when useful, provided that their relationship to canonical BitPangea space can be deterministically established where authoritative World location matters. The Survey Fabric need not know or encode those higher systems.

Governing PrincipleOne foundational reference. Many derived views. Local coordinates may describe a perspective on place. Canonical Survey references preserve the place itself.

Section Standing

Findings #7–#15 establish the requirements for universal addressability, permanent canonical meaning, refinement by extension, independent implementation, semantic separation between space and the things in space, derived relationships, distinction between Survey Domain and World Form, specification over materialization, and one foundational spatial reference shared by higher systems.
