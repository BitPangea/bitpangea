Requirements Framework · Findings #34–#43

The native scale, direction, origin, and canonical frame through which BitPangea measures and orients its own space.

This section establishes the requirements for BitPangea-native spatial measurement and orientation. It defines the Pang as the canonical linear scale, requires exact distance and area relationships, establishes the native directional vocabulary, and requires one permanent origin and one global canonical coordinate frame.

These findings also preserve a strict distinction between canonical spatial measure and higher-layer presentation. External units, local frames, user-facing directions, and derived coordinate systems may exist, but they must remain exact translations of the unchanged foundational reference beneath them.

Atlas Working Principle Pang defines scale. Refinement defines precision.
Requirement Finding #34

Native Spatial Scale: Pang

The Foundational Survey Fabric should use a BitPangea-native canonical spatial scale (Pang) rather than making meters, feet, terrestrial geographic coordinates, or another external physical-world measurement system foundational. Canonical spatial relationships, position, distance, and extent should be defined within BitPangea’s own exact mathematics. Higher layers may translate those relationships into human-readable, physical-world, experiential, or application-specific units without altering foundational spatial truth.

Governing PrincipleBitPangea should measure itself in BitPangea (Pang) before others translate it into their own units.
Requirement Finding #35

Pang Defines Scale

The Pang should serve as BitPangea’s native canonical unit of linear spatial measure within the Foundational Survey Fabric. A Pang should define spatial scale rather than Parcel identity, survey-cell identity, display scale, physical-world distance, or ultimate spatial precision. The canonical Survey Domain should be measurable in Pangs through exact BitPangea-native mathematics, while finer-than-Pang precision may be expressed through the Survey Fabric’s refinement system.

Governing PrinciplePang defines scale. Refinement defines precision.
Requirement Finding #36

Canonical Spatial Separation

The Foundational Survey Fabric should ultimately define one exact canonical mathematical measure of spatial separation expressed in Pangs. That measure should describe geometric relationship within canonical BitPangea space only; routing distance, travel cost, accessibility, traversal time, perceived scale, network distance, and other functional or experiential meanings of distance should remain higher-layer concerns. The specific canonical distance mathematics should remain unresolved until the Survey Fabric’s underlying mathematical design is selected and tested.

Governing PrincipleThe Pang tells how far apart places are in BitPangea space. Higher layers decide what crossing that distance means.
Requirement Finding #37

Canonical Area

The Foundational Survey Fabric should ultimately define one exact canonical mathematical measure of spatial area expressed in square Pangs. That measure should describe the amount of canonical BitPangea space occupied by an exact spatial extent without assigning identity, ownership, use, value, zoning, or other higher-layer meaning to that area. The specific canonical area mathematics should remain unresolved until the Survey Fabric’s underlying mathematical design is selected and tested.

Governing PrinciplePangs measure distance. Square Pangs measure space. Higher layers decide what that space means.
Requirement Finding #38

Native Canonical Directions

The Foundational Survey Fabric should establish one permanent canonical orientation and directional frame using BitPangea-native directional terminology: **Pankor, Panvath, Panoris, and Panvel**. The canonical frame should remain stable and implementation-independent, while higher layers may translate, rotate, mirror, rename, or otherwise present direction differently for local, experiential, application-specific, or user-facing purposes, provided those representations can be deterministically mapped back to the canonical Survey frame.

  • **Pankor** — North
  • **Panvath** — South
  • **Panoris** — East
  • **Panvel** — West
Governing PrincipleThe World may be viewed from many directions. Its canonical orientation should not move.
Requirement Finding #39

Permanent Canonical Origin

The Foundational Survey Fabric should establish one permanent canonical origin as the exact mathematical zero-reference from which BitPangea spatial position is determined. The origin should not be required to correspond to a Parcel, landmark, settlement, capital, geographic center, cultural center, or other higher-layer feature. Its eventual placement within the finite Survey Domain should be chosen for mathematical clarity, determinism, and long-term stability when the Survey Fabric’s final mathematics are selected. Higher layers may later assign symbolic or cultural meaning to the origin without altering its foundational role.

Governing PrincipleThe origin establishes reference, not importance.
Requirement Finding #40

One Global Canonical Frame

The Foundational Survey Fabric should establish one global canonical coordinate frame for all of BitPangea, defined by a single permanent origin, canonical orientation, native Pang scale, and exact reference mathematics. Higher layers may establish local, regional, Parcel-relative, dimensional, application-specific, or user-facing coordinate systems where useful, but authoritative World-space location should remain deterministically resolvable to the single canonical Survey frame.

Governing PrincipleOne World. One foundational frame. Many local perspectives.
Requirement Finding #41

Address and Pang Measurement Must Agree

The Foundational Survey Fabric should maintain an exact deterministic relationship between canonical Survey references and spatial measurement in Pangs, but should not yet require that the canonical address itself be expressed directly as Pang coordinates or that Pang measurements be secondary to a hierarchical address. The eventual mathematical design should determine the most durable canonical representation, provided that authoritative addresses and Pang-based position, distance, and extent remain exactly and reproducibly related.

Governing PrincipleAddress tells which place. Pang tells its measure. The mathematics must make them agree.
Requirement Finding #42

Absolute Canonical Reference

Canonical Foundational Survey Fabric references should be absolute within BitPangea’s single global canonical coordinate frame rather than depend on Parcels, Regions, landmarks, neighboring entities, or other higher-layer objects for their foundational spatial meaning. Higher layers may define relative, local, object-centered, Parcel-relative, or application-specific references where useful, but authoritative World-space location should remain independently and deterministically resolvable within the global Survey frame.

Governing PrincipleRelative coordinates describe relationships. Absolute Survey reference preserves place.
Requirement Finding #43

Canonical Angular System

The Foundational Survey Fabric should ultimately define one exact canonical system for angular relationship and rotation relative to BitPangea’s permanent directional frame. That system should support deterministic interpretation of orientation and geometry across implementations, while higher layers may present angles and headings using degrees, local directions, visual conventions, Parcel-relative systems, or other derived formats. The specific canonical angular unit and notation should remain unresolved until the Survey Fabric’s mathematical design is selected and tested.

Governing PrincipleCanonical geometry needs one way to turn. Experience may describe that turn many ways.

Section Standing

Findings #34–#43 establish BitPangea’s native canonical measurement and orientation requirements: Pang as the linear spatial scale, square Pangs for area, exact spatial separation, permanent native directions, one canonical origin, one global canonical frame, exact agreement between addresses and Pang measurement, absolute foundational reference, and one canonical angular system for orientation and rotation.
