Conformance Section 07 · Specification Evolution Compatibility

The conformance rules for determining whether implementations, serializations, and specifications remain compatible as the governing Survey standard evolves.

Version Compatibility establishes how a conforming implementation proves that it applies the correct governing Survey specification and preserves established canonical spatial meaning across compatible specification evolution.

The standard may acquire new versions, clarifications, conformance materials, encodings, or optional capabilities. Existing canonical place must not be renumbered, relocated, reinterpreted, or otherwise altered merely because the specification document changes.

Conformance Principle Version the standard. Do not version the place.
07.1 · Governing Version Identification

The Applicable Standard Must Be Explicit

A conforming implementation shall be able to identify the governing Foundational Survey Fabric specification version or compatibility identity applicable to the canonical operation, serialization, or conformance claim being evaluated.

Version ambiguity shall not be permitted to alter authoritative interpretation.

07.2 · Compatible Evolution

New Versions May Extend Without Moving Place

A later compatible specification version may clarify rules, improve conformance material, add optional capabilities, or introduce additional durable representations.

Such evolution shall preserve all previously established canonical spatial meaning.

07.3 · Spatial Meaning Preservation

Established Canonical References Must Remain Stable

A compatible version shall not renumber, relocate, reinterpret, reuse, or otherwise change the canonical spatial meaning of an established Survey reference.

An implementation that applies a newer version shall continue to resolve previously established canonical references to the same authoritative place.

07.4 · Cross-Version Result Equivalence

Compatible Versions Preserve Canonical Results

Where an operation and its canonical semantics remain unchanged across compatible specification versions, identical canonical inputs shall continue to produce identical authoritative results.

A version change shall not silently alter canonical output under an unchanged rule set.

07.5 · Serialization Version Compatibility

Versioned Representations Must Preserve Meaning

Where serialization formats evolve, conforming implementations shall determine whether a serialized representation is compatible with the governing specification version and whether it can be interpreted without loss or reinterpretation of canonical meaning.

Compatible serialization evolution may change representation while preserving the same mathematical truth.

07.6 · Unsupported Version Handling

Do Not Guess Across Unknown Versions

An implementation shall not silently interpret an unknown or unsupported specification version according to a different version’s rules.

Unsupported or indeterminate versions shall be rejected or explicitly classified according to the governing Conformance rules.

07.7 · Incompatible Change Boundary

Spatial Mutation Is Not Ordinary Compatibility

A proposed change that would alter established canonical spatial meaning shall not be treated as ordinary compatible specification evolution.

Such a change belongs to a fundamentally different class of architectural or constitutional decision and shall not be disguised as routine version compatibility.

07.8 · Backward Compatibility

Previously Valid Canonical Truth Must Remain Interpretable

A compatible implementation of a later specification version shall remain able to interpret previously valid canonical references and expressions whose meaning was established under earlier compatible versions, subject to the governing compatibility rules.

Historical canonical truth shall not become ambiguous merely because the standard gains new capability.

07.9 · Forward Compatibility

Unknown Capability Must Not Be Invented

An implementation encountering a future extension or optional capability it does not understand shall not fabricate canonical interpretation for that unsupported feature.

Forward-compatibility behavior shall preserve known canonical meaning while clearly identifying unsupported capability.

07.10 · Conformance Claim Version Scope

Compatibility Claims Must Name Their Basis

Every formal conformance claim shall identify the specification version, compatibility identity, serialization version where applicable, and conformance profile against which the implementation was tested.

Compatibility shall therefore be demonstrable against a defined standard rather than asserted generically.

07.11 · Version Transition Testing

Compatibility Must Be Proven Across Change

Conformance testing shall ultimately include reference vectors and transition cases sufficient to demonstrate that compatible specification evolution preserves established spatial meaning and required canonical results.

Version transition tests should expose unintended reinterpretation before a compatibility claim is accepted.

07.12 · Version Compatibility Failure

Meaning Drift Is a Conformance Failure

If an implementation changes established canonical meaning solely because it applies a different supposedly compatible specification version, that behavior shall constitute a conformance failure.

Compatibility requires continuity of canonical spatial truth, not merely successful parsing of a newer document or format.

Established by Requirements

The requirements establish canonical authority through valid consensus, compatible specification evolution without spatial mutation, explicit version and specification identity, persistence of spatial meaning across compatible versions, one meaning across durable representations, and the principle that verification or implementation technology may evolve without changing canonical Survey truth.

Still Open in Conformance

The exact version identifier format, compatibility matrix, transition rules, backward-compatibility guarantees, forward-compatibility behavior, deprecation policy, required transition vectors, machine-readable compatibility declarations, and formal classification of incompatible change remain unresolved until the Specification and Conformance model are completed.
