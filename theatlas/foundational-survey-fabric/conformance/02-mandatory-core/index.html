Conformance Section 02 · Required Canonical Compatibility

The minimum set of canonical behaviors every implementation must satisfy before it may claim Foundational Survey Fabric compatibility.

The Mandatory Core is the direct conformance realization of Requirements Finding #84. It establishes that Foundational Survey Fabric compatibility cannot be defined by selective support for convenient portions of the Specification.

Every conforming implementation must ultimately satisfy one common body of mandatory canonical behavior. Optional encodings, optimizations, extensions, interfaces, or implementation features may exist above that core, but none may weaken, override, or create competing spatial truth.

Conformance Principle One mandatory truth. Optional capability above it.
02.1 · Purpose of the Mandatory Core

Establish the Minimum Claim of Compatibility

The Mandatory Core shall define the minimum canonical capabilities and behaviors that every implementation must demonstrate before claiming compatibility with the Foundational Survey Fabric.

An implementation that does not satisfy the Mandatory Core shall not claim Foundational Survey Fabric conformance, regardless of how many optional features or higher-layer capabilities it provides.

02.2 · One Canonical Meaning

No Competing Foundational Truth

All Mandatory Core behavior shall resolve to the same canonical Survey meaning defined by the governing Specification.

Implementations may differ internally, but the Mandatory Core shall not permit alternate interpretations of canonical address meaning, geometry, measurement, normalization, serialization, or authoritative result.

02.3 · Required Interpretation

Canonical Inputs Must Resolve Correctly

Every conforming implementation shall correctly interpret the canonical references and foundational spatial expressions required by the governing Specification.

Correct interpretation includes preserving permanent spatial meaning, respecting the canonical frame and measurement system, and resolving valid expressions without dependence on hidden implementation state.

02.4 · Required Validation

Valid and Invalid States Must Be Distinguished

The Mandatory Core shall require deterministic validation of canonical references and expressions within its scope.

Canonical input, valid noncanonical input, malformed expressions, impossible refinement, out-of-domain references, and other invalid states shall be distinguished according to the governing Specification rather than implementation-specific guesswork.

02.5 · Required Canonicalization

Equivalent Meaning Must Normalize Identically

Where the Specification permits multiple equivalent representations, every conforming implementation shall normalize them to the same authoritative canonical meaning and required canonical form.

Canonicalization shall preserve place exactly and shall not silently move, snap, approximate, or reinterpret spatial meaning.

02.6 · Required Exactness

Canonical Truth Is Not Tolerance-Based

Mandatory Core operations shall use the exact comparison, measurement, equivalence, and geometric behavior established by the Specification.

Tolerance-based equality, floating approximation, hidden snapping, or implementation-specific convergence shall not substitute for canonical exactness.

02.7 · Required Result Equivalence

Identical Inputs, Identical Authoritative Results

Independent implementations receiving identical valid canonical inputs shall produce identical authoritative results for every Mandatory Core operation within scope.

Performance, programming language, storage model, cache strategy, internal representation, and optimization may differ without altering the canonical answer.

02.8 · Required Serialization

Canonical Results Must Be Interchangeable

Every conforming implementation shall support the canonical interchange representation required by the governing Specification for Mandatory Core references, expressions, and results.

Alternative internal or presentation formats may be used, but they shall not replace the canonical representation where authoritative interchange is required.

02.9 · Specification and Version Identity

The Governing Standard Must Be Explicit

A Mandatory Core conformance claim shall identify the governing Foundational Survey Fabric specification and applicable version or compatibility identity.

Conformance shall therefore be asserted against a defined canonical standard rather than against an unspecified interpretation of the Survey Fabric.

02.10 · Independent Reproducibility

The Core Must Be Open to Independent Proof

Mandatory Core conformance shall be independently testable from openly inspectable specifications, conformance rules, and reference vectors.

No proprietary software, unpublished algorithm, exclusive service, secret convention, or institutional permission shall be required to understand or reproduce the canonical result.

02.11 · Optional Capability Boundary

Optional Features May Extend, Never Replace

Optional conformance classes, representations, accelerators, extensions, or implementation conveniences may provide additional capabilities above the Mandatory Core.

Such capabilities shall not alter Mandatory Core semantics, weaken its exactness, bypass its validation behavior, or create an alternate source of canonical spatial truth.

02.12 · Failure of the Mandatory Core

Core Failure Prevents Compatibility Claim

Failure of any required Mandatory Core behavior shall prevent an implementation from claiming full Foundational Survey Fabric compatibility.

Partial implementation may be described accurately for development or testing purposes, but it shall not be represented as conforming unless the governing Conformance specification explicitly defines a permissible nonconforming or provisional status.

Established by Requirements

Finding #84 requires a mandatory minimum conformance core for every implementation claiming canonical compatibility. Findings #10, #24, #30, #57–#60, #63–#66, #71–#75, and #78–#83 further establish the exactness, reproducibility, normalization, interchange, finite representation, validity, and implementation independence that the Mandatory Core must ultimately prove.

Still Open in Conformance

The exact Mandatory Core test inventory, operation list, required reference vectors, canonical test identifiers, pass thresholds where binary pass/fail is insufficient, declaration syntax, and treatment of provisional implementations remain unresolved until the Specification and remaining Conformance sections are complete.
