Conformance Section 06 · Canonical Interchange Compatibility

The conformance rules ensuring that independent implementations exchange, parse, normalize, and reproduce canonical Survey representations without ambiguity.

Serialization Compatibility establishes how a conforming implementation proves that its machine-readable Survey expressions are compatible with the canonical interchange representation defined by the Specification.

Implementations may use different internal storage models, data structures, encodings, or APIs. Where authoritative interchange is required, however, those differences must resolve to one canonical serialized meaning.

Conformance Principle Implement however you like. Exchange one spatial truth.
06.1 · Canonical Interchange Requirement

Authoritative Exchange Uses Canonical Form

A conforming implementation shall support the canonical machine-readable interchange representation required by the governing Specification for all Mandatory Core references, extents, expressions, and results within scope.

Internal representations may differ, but authoritative exchange shall normalize to the required canonical form.

06.2 · Deterministic Serialization

Same Meaning, Same Canonical Representation

The same canonical spatial meaning shall serialize deterministically to the authoritative representation defined by the Specification.

A conforming implementation shall not emit multiple competing canonical serializations for the same meaning where the Specification requires one normalized form.

06.3 · Deterministic Parsing

Canonical Input Must Be Interpreted Identically

Independent conforming implementations shall parse the same canonical serialized input to the same authoritative Survey meaning.

Parsing shall not depend on undocumented defaults, locale, implementation-specific field interpretation, database state, or other hidden context.

06.4 · Noncanonical Representation Handling

Valid Alternatives Must Normalize

Where the Specification permits valid noncanonical serialized forms, conforming implementations shall classify and normalize them deterministically.

Equivalent noncanonical forms may be accepted for convenience, but they shall not create alternate authoritative interchange truth.

06.5 · Field and Structural Integrity

Structure Must Preserve Meaning

Required fields, ordering rules, delimiters, nesting, identifiers, precision markers, and other structural elements defined by the canonical serialization shall be interpreted consistently.

Omission, duplication, malformed structure, or conflicting values shall be handled according to the governing validation rules rather than implementation-specific guesswork.

06.6 · Precision Preservation

Serialization Must Not Silently Lose Canonical Precision

Canonical interchange shall preserve the complete canonical precision required by the serialized Survey expression.

Truncation, rounding, lossy compression, reduced numeric precision, or other precision loss shall not be represented as equivalent canonical serialization unless the transformation is mathematically lossless.

06.7 · Round-Trip Compatibility

Serialize, Parse, and Preserve Meaning

A canonical Survey expression serialized by a conforming implementation and parsed by another conforming implementation shall preserve identical canonical meaning.

Where canonical normalization is required, the round-trip result shall normalize to the same authoritative representation.

06.8 · Cross-Implementation Exchange

Interchange Must Not Require Shared Internals

Canonical serialized Survey data shall be independently exchangeable across conforming implementations without dependence on proprietary internal structures, private schema extensions, hidden registries, or inaccessible services.

Compatibility shall arise from the published Specification and conformance rules.

06.9 · Specification Identity

Serialized Meaning Must Identify Its Governing Standard

Where required by the Specification, serialized data shall carry or be unambiguously associated with sufficient specification and version identity to determine the applicable canonical interpretation rules.

Such identity identifies the governing standard and shall not become part of the spatial identity of the place being described.

06.10 · Unknown and Unsupported Forms

Unsupported Serialization Must Fail Clearly

A conforming implementation shall not silently reinterpret an unknown, unsupported, malformed, or incompatible serialization as valid canonical Survey input.

Unsupported forms shall be rejected or classified according to the governing validation and version-compatibility rules.

06.11 · Canonical Byte or Text Equality

Representation Equality Depends on the Specification

Where the Specification defines a unique canonical byte sequence, text form, or other normative serialized representation, conforming implementations shall reproduce that representation exactly.

Where only canonical mathematical meaning is normative, representational equivalence shall be determined through the normalization rules defined by the Specification rather than superficial formatting comparison.

06.12 · Serialization Failure

Interchange Disagreement Is a Conformance Issue

If conforming implementations cannot exchange, parse, normalize, or reproduce a required canonical serialization consistently, the disagreement shall be treated as a conformance, implementation, or specification issue.

Proprietary convention or implementation preference shall not resolve a disagreement in canonical interchange truth.

Established by Requirements

The requirements establish canonical serialization and interchange, deterministic normalization, one mathematical meaning across durable representations, implementation independence, exact canonical precision, and the requirement that independently conforming systems exchange and reproduce authoritative Survey truth without ambiguity.

Still Open in Conformance

The exact canonical serialization format, field ordering, byte representation, schema language, text encoding, normalization procedure, required round-trip tests, compatibility fixtures, machine-readable error codes, and formal interchange test suite remain unresolved until the Specification is complete.
