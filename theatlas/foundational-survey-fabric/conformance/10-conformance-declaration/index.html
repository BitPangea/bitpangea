Conformance Section 10 · Formal Compatibility Evidence

The formal record by which an implementation demonstrates, limits, and declares its compatibility with the Foundational Survey Fabric Specification.

Conformance Report / Declaration is the final evidentiary layer of Foundational Survey Fabric conformance. It records what was tested, against which governing specification, under which conformance classes, with what results, and therefore what compatibility claim the implementation is entitled to make.

A declaration is not a substitute for proof. It is the structured statement produced from conformance evidence. The report preserves that evidence; the declaration summarizes the resulting compatibility claim.

Conformance Question How does software prove that it correctly implements the Specification?
10.1 · Purpose of the Conformance Report

Preserve the Evidence Behind the Claim

A conformance report shall preserve the evidence necessary to evaluate whether an implementation satisfies the applicable Foundational Survey Fabric Conformance requirements.

The report shall identify the tested implementation, governing specification identity, applicable version, declared conformance classes, required reference vectors, test outcomes, failures, unsupported capabilities, and any other evidence required by the governing Conformance framework.

10.2 · Purpose of the Declaration

State Only What the Evidence Supports

A conformance declaration shall state the compatibility claim established by the completed report.

It shall not claim broader compatibility than the tested evidence supports and shall clearly identify the specification version, conformance scope, and any limitations applicable to the declaration.

10.3 · Implementation Identity

The Tested Software Must Be Identifiable

The report shall identify the implementation with sufficient precision to distinguish the tested software from materially different builds, configurations, forks, or releases.

The exact identity mechanism remains open, but it should permit later verification of what implementation was actually evaluated.

10.4 · Governing Specification Identity

The Standard Under Test Must Be Explicit

Every report shall identify the governing Foundational Survey Fabric Specification and applicable version or compatibility identity against which conformance was evaluated.

A declaration without a defined governing standard shall not constitute a complete compatibility claim.

10.5 · Conformance Class Scope

The Claim Must Define Its Boundaries

The report shall identify each conformance class claimed by the implementation, including the Mandatory Core and any optional capability classes that have been successfully demonstrated.

Capabilities that were not tested, are unsupported, remain experimental, or fall outside the claimed scope shall be identified separately rather than implied as conforming.

10.6 · Test and Reference Vector Record

Canonical Expectations Must Be Traceable

The report shall record the required conformance tests and Reference Vectors applicable to the declared scope, together with their expected authoritative outcomes and the implementation’s observed results.

Evidence shall be sufficient to determine whether the implementation reproduced the canonical behavior required by the Specification.

10.7 · Result Classification

Pass, Fail, Unsupported, and Indeterminate Must Be Distinguished

Each applicable conformance test shall ultimately receive a deterministic disposition defined by the Conformance framework.

Successful behavior, implementation failure, unsupported optional capability, specification ambiguity, and other non-equivalent outcomes shall not be collapsed into one generic status.

10.8 · Failure Disclosure

Failures Must Remain Visible

A conformance report shall preserve all material failures, mismatches, unresolved ambiguities, or unsupported mandatory capabilities discovered during testing.

A declaration shall not omit known evidence that invalidates or materially limits the compatibility claim.

10.9 · Independent Reproducibility

The Evidence Must Be Verifiable by Others

The report should preserve enough information for an independent evaluator to reproduce the applicable tests and confirm the claimed result from the same governing materials.

Where independent implementation or evaluator evidence is required, that evidence shall be referenced explicitly.

10.10 · Declaration Integrity

The Claim Must Not Exceed the Proof

A conformance declaration shall be invalid if it claims compatibility beyond the tested specification version, demonstrated conformance classes, or successfully completed Mandatory Core requirements.

The declaration shall accurately represent the boundaries of the proven compatibility.

10.11 · Report and Declaration Versioning

Evidence Must Remain Bound to What Was Tested

Conformance reports and declarations shall carry sufficient identity and version information to determine which implementation build, specification version, test corpus, and conformance rules produced the recorded result.

A later implementation release or specification revision shall not automatically inherit an earlier declaration without whatever reevaluation the governing Conformance framework requires.

10.12 · Provenance and Authenticity

The Record May Be Verified Without Becoming Spatial Truth

Conformance reports and declarations may use signatures, hashes, publication records, provenance systems, or other verification mechanisms to establish authenticity and integrity.

Those mechanisms authenticate the conformance record; they do not define canonical spatial meaning.

10.13 · Machine-Readable Declaration

Conformance Should Be Interoperably Expressible

The completed Conformance framework should support a deterministic machine-readable representation of the declaration so that software, registries, validators, and future infrastructure can evaluate compatibility claims consistently.

The exact declaration schema and serialization remain unresolved until the Conformance model is finalized.

10.14 · Human-Readable Declaration

The Claim Must Also Be Understandable

Conformance should also be expressible in a concise human-readable form that identifies what implementation was tested, against what specification, for which conformance classes, and with what overall result.

Human-readable summaries shall remain faithful to the underlying machine-verifiable evidence.

10.15 · Reassessment Boundary

Material Change May Require New Proof

Material changes to implementation behavior, governing specification version, serialization rules, conformance classes, or Mandatory Core requirements may require renewed conformance evaluation.

The exact reassessment triggers remain to be defined, but a declaration shall not be treated as timeless evidence for software or rules that materially differ from what was tested.

10.16 · Final Conformance Standing

Proof Produces the Claim

An implementation may declare Foundational Survey Fabric compatibility only after satisfying the applicable Mandatory Core, all requirements of each claimed conformance class, the governing validation and exactness rules, serialization and version compatibility, independent implementation obligations, and applicable failure conditions.

The declaration is therefore the outcome of the Conformance framework—not an assertion that precedes it.

Established by Requirements

The requirements establish independent reproducibility, canonical result equivalence, exactness, deterministic validation, canonical interchange, specification identity and versioning, a mandatory conformance core, and readiness evidence through conformance analysis, reference testing, independent implementation, edge cases, and deliberate attempts to expose ambiguity or hidden dependency.

Still Open in Conformance

The exact report schema, declaration schema, machine-readable format, evaluator identity requirements, evidence-retention rules, certification authority if any, signing or provenance mechanism, test-run identifiers, reassessment triggers, publication process, and formal status vocabulary remain unresolved until the complete Conformance framework and Specification are finalized.
