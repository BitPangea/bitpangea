Reference Vector Section 12 · Adversarial Spatial Testing

The adversarial canonical test set for exposing ambiguity, hidden assumptions, implementation shortcuts, and failures at the limits of exact Survey behavior.

Pathological Edge Cases define the reference cases through which the Foundational Survey Fabric is deliberately stressed at its most difficult boundaries: exact contact, extreme refinement, structurally different but equivalent geometry, complex finite extents, normalization stress, precision limits, and other cases most likely to reveal ambiguity or implementation dependence.

These vectors are not exceptions to the Specification. They are proof that the Specification remains exact when the easy cases end. The final corpus cannot yet be constructed because the formal mathematics, refinement model, serialization, and normalization rules remain unresolved. This page therefore establishes the adversarial categories the completed architecture must survive.

Reference Vector Question Given this exact input, what exact answer must every conforming implementation produce?
12.1 · Exact Boundary-Contact Cases

Touching at the Limit Must Not Become Overlap or Separation

The vector set shall include spatial expressions that meet at exact canonical boundaries, endpoints, vertices, or shared edges without interior overlap.

Conforming implementations shall distinguish exact contact from overlap and separation according to the governing boundary semantics.

12.2 · Equivalent but Structurally Different Geometry

Different Construction Must Not Create Different Truth

Reference vectors shall include geometrically equivalent extents expressed through substantially different valid structures, orderings, decompositions, or compositions.

Every conforming implementation shall recognize the same canonical spatial meaning despite representational complexity.

12.3 · Deepest Tested Refinement

Extreme Precision Must Remain Exact and Reachable

The corpus shall include references at the deepest refinement depth selected for practical conformance testing.

These cases shall prove finite parsing, exact resolution, deterministic termination, and preservation of canonical meaning under extreme but valid precision.

12.4 · Complex Finite Extent Cases

High Complexity Must Remain Finitely Knowable

Reference vectors shall include valid spatial extents with substantial but finite geometric complexity.

Conforming implementations shall interpret, normalize, compare, measure, and serialize such expressions without ambiguity or nontermination.

12.5 · Normalization Stress Cases

Redundancy and Reordering Must Still Converge

The vector set shall include highly redundant, deeply nested, differently ordered, or compositionally complex valid expressions that nevertheless resolve to a simpler canonical meaning.

Normalization shall remove unnecessary representational complexity without changing spatial truth.

12.6 · Precision-Boundary Cases

Nearness Must Not Become Identity

The corpus shall include references or measurements positioned immediately on opposite sides of refinement, equality, or geometric decision boundaries.

Implementations shall not collapse distinct canonical values through tolerance, rounding, or hidden snapping.

12.7 · Domain-Limit Stress Cases

Extreme Validity Near the Survey Boundary Must Remain Deterministic

Reference vectors shall include positions and extents touching, approaching, crossing, or narrowly exceeding canonical Survey Domain limits.

Exact validity shall be determined from the formal Domain rules rather than implementation convenience or numerical tolerance.

12.8 · Transformation Composition Stress Cases

Long Transformation Chains Must Still Resolve Exactly

Where permitted by the Specification, the vector corpus shall include nontrivial compositions of rotations, reflections, translations, scaling, and inverse operations.

Equivalent transformation chains shall converge to identical canonical results without cumulative approximation or drift.

12.9 · Serialization Stress Cases

Complex Canonical Meaning Must Survive Interchange

Reference vectors shall include complex but valid canonical expressions whose serialization exercises field ordering, precision preservation, normalization, parsing, and round-trip behavior simultaneously.

Interchange shall preserve exact meaning even when the serialized expression is large or structurally intricate.

12.10 · Hidden-State Detection Cases

The Same Input Must Remain the Same Under Different Execution Histories

The vector set shall include repeated evaluations under varied cache state, execution order, process history, or other implementation conditions to prove that hidden mutable state does not alter canonical output.

State may affect performance. It shall not affect truth.

12.11 · Termination Stress Cases

Difficult Valid Input Must Still Finish

Reference vectors shall include valid finite expressions intentionally chosen to challenge parsing, normalization, geometry, comparison, or measurement complexity.

Conforming implementations shall deterministically complete the required canonical operation without indefinite convergence or unresolved recursion.

12.12 · Ambiguity-Exposure Cases

The Corpus Should Attack the Specification, Not Merely the Code

The edge-case corpus shall include cases specifically designed to expose underspecified behavior, conflicting interpretation, or hidden dependency in the governing Specification itself.

If independent implementations reasonably disagree because the Specification does not determine one result, the case shall reveal a specification ambiguity rather than be concealed as implementation variation.

12.13 · Adversarial Equivalence Cases

Canonical Equality Must Survive Hostile Representation

Reference vectors shall include expressions intentionally structured to make equivalent geometry difficult to recognize through superficial textual or structural comparison.

Exact equivalence shall depend on canonical mathematics rather than representational similarity.

12.14 · Cross-Implementation Stress Agreement

Every Implementation Must Survive the Same Hard Cases

Independent conforming implementations shall produce identical authoritative results for every valid pathological case and identical rejection or classification for every invalid adversarial case.

Divergence under stress is evidence of implementation defect, specification ambiguity, version incompatibility, or hidden dependency requiring correction before full architectural confidence is warranted.

Established by Requirements and Conformance Scope

The Survey Fabric must remain exact, finitely representable, independently reproducible, hidden-state independent, deterministically terminating, normalization-stable, resistant to silent snapping or precision loss, and capable of exposing ambiguity or hidden dependency through deliberate edge-case and adversarial testing.

Canonical Vector Corpus Not Yet Possessed

Actual pathological fixtures, deepest tested refinement values, complex finite extents, adversarial normalization inputs, transformation chains, serialization stress cases, ambiguity probes, and authoritative expected results cannot be created until the formal Survey mathematics and complete conformance rules are selected and tested.
