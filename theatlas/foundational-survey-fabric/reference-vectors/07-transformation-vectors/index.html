Reference Vector Section 07 · Derived Spatial Transformation

The canonical test set for proving exact rotation, reflection, translation, scaling where permitted, inverse transformation, and resolution back to canonical Survey space.

Transformation Vectors define the reference cases through which implementations will prove that derived spatial transformations preserve one underlying canonical Survey truth.

The final vector corpus does not yet exist because the precise transformation notation, canonical angular system, scaling rules, and formal transformation mathematics remain unresolved within the Specification. This page therefore establishes the required transformation tests without inventing canonical formulas prematurely.

Reference Vector Question Given this exact input, what exact answer must every conforming implementation produce?
07.1 · Rotation Vectors

Canonical Rotation Must Resolve Exactly

The vector set shall include exact rotational transformations relative to the canonical Survey frame once the angular system and positive rotational convention are formally established.

Identical rotation inputs shall produce identical transformed results across conforming implementations.

07.2 · Reflection Vectors

Mirroring Must Remain a Derived View

Reference vectors shall include exact reflection or mirroring transformations where supported by the Specification.

A reflected result shall remain deterministically resolvable to the unchanged canonical Survey frame and shall not create a competing foundational orientation.

07.3 · Translation Vectors

Translated Geometry Must Preserve Exact Relationship

Where translation is supported as a derived geometric operation, vectors shall test exact displacement of canonical spatial expressions.

The transformed result shall remain precisely related to the original through the governing transformation rules.

07.4 · Scaling Vectors

Scale the Representation, Not the Pang

The corpus shall include exact scaling transformations where permitted by the Specification, while preserving the invariant canonical meaning of the Pang.

Scaling shall transform derived geometry or representation without redefining the foundational Survey scale.

07.5 · Inverse Transformation Vectors

Every Valid Transformation Should Resolve Back Exactly

Reference vectors shall include inverse operations sufficient to prove that a valid transformation can be deterministically resolved back to its canonical spatial source where mathematically invertible.

Inversion shall restore exact canonical meaning rather than an approximate reconstruction.

07.6 · Composition Vectors

Combined Transformations Must Compose Deterministically

The vector set shall include compositions of supported transformations such as rotation followed by translation, reflection followed by rotation, or other valid combinations.

Equivalent transformation sequences shall converge to the same canonical result where the governing mathematics establishes equivalence.

07.7 · Identity Transformation Vectors

No-Op Transformations Must Preserve Exact Meaning

The corpus shall include identity cases such as zero rotation, zero translation, neutral scale, or equivalent composed transformations.

Identity transformations shall return the original canonical spatial meaning exactly.

07.8 · Canonical Frame Resolution Vectors

Derived Views Must Map Back to One Foundational Frame

Reference vectors shall prove that transformed local, rotated, mirrored, or scaled representations can be deterministically resolved to the single canonical Survey frame where authoritative World-space location matters.

Derived coordinate systems may vary. Canonical Survey truth may not.

07.9 · Precision-Preserving Transformation Vectors

Transformation Must Not Silently Reduce Precision

The vector corpus shall include refined or sub-Pang geometry to prove that exact transformations preserve canonical precision where mathematically lossless.

Any transformation that reduces precision shall be identified as lossy rather than canonical equivalence.

07.10 · Boundary Transformation Vectors

Transformed Geometry Must Respect Domain and Boundary Rules

Reference vectors shall include transformed positions or extents that approach, touch, cross, or move beyond canonical Survey Domain limits where such cases are mathematically permitted.

Implementations shall classify resulting geometry according to the same exact Domain and validity rules applied without transformation.

07.11 · Equivalent Transformation Vectors

Different Paths to the Same Geometry Must Converge

Where multiple transformation sequences produce the same canonical spatial result, vectors shall demonstrate exact equivalence and deterministic normalization.

Different operational histories shall not create distinct canonical truths for equivalent transformed space.

07.12 · Cross-Implementation Transformation Agreement

Every Implementation Must Return the Same Transformed Result

Independent conforming implementations shall produce identical authoritative results for the same transformation inputs under the same governing Specification.

A disagreement indicates an implementation defect, specification ambiguity, version incompatibility, or unresolved transformation dependency requiring correction.

Established by Requirements and Specification Scope

The Survey Fabric must support exact interpretation of rotation, reflection, scaling, translation, and other derived transformations relative to one canonical frame; transformed views must remain resolvable to canonical space; scale transformation must not redefine the Pang; and handedness and orientation must remain singular and implementation-independent.

Canonical Vector Corpus Not Yet Possessed

Actual transformation inputs, matrices or equivalent formalism, angular values, scale factors, inverse fixtures, composed transformation cases, and authoritative expected outputs cannot be created until the final transformation and angular mathematics are selected and tested.
