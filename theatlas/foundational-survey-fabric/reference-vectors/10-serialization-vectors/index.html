Reference Vector Section 10 · Canonical Machine Representation

The canonical test set for proving exact encoding, parsing, normalization, round-trip preservation, structural requirements, and machine-readable output.

Serialization Vectors define the reference cases through which implementations will prove that canonical Survey expressions can be encoded, parsed, normalized, exchanged, and reproduced without ambiguity or loss of spatial meaning.

The final vector corpus does not yet exist because the exact canonical serialization format, field ordering, schema language, byte or text representation, and normalization procedure remain unresolved within the Specification. This page therefore establishes the required serialization tests without inventing a canonical encoding prematurely.

Reference Vector Question Given this exact input, what exact answer must every conforming implementation produce?
10.1 · Canonical Encoding Vectors

Canonical Meaning Must Produce One Authoritative Encoding

The vector set shall include canonical Survey references, extents, and foundational expressions together with the exact machine-readable encoding required by the Specification.

Where a unique canonical representation is defined, independent implementations shall emit the identical authoritative encoding for the same canonical meaning.

10.2 · Canonical Parsing Vectors

Authoritative Encoding Must Resolve Identically

Reference vectors shall include canonical serialized inputs and the exact Survey meaning each must resolve to.

Parsing shall be deterministic and independent of locale, undocumented defaults, database state, or other hidden implementation context.

10.3 · Noncanonical Serialization Vectors

Permitted Alternate Forms Must Normalize

Where valid noncanonical serialized representations are permitted, vectors shall demonstrate their exact classification and normalization to the authoritative canonical form.

Alternate encodings may aid convenience or interoperability, but they shall not create competing canonical representations.

10.4 · Field Requirement Vectors

Required Structure Must Be Testable

The corpus shall include cases proving the presence, meaning, and exact interpretation of required fields, elements, markers, or components defined by the canonical serialization.

Missing, duplicated, malformed, or conflicting required fields shall be handled according to the governing validation rules.

10.5 · Field-Order Vectors

Ordering Rules Must Be Deterministic

Where field or component ordering is normative, vectors shall prove the exact required order.

Where ordering is semantically irrelevant but canonical normalization requires one deterministic sequence, vectors shall demonstrate convergence to that sequence.

10.6 · Canonical Whitespace and Formatting Vectors

Presentation Variance Must Not Create Meaning Variance

Where text-based serialization permits insignificant formatting differences, vectors shall demonstrate which differences normalize away and which are invalid.

If the final canonical form requires exact formatting, conforming implementations shall reproduce that form exactly.

10.7 · Precision-Preserving Serialization Vectors

Encoding Must Preserve Canonical Precision

The vector set shall include refined and sub-Pang expressions to prove that serialization preserves every required unit of canonical spatial precision.

Truncation, rounding, or lossy conversion shall not be accepted as canonical interchange where exact representation is required.

10.8 · Round-Trip Vectors

Encode, Parse, and Recover the Same Canonical Truth

Reference vectors shall test canonical serialize-parse and parse-serialize round trips.

Where the representation is defined as canonical and lossless, the resulting meaning and authoritative serialized form shall be identical to the source.

10.9 · Cross-Implementation Exchange Vectors

One Implementation Must Read Another’s Canonical Output

The corpus shall include interchange cases proving that canonical output produced by one conforming implementation can be parsed and interpreted identically by another.

Exchange shall not depend on shared proprietary data structures, hidden registries, or private conventions.

10.10 · Specification Identity Vectors

Governing Standard Identity Must Be Interpretable

Where serialization carries or references specification identity, version, conformance profile, or compatibility metadata, vectors shall prove that those identifiers are encoded and interpreted consistently.

Such metadata identifies the governing standard and shall not become part of the spatial identity of the place described.

10.11 · Invalid Serialization Vectors

Malformed Machine Representation Must Fail Deterministically

The vector corpus shall include malformed, incomplete, contradictory, unsupported, or precision-corrupting serialized forms.

Conforming implementations shall reject or classify those forms consistently rather than invent canonical meaning.

10.12 · Exact Machine-Output Agreement

Every Implementation Must Produce the Required Output

Independent conforming implementations shall produce identical authoritative machine-readable output wherever the Specification defines a unique canonical serialization.

Where multiple permitted durable representations exist, each shall remain deterministically reducible to the same canonical mathematical meaning according to the governing rules.

Established by Requirements and Specification Scope

The Survey Fabric must support canonical serialization and interchange, deterministic normalization, one mathematical meaning across durable representations, explicit specification identity and versioning, precision-preserving exchange, independent implementation, and exact machine interpretation without hidden state.

Canonical Vector Corpus Not Yet Possessed

Actual canonical bytes or text, schemas, field names, field order, formatting rules, version identifiers, normalized examples, round-trip fixtures, and exact machine-readable expected outputs cannot be created until the final serialization and specification-identity rules are selected.
