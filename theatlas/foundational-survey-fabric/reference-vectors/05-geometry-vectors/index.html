Reference Vector Section 05 · Exact Spatial Predicates

The canonical test set for proving exact agreement on touch, intersection, overlap, containment, separation, shared boundary, connectedness, and related spatial predicates.

Geometry Vectors define the reference cases through which implementations will prove identical interpretation of exact spatial relationships among canonical positions and extents.

The final vector corpus does not yet exist because the formal geometric primitive set, extent representation, boundary conventions, and predicate algorithms remain unresolved within the Specification. This page therefore establishes the required geometric test categories without inventing canonical geometry prematurely.

Reference Vector Question Given this exact input, what exact answer must every conforming implementation produce?
05.1 · Touch Vectors

Boundary Contact Must Resolve Exactly

The vector set shall include spatial expressions that touch at exact canonical boundaries without overlapping in interior extent.

Conforming implementations shall return the same canonical touch predicate according to the governing endpoint and boundary conventions.

05.2 · Intersection Vectors

Common Space Must Be Identified Deterministically

Reference vectors shall include exact cases where two canonical spatial expressions intersect.

The resulting intersection predicate and any canonical intersection extent required by the Specification shall be identical across conforming implementations.

05.3 · Overlap Vectors

Shared Interior Extent Must Be Exact

The corpus shall include cases where canonical extents share nonzero spatial extent and therefore overlap under the governing geometry.

Overlap shall be distinguished exactly from mere touch or boundary contact.

05.4 · Containment Vectors

Spatial Nesting Must Resolve Identically

Reference vectors shall include cases of complete containment, partial containment where applicable, equality of extents, and containment at exact boundaries.

Conforming implementations shall derive the same canonical containment result without assigning semantic meaning to the relationship.

05.5 · Separation Vectors

Noncontact Must Be Distinguished From Contact

The vector set shall include spatial expressions that are exactly separated under the canonical geometry.

These cases shall prove that conforming implementations do not confuse small separation with touch, overlap, or equivalence.

05.6 · Shared-Boundary Vectors

Common Boundary Must Be Deterministically Derived

The corpus shall include extents that share a canonical boundary segment or other exact boundary expression as defined by the final Specification.

Shared-boundary results shall remain geometric facts and shall not imply Parcel adjacency, ownership, jurisdiction, or other higher-layer consequence.

05.7 · Connectedness Vectors

Canonical Connection Must Be Exact

Reference vectors shall include expressions that are connected, disconnected, or connected only through exact boundary contact where the governing mathematics distinguishes those states.

Each case shall produce one deterministic connectedness result across implementations.

05.8 · Continuity and Discontinuity Vectors

Continuity Must Follow the Canonical Mathematics

The vector set shall include spatial expressions that demonstrate the exact continuity and discontinuity rules of the Survey geometry.

Higher-layer meaning shall remain outside the vector result; the test concerns only canonical geometric structure.

05.9 · Equivalent Geometry Vectors

Differently Expressed Geometry May Still Be the Same Space

Reference vectors shall include differently expressed spatial constructions that resolve to the same canonical geometry where the Specification permits equivalent forms.

Every conforming implementation shall recognize exact spatial equivalence and normalize or compare those expressions consistently.

05.10 · Boundary-Inclusion Vectors

Edge Membership Must Be Unambiguous

The corpus shall include positions or extents lying exactly on boundaries to prove the canonical inclusion, exclusion, endpoint, and shared-edge conventions.

No implementation-specific tolerance shall determine whether a boundary position belongs to an extent.

05.11 · Degenerate and Limit Geometry Vectors

Edge Cases Must Still Produce One Exact Result

Where permitted by the final primitive model, reference vectors shall include zero-area, zero-length, coincident, repeated, or other mathematically limiting cases sufficient to expose ambiguity in predicate behavior.

Such cases shall be accepted, normalized, or rejected according to the formal Specification rather than implementation preference.

05.12 · Cross-Implementation Geometry Agreement

Every Implementation Must Derive the Same Predicate

Independent conforming implementations shall return identical canonical touch, intersection, overlap, containment, separation, shared-boundary, connectedness, continuity, and equivalence results for identical canonical geometry.

Disagreement indicates an implementation defect, specification ambiguity, version incompatibility, or unresolved geometric dependency requiring correction.

Established by Requirements and Specification Scope

The Survey Fabric must support exact canonical positions and extents, exact endpoint and boundary semantics, exact continuity and connectedness, and deterministic derivation of touch, intersection, overlap, containment, separation, shared boundary, equivalence, and related spatial predicates from one canonical geometry.

Canonical Vector Corpus Not Yet Possessed

Actual geometric coordinates, primitive encodings, extent fixtures, boundary cases, predicate outputs, canonical equivalence examples, and machine-readable expected results cannot be created until the final geometric primitive set, extent representation, and boundary conventions are selected and tested.
