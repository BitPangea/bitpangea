Reference Vector Section 01 · Canonical Address Resolution

The canonical test set for proving that independent implementations interpret, refine, normalize, and resolve Survey addresses identically.

Addressing Vectors define the reference cases through which implementations will prove consistent interpretation of basic addresses, refined addresses, canonicalization behavior, valid noncanonical forms, aliases where permitted, and expected canonical resolution.

The final vector corpus does not yet exist because the formal addressing grammar and refinement mathematics remain unresolved within the Specification. This page therefore establishes the required vector categories and proof obligations without inventing canonical addresses prematurely.

Reference Vector Question Given this exact input, what exact answer must every conforming implementation produce?
01.1 · Basic Address Vectors

Canonical References at Foundational Precision

The Addressing Vector set shall include basic canonical Survey references sufficient to prove that independent implementations interpret the same valid address as the same exact canonical place.

These vectors shall eventually include representative positions across the Survey Domain without assigning higher-layer meaning to those positions.

01.2 · Refined Address Vectors

Increasing Precision Without Rewriting Place

The vector set shall include canonical references at multiple supported refinement depths to prove that increased precision extends the addressing system without renumbering or reinterpreting previously established spatial meaning.

Refined vectors shall demonstrate the exact relationship between coarser and finer canonical references once the refinement mathematics are formally selected.

01.3 · Canonicalization Vectors

Equivalent Meaning Must Normalize Identically

The vector set shall include valid expressions that differ in representation but resolve to the same canonical spatial meaning where the Specification permits such equivalence.

Every conforming implementation shall normalize each applicable case to the same authoritative canonical result.

01.4 · Valid Noncanonical Representation Vectors

Recognize Valid Form Without Creating Alternate Truth

Where the Specification permits noncanonical but valid address forms, reference vectors shall demonstrate how those forms are classified and converted to the required canonical representation.

A valid noncanonical form may be accepted for interchange or convenience, but it shall not establish an independent spatial truth.

01.5 · Alias Vectors

Aliases Resolve to Canonical Reference Where Permitted

If the completed Specification permits aliases or alternate human-facing address expressions, the vector set shall demonstrate their exact deterministic resolution to canonical Survey references.

Aliases shall remain presentation mechanisms and shall not alter the permanent canonical meaning of place.

01.6 · Absolute Reference Vectors

Canonical Addressing Must Stand Without Higher-Layer Objects

Reference vectors shall demonstrate that canonical Survey addresses resolve independently of Parcels, Regions, landmarks, ownership, or other higher-layer entities.

The expected result shall be derivable solely from the canonical address and governing Survey mathematics.

01.7 · Address and Measurement Agreement

Reference and Pang Mathematics Must Resolve to One Place

Once the formal addressing and Pang-based coordinate relationship is selected, vectors shall demonstrate that canonical address resolution and canonical measurement agree exactly.

No conforming implementation may resolve an address to one place while its canonical Pang interpretation resolves to another.

01.8 · Canonical Reference Permanence

The Same Address Must Keep the Same Meaning

The vector corpus shall include stability cases sufficient to prove that previously established canonical references retain identical spatial meaning under compatible specification evolution.

Address reuse, reassignment, or reinterpretation shall never be represented as compatible behavior.

01.9 · Representation Independence

Display Form Must Not Change Canonical Resolution

Where multiple textual, machine-readable, or presentation forms represent one canonical address, vectors shall demonstrate that those forms resolve to identical canonical meaning.

Presentation may vary. Place must not.

01.10 · Boundary Between Addressing and Invalidity

Addressing Vectors Define Success; Invalid-Input Vectors Define Failure

This section shall focus primarily on valid canonical and valid noncanonical address behavior.

Malformed, impossible, ambiguous, or out-of-domain cases may be referenced here where necessary to establish the addressing boundary, but the comprehensive negative corpus belongs in the dedicated Invalid-Input Vectors section.

Established by Requirements and Specification Scope

Canonical addresses must be deterministic, permanent in meaning, self-resolving from the governing specification, absolute within one global frame, capable of extensible refinement, independently reproducible, and deterministically canonicalizable where equivalent representations exist.

Canonical Vector Corpus Not Yet Possessed

Actual address strings, refinement examples, alias forms, canonicalization outputs, encoded test fixtures, and expected machine-readable results cannot be defined until the formal address grammar, refinement mathematics, and canonical serialization are selected and tested.
