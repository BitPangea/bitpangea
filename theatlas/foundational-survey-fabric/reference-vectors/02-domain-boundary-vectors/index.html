Reference Vector Section 02 · Survey Domain Limits

The canonical test set for proving exact interpretation of the Survey origin, finite Survey Domain limits, and validity at and around the exact geometric boundary of canonical reference.

Survey Domain Limit Vectors define exact executable cases for the canonical origin and for the mathematical boundary behavior of the finite Survey Domain.

The candidate Specification now resolves the Domain's geometry and boundary form sufficiently to test it symbolically and parametrically: the Survey Domain is a closed, axis-aligned square centered at the canonical origin, represented as DH = {(x,y) : -H ≤ x ≤ H and -H ≤ y ≤ H}, where H is an exact positive CRPC value.

The remaining open question is numerical capacity under FMD-04B: the canonical value of H has not yet been selected. FMD-04A has already resolved the square geometry and closed-boundary semantics. Reference Vectors may therefore prove the exact boundary rules, inclusion semantics, origin behavior, and symbolic inside/on/outside classification, but they shall not invent a numerical half-span merely to complete the corpus.

Reference Vector Question Given this exact input, what exact answer must every conforming implementation produce?
02.0 · Current Survey Domain Model

The Geometry Is Defined; Numerical Capacity Is Still Open

The candidate Survey Domain is a finite, connected, closed, axis-aligned square centered at the canonical origin:

DH = {(x,y) : -H ≤ x ≤ H and -H ≤ y ≤ H}

with H > 0 represented exactly in CRPC. The origin is (0,0). The boundary is included because the Domain is closed.

The shape, closure, centering, axis alignment, and boundary semantics are therefore testable now under FMD-04A. The numerical value of H remains open under FMD-04B.

02.1 · Canonical Origin Vectors

The Zero-Reference Must Resolve Identically

The vector set shall include the canonical origin (0,0) and exact neighboring Points sufficient to prove that all conforming implementations interpret the zero-reference identically.

The origin is a mathematical reference point only. It does not imply Parcel identity, World membership, landmark status, civic importance, ownership, or cultural meaning.

02.2 · Symbolic Boundary Vectors

The Four Canonical Domain Limits Are Exact

The corpus shall test the four boundary equations:

x = -H, x = +H, y = -H, and y = +H.

These vectors shall prove that all conforming implementations classify Points against the same exact parameterized Domain, even before a numerical value for H is selected.

02.3 · Interior Vectors

Strictly Interior Points Must Be Valid

A Point satisfying -H < x < H and -H < y < H is strictly interior to the Survey Domain and shall be classified as valid Survey reference.

Capacity-independent fixtures may use symbolic or parameterized coordinates that prove the rule without assuming a final numerical value for H.

02.4 · Exact-Boundary Vectors

Boundary Inclusion Is Deterministic

Because the candidate Survey Domain is closed, Points satisfying the Domain inequalities with equality on one or more sides are valid Survey references.

Examples include Points with x = ±H and -H ≤ y ≤ H, or y = ±H and -H ≤ x ≤ H.

No implementation may treat an exact boundary Point as outside solely because it lies on the Domain edge.

02.5 · Outside-Boundary Vectors

Any Coordinate Beyond H Must Fail

A Point is outside the Survey Domain if x < -H, x > H, y < -H, or y > H.

Such a Point shall fail as a valid canonical Survey reference. It shall not be clipped, snapped, wrapped, translated, rounded, or otherwise repaired into the Domain.

02.6 · Corner Vectors

Simultaneous Boundary Conditions Produce One Result

The four corners are:

(-H,-H), (-H,+H), (+H,-H), and (+H,+H).

Each corner lies on two included boundaries simultaneously and is therefore a valid Survey Point under the current closed-Domain model.

02.7 · ECEM Boundary Stability Vectors

Greater Representational Capacity Must Not Move the Boundary

Under ECEM, expansion of exact coordinate representability does not expand, contract, or reinterpret the Survey Domain.

A future compatible version may represent additional exact Points near the boundary, but the governing inequalities remain the same and previously valid or invalid Points retain their meaning.

02.8 · Domain Validity Vectors

Valid Survey Reference and Invalid Survey Reference Must Be Distinguishable

Vectors shall prove the exact three-way geometric distinction among:

INTERIOR, BOUNDARY, and OUTSIDE.

For Survey validity, INTERIOR and BOUNDARY are valid; OUTSIDE is invalid. These labels describe Survey Domain relation only and do not by themselves determine World membership.

02.9 · World-Space Separation Vectors

Survey Validity Is Not World Membership

Reference Vectors shall preserve the architectural distinction between the FSF Survey Domain and Spatial Ground membership.

A Point may be a valid Survey reference inside DH while still being classified by Spatial Ground as NON_WORLD. FSF proves reference capacity; Spatial Ground determines canonical participation in The World.

02.10 · Cross-Implementation Boundary Agreement

Every Implementation Must Classify the Same Point the Same Way

Independent conforming implementations shall classify the same exact Point identically as interior, boundary, or outside under the same governing H and Specification profile.

A disagreement indicates implementation defect, Specification ambiguity, version incompatibility, or fixture defect requiring resolution.

02.11 · Numerical H Dependency

Concrete Edge Fixtures Must Wait for Capacity Selection

The earlier ±1,000,000 Pang half-span is a superseded working candidate and shall not be used as a canonical fixture value.

The unresolved value of H prevents final publication of concrete numerical vectors such as “the eastern boundary is x = N Pang” or “this specific coordinate is one exact unit outside the Domain.”

Such examples become canonical only after FMD-04B resolves the numerical Domain capacity through a justified mathematical decision and that result is incorporated into the governing Specification. Until then, symbolic and parameterized fixtures are the correct Reference Vector form.

02.12 · No Inference Beyond the Domain

Outside the Survey Domain Means No Valid FSF Reference

A failed Survey reference outside DH does not imply an exterior realm, alternate geography, World edge, Verge, ownership boundary, or any other higher-layer ontology.

The Reference Vector proves only that the input is outside the canonical FSF reference domain.

Executable Now

Canonical origin; parameterized Domain geometry; closed-boundary semantics; symbolic interior/on-boundary/outside classification; corner inclusion; ECEM boundary stability; Survey-validity versus World-membership separation; cross-implementation classification agreement.

Still Open

Numerical value of H; final numerical edge coordinates; concrete just-inside / just-outside canonical fixtures tied to one selected H; final vector identifiers, corpus packaging, and canonical publication/adoption.

Reference Vector Integration Standing

Boundary Semantics Are Testable Before Numerical Capacity Is Final

The original page correctly deferred Survey Domain vectors while both geometry and boundary conventions were unresolved. That is no longer the current standing.

The candidate Specification now defines the Domain as a closed axis-aligned square centered at the origin, so exact symbolic and parameterized boundary behavior can be tested. What remains open in this section's Domain mathematics is the numerical capacity parameter H under FMD-04B. Reference Vectors therefore may prove the rules now without inventing the missing number.
