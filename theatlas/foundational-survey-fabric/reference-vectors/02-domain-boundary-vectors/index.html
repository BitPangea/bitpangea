Reference Vector Section 02 · Survey Domain Limits

The canonical test set for proving exact interpretation of the Survey origin, finite Domain limits, and validity at and around the boundary of canonical reference.

Domain-Boundary Vectors define the reference cases through which implementations will prove exact agreement on the canonical origin, the exact limits of the finite Survey Domain, references immediately inside or on those limits, and references that must fail because they lie outside the Domain.

The final vector corpus does not yet exist because the formal Survey Domain geometry and exact boundary conventions remain unresolved within the Specification. This page therefore establishes the required vector categories and proof obligations without inventing boundary coordinates prematurely.

Reference Vector Question Given this exact input, what exact answer must every conforming implementation produce?
02.1 · Canonical Origin Vectors

The Zero-Reference Must Resolve Identically

The vector set shall include the permanent canonical origin and sufficient neighboring cases to prove that all conforming implementations interpret the zero-reference identically.

The origin shall be tested as a mathematical reference point only and shall not imply Parcel, civic, cultural, or geographic importance.

02.2 · Exact Domain Limit Vectors

The Finite Survey Domain Must Have One Exact Extent

Reference vectors shall identify the exact mathematical limits of the canonical Survey Domain once its formal geometry is selected.

Independent implementations shall derive the same valid reference extent and the same boundary conditions from the governing Specification.

02.3 · Interior-Near-Boundary Vectors

Just-Inside References Must Remain Valid

The vector corpus shall include canonical references positioned immediately inside the Survey Domain limits at the finest testable precision selected for the relevant test suite.

These cases shall prove that valid near-boundary positions are not rejected merely because they lie close to the Domain limit.

02.4 · Exact-Boundary Vectors

Boundary Inclusion Must Be Deterministic

The vector set shall include references that lie exactly on canonical Survey Domain boundaries wherever the formal Domain model permits such references.

Expected results shall prove the precise inclusion, exclusion, endpoint, and edge semantics established by the Specification.

02.5 · Outside-Boundary Vectors

Just-Outside References Must Fail

The corpus shall include references immediately beyond each applicable Survey Domain limit to prove deterministic rejection of out-of-domain spatial claims.

Such references shall not be silently clipped, snapped, wrapped, translated, or reinterpreted as valid Survey positions.

02.6 · Corner and Intersection Vectors

Multiple Boundary Conditions Must Agree

Where the selected Survey Domain geometry contains corners, vertices, joined limits, or equivalent compound boundary conditions, the vector set shall include exact cases at those intersections.

These vectors shall prove that multiple simultaneous boundary rules produce one deterministic result.

02.7 · Boundary Refinement Vectors

Greater Precision Must Not Change the Domain

The corpus shall test references near Domain limits at multiple supported refinement depths to prove that increasing precision does not expand, contract, or reinterpret the finite Survey Domain.

Refinement may increase positional precision. It shall not migrate the foundational reference extent.

02.8 · Domain Validity Vectors

Referenceable and Nonreferenceable Space Must Be Distinguishable

Vectors shall demonstrate the exact transition between valid Survey reference and the absence of valid Survey reference beyond the canonical Domain.

A location outside the Survey Domain shall simply fail as a canonical BitPangea Survey reference; the failure shall not assign cartographic or territorial meaning to that space.

02.9 · World-Space Separation Vectors

Survey Validity Is Not World Membership

Where appropriate, vectors shall demonstrate that a location may be validly referenceable within the Survey Domain without thereby proving that the location constitutes BitPangea World-space.

These cases shall preserve the architectural distinction between canonical reference capacity and higher-layer World membership.

02.10 · Cross-Implementation Boundary Agreement

Every Implementation Must Draw the Same Mathematical Limit

Independent conforming implementations shall classify every boundary vector identically under the same governing Specification.

A disagreement about whether a reference is inside, on, or outside the Survey Domain indicates an implementation defect, specification ambiguity, or version incompatibility requiring resolution.

Established by Requirements and Specification Scope

The Survey Domain must be finite, connected, exact in extent, globally canonical, independent of World membership, and governed by deterministic endpoint and boundary semantics. The permanent canonical origin must serve as one exact zero-reference without acquiring higher-layer importance.

Canonical Vector Corpus Not Yet Possessed

Actual origin coordinates, Domain-limit values, exact boundary expressions, inclusion conventions, near-boundary refinement cases, and authoritative machine-readable expected results cannot be created until the formal Survey Domain geometry, canonical frame, addressing model, and serialization are selected.
