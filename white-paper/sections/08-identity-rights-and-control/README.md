# White Paper — Section VIII

## Identity, Rights, and Control

**BitPangea — A World Before a Civilization**  
**White Paper · Version 0.1 · September 2026**

This directory contains the web presentation of **Section VIII — Identity, Rights, and Control** from **BitPangea — A World Before a Civilization, White Paper Version 0.1**.

The section preserves the distinctions among actor identity, governed relationships, exercisable authority, ownership, authorization, and legitimate change.

Its governing distinctions are:

> **Identity → Who or what is this?**

> **Rights → What recognized relationship exists?**

> **Control / Authorization → What may be done?**

The section’s central principle is:

> **Identity, ownership, rights, control, permission, and authority are not synonyms.**

## Section Role

Section VIII explains why legitimate interaction requires more than recognizing an actor.

BitPangea must distinguish among:

- who or what is acting;
- what governed relationship exists;
- what authority may actually be exercised;
- whether a requested operation is permitted;
- whether Runtime may execute it.

These responsibilities are related.

They are not interchangeable.

---

## Identity

Identity answers:

> **Who or what is this?**

An identity may represent:

- a person;
- an institution;
- a Builder;
- a service;
- an automated system;
- an authorized agent;
- another recognized actor.

Identity allows BitPangea to determine that an action originates from a particular recognized entity.

Identity alone does not determine what that entity may do.

---

## Authentication Is Not Authority

The section preserves an important distinction:

> **Authentication proves access to an identity mechanism. Identity establishes the actor. Neither, by itself, establishes authority.**

Control of a credential does not automatically establish:

- ownership;
- development rights;
- administrative authority;
- access rights;
- governance rights;
- permission to modify World State.

BitPangea should therefore avoid systems where possession of a credential silently becomes equivalent to every form of authority.

---

## Rights

Rights answer:

> **What governed relationship exists between an actor and something within BitPangea?**

A future rights Architecture may recognize relationships such as:

- ownership;
- authorized use;
- access;
- development authority;
- delegation;
- tenancy;
- stewardship;
- operational responsibility;
- shared control;
- other governed relationships.

The complete rights model remains intentionally unresolved in Version 0.1.

The section preserves the distinction between the existence of a governed relationship and whatever economic or legal model may later express it.

---

## Ownership Is One Possible Right

Ownership is one possible governed relationship.

It is not the definition of rights itself.

This distinction is particularly important for Parcels.

The Parcel Cadastre establishes canonical Parcel identity and territory.

Identity / Rights / Control may later record who possesses a governed relationship to that Parcel.

It must not redefine:

- which Parcel exists;
- where that Parcel is located;
- what territory canonically belongs to it.

The section preserves:

> **The Parcel Cadastre owns Parcel truth. Identity / Rights / Control may reference Parcel truth without redefining it.**

And more generally:

> **Reference does not transfer authority.**

---

## Control and Authorization

Control concerns exercisable authority.

Authorization asks whether a particular action is permitted under:

- the actor’s identity;
- recognized rights;
- applicable rules;
- current World condition;
- any additional required approvals.

The governing question is:

> **What may this actor do here, now, and under what authority?**

Possible future authorized operations may include:

- entering a restricted environment;
- modifying a structure;
- publishing content;
- operating a service;
- delegating authority;
- changing an access rule;
- initiating a transaction;
- requesting a change to canonical World State.

---

## Identity, Rights, and Control Must Remain Distinct

The section summarizes the responsibility separation as:

> **Identity establishes the actor.**

> **Rights establish the relationship.**

> **Control establishes exercisable authority.**

Authorization should not be inferred merely because:

- the actor is known;
- the actor possesses a right;
- a technical operation is possible.

Rights may be scoped.

Control may be delegated.

Permission may be conditional.

Authority may expire.

Some operations may require multiple approvals.

---

## Ownership Is Not the Architecture

The finite Parcel supply naturally raises ownership questions.

But Section VIII explicitly resists allowing ownership to dominate Architecture before the World itself is fully defined.

The section preserves that:

- a Parcel can exist without being owned;
- a Parcel can remain the same while ownership changes;
- a Parcel can be referenced without being transferable;
- a participant may use a place without owning it;
- an institution may hold responsibility without economic ownership;
- a Builder may modify something within a Parcel without obtaining permanent control over the Parcel itself.

BitPangea should therefore support multiple governed relationships rather than reduce every legitimate interaction to ownership.

---

## No Predetermined Token Model

Section VIII explicitly states that the current Architecture does not require:

- NFTs;
- Parcel tokens;
- a particular blockchain;
- transferable cryptographic ownership assets;
- public transferability of every right;
- a predetermined ownership model.

These technologies may later be considered if they solve real architectural problems.

They are not foundational assumptions.

The governing order is:

> **The Parcel exists before any instrument that may later represent a relationship to the Parcel.**

The section further preserves:

- a representation of ownership is not the Parcel;
- a token is not the territory;
- a wallet address is not the actor;
- a credential is not the right;
- a technically valid transaction is not automatically legitimate.

---

## Place Before Instrument

The deeper architectural order is:

```text
canonical place
↓
governed relationship
↓
possible instrument representing that relationship
```

This protects Parcel identity from being collapsed into whatever ownership or transfer mechanism may later be adopted.

---

## Bounded Authority

The section preserves the principle:

> **Authority should not exceed the responsibility that justifies it.**

Examples include:

- an identity system should not redefine Parcel territory;
- a rights registry should not become canonical spatial authority;
- Runtime should not create authority merely because it can execute an operation;
- an interface should not grant permission merely because it exposes a control.

Each Architecture domain should remain within its legitimate responsibility boundary.

---

## From Authority to Action

The conceptual responsibility flow is:

```text
Actor Identity
↓
Rights Relationship
↓
Control / Authorization
↓
Permitted Operation
↓
World Runtime
↓
Resulting World State
```

The section explicitly clarifies that this is:

> **a conceptual responsibility flow, not a claim that every implementation must use one identical technical pipeline.**

The purpose is responsibility separation.

Identity identifies the actor.

Rights identify the governed relationship.

Authorization determines whether an operation is permitted.

World Runtime executes legitimate change.

Persistence / Provenance preserves resulting continuity and evidence.

---

## Legitimate Change

The purpose of Identity / Rights / Control is not simply to restrict activity.

Its deeper purpose is:

> **to make change legitimate**

The section preserves the need to distinguish:

- observation from modification;
- presence from authority;
- ownership from use;
- rights from permissions;
- permission from execution;
- execution from canonical truth.

Without these distinctions, BitPangea might record that change occurred while being unable to explain why the change was legitimately allowed.

---

## Relationship to World Runtime

Identity / Rights / Control establishes legitimacy before execution.

World Runtime executes permitted operations.

The responsibility boundary is therefore:

```text
Identity / Rights / Control
→ determines legitimate authority

World Runtime
→ executes permitted change
```

Runtime may consume authorization.

It does not thereby become the source of rights.

---

## Relationship to Parcel Cadastre

Parcel Cadastre owns Parcel-specific canonical truth.

Identity / Rights / Control may refer to that truth when expressing:

- ownership;
- use;
- stewardship;
- tenancy;
- development authority;
- delegation;
- control.

But reference does not transfer Parcel authority.

The Parcel remains the same canonical place regardless of who holds a governed relationship to it.

---

## Relationship to Persistence / Provenance

Persistence / Provenance preserves evidence and continuity surrounding consequential changes.

Section VIII therefore implies a responsibility relationship where:

```text
Identity / Rights / Control
→ explains who and under what authority

World Runtime
→ executes change

Persistence / Provenance
→ preserves resulting lineage and evidence
```

These responsibilities should remain distinct.
