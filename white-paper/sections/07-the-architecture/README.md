## Section Role

Section VII explains how BitPangea moves from spatial truth toward a living, operational, interoperable, and experienceable World without allowing higher systems to silently redefine the deeper World beneath them.

It presents The Architecture as the enduring design record for responsibilities including:

- spatial truth;
- identity;
- authority;
- change;
- persistence;
- interoperability;
- runtime;
- experience.

The section emphasizes responsibility boundaries rather than implementation uniformity.

---

## The Architecture Under The Atlas

The Architecture is preserved under **The Atlas**.

Its purpose is to record enduring responsibilities required to make BitPangea viable while keeping those responsibilities distinct from constitutional truth and implementation detail.

The section preserves the progression:

> **Foundation constrains.**

> **Codex defines.**

> **Atlas designs.**

> **Implementation realizes.**

The Architecture belongs to the design layer of that progression.

---

## Architecture Is Not Constitution

Section VII explicitly states:

> **An Architecture domain is not automatically a constitutional Domain.**

The Codex defines constitutional truth and recognized properties of the World.

The Architecture defines enduring responsibility required to make that World viable.

This distinction protects BitPangea from turning every important technical or operational concern into permanent constitutional truth.

---

## The Nine Recognized Architecture Domains

Section VII recognizes nine Architecture domains:

1. Foundational Survey Fabric
2. Spatial Ground
3. General Spatial Interpretation
4. Parcel Cadastre
5. Identity / Rights / Control
6. Persistence / Provenance
7. Interoperability
8. World Runtime
9. Experience Architecture

Recognition means a domain has an enduring architectural responsibility.

Recognition does **not** mean:

- equal maturity;
- implementation readiness;
- finalized internal design;
- strict dependency on all domains listed before it.

---

## Foundational Survey Fabric

**Foundational Survey Fabric** preserves:

- canonical spatial reference;
- exact measurement;
- orientation;
- refinement;
- Survey conformance.

It is the deepest recognized spatial Architecture domain in the White Paper’s demonstrated chain.

---

## Spatial Ground

**Spatial Ground** mediates between canonical Survey reference and usable World-space ground.

Its detailed internal Architecture remains under development in Version 0.1.

---

## General Spatial Interpretation

**General Spatial Interpretation** derives higher spatial meaning without redefining the canonical spatial truth beneath it.

It exists above Spatial Ground and below Parcel Cadastre in the demonstrated spatial dependency.

---

## Parcel Cadastre

**Parcel Cadastre** preserves canonical Parcel truth.

It is responsible for the fixed identity, position, and territory of exactly **21,000,000 canonical Parcels**.

---

## Identity / Rights / Control

**Identity / Rights / Control** establishes:

- who or what is acting;
- what recognized relationships exist;
- what authority may legitimately be exercised.

It must remain distinct from Parcel identity and canonical place.

---

## Persistence / Provenance

**Persistence / Provenance** preserves:

- continuity;
- lineage;
- evidence;
- the ability to understand how present World State came to exist.

It supports durable meaning across change.

---

## Interoperability

**Interoperability** ensures that multiple conforming implementations can exchange authoritative meaning without producing competing versions of canonical truth.

Its central objective is:

> **One meaning. Many durable expressions.**

---

## World Runtime

**World Runtime** evaluates legitimate change, executes permitted operations, preserves coherent World State, and coordinates operational behavior through time.

Runtime performs change.

It should not become the source of the authority that permits that change.

---

## Experience Architecture

**Experience Architecture** makes the World perceptible and usable to participants.

It should not become the source of the truths it presents.

Experience may display canonical state.

Display does not transfer authority.

---

## Membership Is Not Dependency

The presence of nine Architecture domains does not imply a strict nine-step stack.

The strongest demonstrated dependency currently preserved by the White Paper is:

```text
Foundational Survey Fabric
↓
Spatial Ground
↓
General Spatial Interpretation
↓
Parcel Cadastre
```

That dependency is demonstrated by the nature of spatial truth.

Beyond it, Architecture becomes more networked.

The section gives examples:

- Identity / Rights / Control may interact with Runtime;
- Runtime may rely upon Persistence / Provenance;
- Interoperability may span several domains;
- Experience Architecture may consume outputs from nearly all domains.

These relationships should be demonstrated rather than assumed.

The governing rule is:

> **Only claim strict ordering where dependency has actually been demonstrated.**

---

## Protect the Boundary

Section VII preserves the Architecture Boundary Rule:

> **A lower Architecture domain may establish the constraints that higher domains must not violate, but it should not prescribe the internal responsibilities, organization, implementation, or lifecycle of a higher domain unless that dependency is irreducibly necessary to the lower domain itself.**

Its shorter form is:

> **Protect the boundary. Do not design the layer above from the layer below.**

The section illustrates that rule through concrete examples:

- Foundational Survey Fabric should not define Parcel ownership;
- Parcel Cadastre should not become the source of actor identity;
- Identity / Rights / Control should not redefine the Parcel;
- World Runtime should not become the source of authorization;
- Experience Architecture should not become canonical merely because something is displayed.

---

## Authority Should Follow Responsibility

The section preserves a bounded-authority model.

Each domain should possess only the authority necessary to perform its legitimate responsibility.

The key principle is:

> **Reference does not transfer authority.**

One domain may reference information owned by another without acquiring the right to redefine it.

Examples preserved by the section include:

```text
Identity / Rights / Control
→ may reference Parcel identity
→ does not become Parcel Cadastre
```

```text
Runtime
→ may reference authorization
→ does not become the source of rights
```

```text
Experience Architecture
→ may display World State
→ does not become authoritative World State
```

---

## Durable Meaning, Replaceable Implementation

The Architecture is intended to preserve meaning longer than any particular implementation.

BitPangea should be able to replace:

- databases;
- protocols;
- rendering engines;
- authentication systems;
- network Architecture;
- implementation stacks

without replacing the World.

The objective is:

> **continuity of meaning**

not technological permanence.

---

## Permanence Gradient

The section preserves the permanence gradient:

> **The deeper the layer, the more permanent it should be.**

> **The closer the layer is to the user, the more freedom it should have to evolve.**

This does not establish one universal dependency chain.

It expresses how permanence should generally increase toward deeper World truth while flexibility increases toward lived experience.

---

## One World, Many Conforming Implementations

BitPangea should not require one eternal software stack.

Different conforming implementations may:

- render differently;
- store internal data differently;
- use different programming languages;
- expose different user experiences;
- optimize operations differently.

Those differences are acceptable so long as they preserve the same canonical meaning where conformance is required.

The interoperability objective is:

> **One meaning. Many durable expressions.**

And the section explicitly preserves:

> **Implementation diversity should not become semantic diversity.**

---

## Architecture Enables; Civilization Emerges

Section VII preserves the distinction:

> **Builders are not an Architecture domain.**

and:

> **civilization is not an Architecture domain.**

The Architecture establishes capabilities and constraints.

Builders use those capabilities to create.

Communities, institutions, economies, culture, and history may emerge from continued participation.

The governing principle is:

> **Architecture enables. Civilization emerges.**

---

## The Architecture Is Not One Diagram

The section explicitly rejects reducing BitPangea’s Architecture to a single layered diagram.

The better model is:

> **a governed collection of enduring responsibilities with demonstrated dependencies where those dependencies are real and bounded interaction where they are not**

The spatial dependency remains the clearest strict sequence:

```text
Foundational Survey Fabric
↓
Spatial Ground
↓
General Spatial Interpretation
↓
Parcel Cadastre
```

Other domains surround and interact with that foundation through bounded authority and demonstrated relationships.

---

## Architectural Direction

The section closes with the following architectural direction:

> **Design the ground.**

> **Govern change without surrendering continuity.**

> **Enable participation.**

> **Leave civilization free to emerge.**

The goal is not to make every layer permanent.

It is:

> **to make the right things permanent.**
