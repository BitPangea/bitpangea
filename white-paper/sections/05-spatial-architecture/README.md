## Section Role

Section V explains why persistent digital place requires Architecture deeper than coordinates, maps, interfaces, renderers, or implementation-specific identifiers.

A digital system can display a location without preserving it permanently.

BitPangea requires more.

If a Parcel is to remain the same place through changing:

- software;
- representation;
- ownership;
- development;
- history;

then its spatial identity cannot depend upon one implementation.

The section therefore separates the major spatial responsibilities that support permanent place.

---

## Persistent Place Requires More Than Coordinates

Coordinates alone are insufficient to establish permanent place.

They can be:

- generated;
- reassigned;
- reformatted;
- reprojected;
- rendered differently;
- stored differently.

The White Paper therefore distinguishes durable spatial meaning from temporary technical representation.

The section preserves the idea that:

> **Persistent place requires a durable spatial architecture beneath experience.**

---

The clearest demonstrated spatial dependency is:

```text
Foundational Survey Fabric
↓
Spatial Ground
↓
General Spatial Interpretation
↓

```

This dependency separates four distinct architectural responsibilities:

### Foundational Survey Fabric

Preserves canonical spatial reference.

### Spatial Ground

Carries canonical Survey reference into enduring World-space.

### General Spatial Interpretation

Provides higher spatial meaning without redefining the canonical reference beneath it.

### Parcel Cadastre

Establishes authoritative Parcel identity and territory.

The broader governing principle is:

> **Survey first. Cadastre later. Experience above both.**

## The Extent — Complete Spatial Scope

Before reference can be meaningful, BitPangea must possess a World to which that reference applies.

That constitutional responsibility belongs to:

> **The Extent**

The Extent expresses BitPangea’s complete spatial scope.

It establishes that the World is:

- finite;
- complete;
- non-expanding.

The Extent is not:

- a coordinate system;
- a Parcel grid;
- the Survey Domain;
- a visible border;
- a wall;
- a coastline;
- a perimeter;
- an Exterior.

The governing constitutional condition is:

> **BitPangea possesses one complete, non-expanding spatial scope.**

The Extent constrains later Architecture without itself prescribing the mathematics of spatial representation.

---

## Foundational Survey Fabric — Canonical Spatial Reference

The deepest current spatial Architecture domain is the **Foundational Survey Fabric**.

Its purpose is not to create Parcels.

Its purpose is to preserve canonical spatial reference.

The White Paper describes it as:

> **minimal, exact, finite, planar, and semantically neutral**

Its primitive spatial question is:

> **Where?**

It supports durable reference for:

- location;
- measurement;
- orientation;
- refinement;
- deterministic geometric relationships.

---

## Survey Domain ≠ World

The section preserves a critical Architecture boundary:

> **The Survey Domain is not BitPangea territory.**

The Survey Domain is a finite mathematical reference domain.

It provides the capacity to reference space.

That does not mean every valid Survey reference belongs to BitPangea World-space.

The distinction is:

```text
Survey reference capacity
≠
World membership
```

Higher Architecture determines which referenced space becomes enduring BitPangea World-space.

---

## Survey Units ≠ Parcels

The section also preserves:

> **Survey units are not Parcels.**

A mathematical unit in the Survey system does not become a canonical Parcel merely because it can be referenced.

The Survey Fabric establishes where.

The Parcel Cadastre eventually establishes which Parcel occupies which authoritative territory.

---

## Reference Capacity ≠ Territory

The section states:

> **Reference capacity is not territory.**

The ability to mathematically identify a location does not establish:

- World membership;
- Parcel identity;
- Parcel territory.

This distinction protects the boundary between foundational reference and higher spatial meaning.

---

## Representation Must Not Redefine Place

BitPangea cannot allow any one:

- display format;
- database key;
- map projection;
- serialization method;
- rendering engine;
- application-specific coordinate label

to become the permanent definition of place merely because it was implemented first.

The governing principle is:

> **Representation may change. Place must not.**

Different conforming implementations may represent the same canonical location differently.

They remain valid if they resolve to the same canonical meaning.

---

## Durable Meaning, Replaceable Implementation

The section emphasizes that BitPangea’s deepest spatial Architecture should preserve meaning rather than one technical expression.

The desired relationship is:

```text
durable canonical spatial meaning
+
replaceable implementation
```

The Foundational Survey Fabric should therefore survive changes in:

- encoding;
- display;
- storage;
- rendering;
- interface technology.

---

## Spatial Ground

Above the Foundational Survey Fabric sits **Spatial Ground**.

The section preserves Spatial Ground as a recognized Architecture domain whose detailed internal design remains under development.

Its necessity arises because:

```text
canonical Survey reference
≠
World-space ground
```

The Survey Fabric can establish exact reference while remaining semantically neutral about what those references mean within BitPangea.

Spatial Ground therefore mediates between:

```text
pure canonical reference
↓
enduring World-space
```

The demonstrated dependency is:

```text
Foundational Survey Fabric
→ Spatial Ground
```

Spatial Ground must respect the canonical reference beneath it.

It must not redefine the Survey Fabric.

---

## Architecture Boundary Rule

The section preserves the broader rule:

> **A lower Architecture domain may constrain what higher domains must not violate without prescribing how those higher domains must internally work.**

Working form:

> **Protect the boundary. Do not design the layer above from the layer below.**

This principle is central to maintaining clear ownership of architectural responsibility.

---

## General Spatial Interpretation

The next demonstrated dependency is **General Spatial Interpretation**.

The domain exists because a persistent World requires more than:

- raw reference;
- foundational ground.

Higher systems need spatial meaning capable of supporting:

- territory;
- geography;
- Parcel placement;
- regional organization;
- non-Parcel portions;
- infrastructure;
- later experience.

General Spatial Interpretation exists to provide higher spatial meaning without rewriting the canonical spatial truth beneath it.

Its exact internal Architecture remains under development in Version 0.1.

---

## Spatial Responsibility Separation

The section summarizes the four spatial roles as:

```text
Foundational Survey Fabric
→ preserves canonical reference

Spatial Ground
→ establishes the next World-space layer

General Spatial Interpretation
→ derives higher spatial meaning

Parcel Cadastre
→ establishes canonical Parcel identity and territory
```

The separation matters because each domain answers a different question.

---

## Parcel Cadastre — Which Parcel Is This?

The Parcel Cadastre owns canonical Parcel truth.

The Survey asks:

> **Where is this?**

The Cadastre asks:

> **Which Parcel is this, where is it fixed, and what territory belongs to it?**

The section summarizes the distinction as:

> **Survey establishes where. Cadastre establishes which Parcel.**

The Parcel Cadastre must ultimately preserve exactly **21,000,000 Parcels** with:

- permanent identities;
- fixed positions;
- authoritative territorial definitions;
- deterministic Parcel relationships.

---

## Parcel Identity vs Representation

A Parcel may change in:

- appearance;
- ownership;
- use;
- structures;
- surrounding development;
- regional context.

The canonical Parcel should remain the same Parcel.

This distinction is what makes cadastral truth different from visual representation.

---

## Deterministic Spatial Relationships

Persistent place requires deterministic canonical relationships.

Two conforming implementations should not disagree about fundamental Parcel facts merely because they use different software or internal data structures.

The section specifically states:

> **Parcel Adjacency is established through shared Boundary and must resolve consistently across conforming implementations.**

Canonical spatial meaning should therefore remain stable across implementation variation.

The interoperability principle is:

> **One meaning. Many durable expressions.**

---

## Parcel Geometry

The Parcel Cadastre ultimately requires exact Parcel geometry.

Exactly **21,000,000 Parcels** must fit within one finite World while preserving:

- unique Parcel identity;
- fixed position;
- indivisibility;
- non-overlap;
- deterministic boundaries;
- Parcel Adjacency;
- World-scale Parcel Contiguity;
- compatibility with non-Parcel spatial conditions;
- compatibility with World Form.

The section presents equal fundamental Parcel area as a Creator design direction.

It also presents a dominant rhombic morphology derived from a three-part hexagonal construction as the leading visible Parcel grammar.

That direction remains provisional in Version 0.1.

---

## Rhombille Direction

The section clarifies that the individual rhombic unit, not the enclosing hexagon, is the prospective Parcel form.

Local orientation may vary while preserving one dominant morphology.

But the visible Parcel grammar is not the Foundational Survey Fabric.

The standing distinction is:

> **Substrate ≠ Parcel experience.**

This protects the deeper Survey layer from being reverse-engineered around a preferred visual tiling.

---

## Geometry Must Fit the Architecture

The section preserves:

> **The geometry must fit the architecture.**

And rejects the reverse:

> the Architecture should not be redesigned merely to preserve an attractive tiling.

The visible Parcel system must conform to deeper spatial truth.

---

## Spatial Truth Before Experience

BitPangea may eventually be experienced through:

- a globe;
- flat lived World;
- geography;
- Regions;
- infrastructure;
- individual Parcels;
- intuitive interfaces.

But the World should not become spatially true only when it is rendered.

Canonical place must already exist beneath experience.

The section summarizes the architecture as:

> **The Extent constrains the World’s complete spatial scope.**

> **The Foundational Survey Fabric preserves canonical spatial reference.**

> **Spatial Ground builds the next World-space layer upon that reference.**

> **General Spatial Interpretation derives higher spatial meaning.**

> **The Parcel Cadastre establishes exactly which persistent Parcel occupies which place.**

Only afterward should Experience Architecture determine how that place is shown.
